\begin{abstract}
We establish a universal finite-dimensional quantum resource theory (QRT) for the boundary-sensitive non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE). The central object is a boundary-conditioned quantum statistical atlas: a finite family of right/left density operators attached to a common cell register and to a finite family of physical boundary conditions. The object class is closed under arbitrary CPTP post-processing and does not require translation invariance, a Bloch Hamiltonian, a generalized Brillouin zone, a point gap, one spatial dimension, diagonalizability, or a microscopic Hamiltonian ansatz. The physical generator sector is embedded through normalized generalized spectral subspaces, with optional vector-resolved refinements at degeneracies and exceptional points.

The universal quantum layer is generated by the fidelity divergence $d_F=-\log F$. Its full statistical monotone is $\Phi_Q$, and its cell-measurement shadow is $\Phi_X$. The NHSE layer is a boundary-response resource with two complementary monotone families: boundary-tail concentration and boundary-condition susceptibility. For a boundary collar $A$, the amplitude leakage exponent is $s(\mu;A)=-(1/2)\log\mu(A^c)$. Taking the supremum over all admissible boundary collars and over right, left, or paired right/left sectors produces $\Gamma_{\rm skin}$. The calibrated resource is the positive boundary excess relative to a reference calibration family.

Free morphisms are common boundary-blind CPTP maps that are position-covariant, cannot transport probability from outside an input boundary collar into the corresponding output collar, and admit target-relative calibration recoveries satisfying the same boundary non-creation rule. This makes the free maps an identity-containing compositional monoidal category. Exact convertibility is characterized by a finite collection of convex Choi semidefinite/linear feasibility problems, one for each finite mode and boundary correspondence branch. Non-convertibility therefore admits branchwise dual witnesses.

The main faithfulness theorem is non-circular. We define physical boundary-sensitive NHSE by a positive-density family of spectral states with boundary leakage decaying on a chosen asymptotic scale relative to a boundary-neutral calibration family. We then prove an exact equivalence between this physical localization criterion and positive calibrated QRT skin resource on the same scale. Hence, for extensive exponential skin localization, $r_{\rm skin}>0$ if and only if a positive density of spectral states is exponentially localized at a boundary relative to the reference experiment. The theorem also applies to arbitrary scale functions and therefore captures stretched-exponential and critical logarithmic regimes without changing the QRT.

The formalism covers ordinary and multiband lattice NHSE, higher-dimensional and corner/higher-order skin effects, quasiperiodic and disordered systems without Bloch theory, many-body/Fock-space realizations after choosing a finite configuration-space cell metric, and Floquet/harmonic realizations after the standard finite Sambe enlargement. Boundary-insensitive non-Hermitian localization is explicitly separated from NHSE: it has zero calibrated skin resource when the same boundary concentration appears in the reference experiment. The Hatano--Nelson chain is an exact calibration battery with $\rskin=|g|$ for the fidelity resource and with the new boundary-tail profile having exact inverse-localization-length coefficient $|g|$.

The resulting theory is universal at the operational QRT level: it is a closed object theory with compositional free operations, exact conversion, complete families of separating witnesses, and a non-circular physical faithfulness theorem for boundary-sensitive NHSE. The construction deliberately does not identify unrelated non-Hermitian localization phenomena with NHSE merely because they are localized.
\end{abstract}

\section{Universality target}

The non-Hermitian skin effect is fundamentally a boundary-sensitive localization phenomenon in which an extensive set of spectral states accumulates anomalously near system boundaries. Current research has demonstrated skin effects in higher dimensions, many-body critical systems, harmonic/Floquet settings, quasiperiodic systems without point-gap topology, and settings in which conventional localization mechanisms must be separated from boundary-sensitive skin accumulation. \cite{Universal2D,ManyBodyCritical,HarmonicNHSE,QuasicrystalNHSE,UnifiedLocalization} A universal QRT should therefore be formulated in terms of boundary experiments and local probability statistics rather than in terms of any single Bloch or topological representation.

\begin{definition}[Universal NHSE quantum resource theory]
The universal NHSE-QRT is the monoidal resource theory
\[
\mathbf Q_{\rm NHSE}
=
(\Obj,\Free,\otimes,\mathcal R_Q,\mathcal R_{\partial}),
\]
where $\Obj$ is the class of finite boundary-conditioned quantum statistical atlases, $\Free$ is the compositional category of calibrated boundary-compatible CPTP morphisms defined below, $\mathcal R_Q$ is the family of quantum statistical monotones generated by $d_F$, and $\mathcal R_{\partial}$ is the family of boundary-skin concentration monotones over all admissible scale functions and right/left test patterns.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universal existence and faithfulness]
For every finite-dimensional boundary-conditioned atlas, the above construction satisfies the structural requirements of a quantum resource theory: the object class is closed under CPTP post-processing; free morphisms contain identities and are closed under composition and tensor product; exact conversion is a finite union of convex Choi-feasibility branches; $\Phi_Q$ and its position shadows satisfy data processing; the complete boundary-skin family is monotone under free morphisms; and, for the physical boundary-sensitive NHSE class defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}, positive resource on any asymptotic tail scale is equivalent to positive-density boundary localization on that scale.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Object closure and quantum data processing are immediate from CPTP closure and fidelity monotonicity. Identity, composition, and tensor closure are proved in Sec.~\ref{sec:free}. Choi exactness is proved in Sec.~\ref{sec:choi}. Boundary monotonicity is established in Sec.~\ref{sec:boundary}. Physical faithfulness follows from the equivalence between leakage exponents and boundary concentration together with subleading calibration in Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}.
\end{proof}

\section{Universal boundary-conditioned quantum statistical atlases}

Let $X_L$ be a finite cell set with a metric $d_L$ and a macroscopic scale $\ell_L\to\infty$. Let
\[
\HH_L=\bigoplus_{x\in X_L}\HH_x,
\qquad
\Pi_x\Pi_y=\delta_{xy}\Pi_x,
\qquad
\sum_x\Pi_x=\id.
\]
The cell metric may be the Euclidean graph metric of a lattice, a boundary-adapted metric of a finite graph, or a configuration-space graph metric for many-body/Fock-space systems.

Let $\calB_L$ be a finite boundary-condition family and $\calC_L\subseteq\calB_L$ a nonempty calibration family.

\begin{definition}[Universal atlas]
A finite boundary-conditioned quantum statistical atlas is
\[
\mathbb X_L=
(\HH_L,X_L,\calB_L,\calC_L,
\{\rho_{m,B}^{h}\}_{m,B,h}),
\qquad h\in\{R,L\},
\]
where every $\rho_{m,B}^h$ is a density operator. Exact conversions preserve the finite mode label up to a bijection.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cell-measurement channel]
The geometric shadow is
\[
\Delta_{X_L}(\rho)
=\sum_{x\in X_L}\Tr(\Pi_x\rho)|x\rangle\langle x|.
\]
We write
\[
p_{m,B}=\Delta_{X_L}(\rho_{m,B}^R),
\qquad
q_{m,B}=\Delta_{X_L}(\rho_{m,B}^L).
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Generator embedding}

\begin{definition}[Generalized spectral atlas]
For a finite non-Hermitian generator $H_B$ and an eigenvalue label $m$, let
\[
E_{m,B}^{R}=\ker(H_B-\lambda_m\id)^{k_m},
\qquad
E_{m,B}^{L}=\ker(H_B^\dagger-\overline{\lambda_m}\id)^{k_m}
\]
for stabilized generalized eigenspaces. If $P_{m,B}^{R,L}$ are their orthogonal projectors, define
\[
\rho_{m,B}^{R,L}
=\frac{P_{m,B}^{R,L}}{\Tr P_{m,B}^{R,L}}.
\]
For simple eigenvalues this reduces to $|r\rangle\langle r|$ and $|\ell\rangle\langle\ell|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Defective-spectrum closure]
Every finite non-Hermitian generator with a chosen cell decomposition defines a valid atlas through stabilized generalized spectral subspaces. Exceptional points and degeneracies require no change to the universal object theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite matrices have finite stabilized generalized eigenspaces. Their orthogonal projectors are positive and nonzero, and normalization gives density operators. The construction does not require diagonalizability.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Vector-resolved refinement]
When a physical protocol resolves individual generalized eigenvectors inside a degenerate subspace, the projector atlas can be refined by appending the resolved vector ensemble. The universal QRT remains unchanged because an ensemble of density operators is again an atlas.
\end{remark}

\section{Quantum divergence and statistical shadows}

Define the Uhlmann fidelity and fidelity divergence by
\[
F(\rho,\sigma)=\|\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma\|_1,
\qquad
 d_F(\rho,\sigma)=-\log F(\rho,\sigma).
\]
For classical distributions,
\[
d_F(p,q)=-\log\sum_x\sqrt{p_xq_x}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Quantum and geometric budgets]
\[
\Phi_Q^B(\mathbb X)
=\frac1M\sum_m d_F(\rho_{m,B}^R,\rho_{m,B}^L),
\]
\[
\Phi_X^B(\mathbb X)
=\frac1M\sum_m d_F(p_{m,B},q_{m,B}).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Data processing hierarchy]
For every CPTP map $\Lambda$,
\[
\Phi_Q^B(\Lambda\mathbb X)\le\Phi_Q^B(\mathbb X).
\]
If $\Lambda$ is position-covariant with induced stochastic map $T$,
\[
\Delta_Y\circ\Lambda=T\circ\Delta_X,
\]
then
\[
\Phi_X^B(\Lambda\mathbb X)\le\Phi_X^B(\mathbb X),
\qquad
0\le\Phi_X^B\le\Phi_Q^B.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fidelity is monotone nondecreasing under CPTP maps and under stochastic maps. The final inequality is data processing under the CPTP measurement channel $\Delta_X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Boundary-response susceptibility]
For any finite-outcome observation channel $\mathsf M$ compatible with the chosen cell
resolution, define
\[
\nu_{m,B}^{\mathsf M}=\mathsf M(\rho_{m,B}),
\qquad
\chi_{m,\mathsf M}(B,C)=d_F(\nu_{m,B}^{\mathsf M},\nu_{m,C}^{\mathsf M}).
\]
The susceptibility profile is
\[
\chi_{\mathsf M}^{(L)}=\frac1M\sum_m\sup_{B,C}\chi_{m,\mathsf M}(B,C).
\]
It measures the statistical response of a spectral experiment to changing the boundary
condition, independently of the boundary-tail localization profile.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-response data processing]
For every common CPTP post-processing $\Lambda$ and every admissible finite observation channel
$\mathsf M$,
\[
\chi_{m,\mathsf M}(B,C;\Lambda\mathbb X)\le
\chi_{m,\mathsf M}(B,C;\mathbb X).
\]
Also
\[
\chi_{m,\mathsf M}(B,C)\le d_F(\rho_{m,B},\rho_{m,C}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both inequalities follow from fidelity data processing, first under $\mathsf M$ and then under
$\Lambda$.
\end{proof}

\section{Boundary geometry and leakage-rate profiles}
\label{sec:boundary}

The previous versions used a single fixed collar score. V17 instead treats the entire
boundary tail as the resource. This is the correct universal object because a genuine
exponentially localized skin mode has an $L$-independent localization length, while a
fixed subextensive collar would artificially convert that localization length into a
system-size-dependent quantity.

\begin{definition}[Admissible boundary region and collar]
For $B\in\calB_L$, let $\mathfrak S_L(B)$ be the finite family of admissible boundary
regions: boundary components, boundary arcs, corner patches, or finite unions of such
regions. For $\Sigma\in\mathfrak S_L(B)$ and integer $w\ge1$, define the metric collar
\[
A_B(\Sigma,w)=\{x\in X_L:d_L(x,\Sigma)\le w\}.
\]
We always work in the regime
\[
1\ll w\ll\ell_L,
\]
so the collar resolves the local localization tail without occupying a macroscopic
fraction of the system.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Boundary leakage-rate profile]
For a cell distribution $\mu$ define
\[
\mathcal S_{B,\Sigma}[\mu](w)
=-\frac12\log \mu(A_B(\Sigma,w)^c),
\]
with the convention $-\log0=+\infty$. For the right and left spectral distributions
set
\[
\Gamma_{m,B}^{R}(w)=
\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathfrak S_L(B)}
\mathcal S_{B,\Sigma}[p_{m,B}](w),
\]
\[
\Gamma_{m,B}^{L}(w)=
\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathfrak S_L(B)}
\mathcal S_{B,\Sigma}[q_{m,B}](w),
\]
and define the paired profile
\[
\Gamma_{m,B}^{RL}(w)=
\min\{\Gamma_{m,B}^{R}(w),\Gamma_{m,B}^{L}(w)\}.
\]
The asymptotic inverse localization length is the slope functional
\[
\kappa_{m,B}^{h}
=
\liminf_{\substack{w\to\infty\\w=o(\ell_L)}}
\frac{\Gamma_{m,B}^{h}(w)}{w}.
\]
The full function $w\mapsto\Gamma_{m,B}^{h}(w)$ is retained as the universal boundary
resource profile; $\kappa_{m,B}^{h}$ is its exponential sector.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Calibrated skin profile and resource]
For $\tau\in\{R,L,RL\}$, define
\[
\Delta_{m,\tau}(B|C;w)
=
[\Gamma_{m,B}^{\tau}(w)-\Gamma_{m,C}^{\tau}(w)]_+.
\]
The finite-size profile resource is
\[
R_{\rm skin,\tau}^{(L)}(w)
=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\sup_{B\in\calB_L,\ C\in\calC_L}
\Delta_{m,\tau}(B|C;w).
\]
The corresponding exponential resource-rate is
\[
r_{\rm skin,\tau}
=\liminf_{\substack{w_L\to\infty\\w_L=o(\ell_L)}}
\frac{R_{\rm skin,\tau}^{(L)}(w_L)}{w_L}.
\]
For arbitrary scale functions $a(w)\to\infty$, define the threshold spectrum
\[
A_{L,\tau}[a,\kappa]
=\frac1M\#\left\{
 m:
\sup_{B,C}\Delta_{m,\tau}(B|C;w_L)
\ge\kappa a(w_L)
\right\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary non-creation for the full profile]
Suppose $T$ is stochastic and for every output collar
$A_{\eta(B)}(\eta(\Sigma),w)$ there is a corresponding input collar
$A_B(\Sigma,w)$ such that
\[
T_{yx}=0
\quad\text{for}\quad
 y\in A_{\eta(B)}(\eta(\Sigma),w),\ x\notin A_B(\Sigma,w).
\]
Then, for every distribution $\mu$,
\[
\mathcal S_{\eta(B),\eta(\Sigma)}[T\mu](w)
\le
\mathcal S_{B,\Sigma}[\mu](w),
\]
and hence
\[
\Gamma_{m,\eta(B)}^{h}(w;\Theta\mathbb X)
\le
\Gamma_{m,B}^{h}(w;\mathbb X).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The support constraint gives $(T\mu)(A')\le\mu(A)$ for the corresponding collars,
so $1-(T\mu)(A')\ge1-\mu(A)$. Applying $-(1/2)\log$ gives the claimed inequality.
Taking the supremum over admissible boundary regions preserves it.
\end{proof}

\section{The free category}
\label{sec:free}

\begin{definition}[Free morphism]
A free morphism
\[
\Theta:\mathbb X_L\longrightarrow\mathbb Y_{L'}
\]
is a tuple $(\Lambda,T,\{\widetilde T_C\}_{C\in\calC_L},\pi,\eta,\Gamma)$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item $\Lambda$ is one CPTP map, common to all $B$ and $R/L$ members;
\item $\pi$ is a bijection of mode labels and $\eta:\calB_L\to\calB_{L'}$ is a bijection of boundary labels;
\item state conversion holds:
\[
\Lambda(\rho_{m,B}^{h})
=\sigma_{\pi(m),\eta(B)}^{h};
\]
\item position covariance holds:
\[
\Delta_Y\circ\Lambda=T\circ\Delta_X;
\]
\item $T$ is boundary-non-creating for the chosen boundary correspondence $\Gamma$;
\item for every calibration $C$, the stochastic recovery $\widetilde T_C$ obeys
\[
\widetilde T_C p_{\pi(m),\eta(C)}^{Y}=p_{m,C}^{X},
\qquad
\widetilde T_C q_{\pi(m),\eta(C)}^{Y}=q_{m,C}^{X},
\]
and is boundary-non-creating for the inverse boundary correspondence.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Free-category closure]
Free morphisms contain identities and are closed under composition and tensor product.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity is immediate. For
\[
\mathbb X\xrightarrow{(\Lambda_1,T_1,\widetilde T_{1,C},\pi_1,\eta_1,\Gamma_1)}\mathbb Y
\xrightarrow{(\Lambda_2,T_2,\widetilde T_{2,C},\pi_2,\eta_2,\Gamma_2)}\mathbb Z,
\]
the composite uses
\[
\Lambda=\Lambda_2\circ\Lambda_1,
\qquad
T=T_2T_1,
\qquad
\widetilde T_C=\widetilde T_{1,C}\widetilde T_{2,\eta_1(C)},
\]
with $\pi=\pi_2\circ\pi_1$ and $\eta=\eta_2\circ\eta_1$. State conversion and covariance compose directly. If $T_1$ cannot move outside $A$ into an intermediate collar and $T_2$ cannot move outside that intermediate collar into the output collar, then $T_2T_1$ cannot move outside $A$ into the output collar. The same argument applies to the recoveries. Tensor products use $\Lambda_1\otimes\Lambda_2$ and $T_1\otimes T_2$ with product boundary collars.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Calibration profile invariance]
For every free morphism, every calibration $C$, every admissible collar width $w$, and every
$\tau\in\{R,L,RL\}$,
\[
\Gamma_{m,C}^{\tau}(w;\mathbb X)
=\Gamma_{\pi(m),\eta(C)}^{\tau}(w;\mathbb Y).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Boundary non-creation for $T$ gives output score no larger than input score. Exact recovery together with boundary non-creation for $\widetilde T_C$ gives the reverse inequality. Hence equality holds for the right and left profiles, and therefore for their paired minimum.
\end{proof}

\section{Monotonicity and complete resource families}

\begin{theorem}[Universal profile monotonicity]
Every free morphism satisfies, for every matched boundary condition $B$,
\[
\Phi_Q^{\eta(B)}\le\Phi_Q^B,
\qquad
\Phi_X^{\eta(B)}\le\Phi_X^B,
\]
and, for every admissible collar width $w$ and every
$\tau\in\{R,L,RL\}$,
\[
\Delta_{\pi(m),\tau}(\eta(B)|\eta(C);w)
\le
\Delta_{m,\tau}(B|C;w).
\]
Consequently the complete finite-size boundary resource profile, its asymptotic
exponential rate, and every threshold abundance are monotones.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Quantum and geometric budgets follow from CPTP/stochastic data processing. Boundary
profiles decrease by boundary non-creation. Calibration profiles are invariant by the
preceding lemma. Therefore the positive calibrated excess cannot increase for any fixed
branch; taking the supremum over matched branches and averaging over the mode bijection
preserves the inequality. Threshold abundances are consequently nonincreasing.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Free and resourceful sectors]
An atlas is skin-free when every calibrated boundary excess vanishes for all test patterns
and collar widths. It is NHSE-resourceful when at least one asymptotic threshold abundance
is positive.
\end{definition}

\section{Exact conversion as a Choi feasibility theorem}
\label{sec:choi}

Let $J_\Lambda$ denote the Choi matrix of the source-to-target map.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-dimensional Choi conversion]
Fix a finite mode bijection $\pi$, boundary bijection $\eta$, and collar correspondences. Then
\[
\mathbb X\xrightarrow{\rm free}\mathbb Y
\]
if and only if there exist variables
\[
J_\Lambda\succeq0,
\qquad
T,
\qquad
\widetilde T_C,
\]
satisfying the affine constraints
\[
\Tr_{\rm out}J_\Lambda=I_{\rm in},
\]
\[
\Tr_{\rm in}
\left[J_\Lambda
\bigl(I\otimes(\rho_{m,B}^{h})^T\bigr)\right]
=
\sigma_{\pi(m),\eta(B)}^h,
\]
\[
\Delta_Y\circ\Lambda=T\circ\Delta_X,
\]
the stochasticity constraints on $T$ and every $\widetilde T_C$,
\[
\widetilde T_C p_{\pi(m),\eta(C)}^Y=p_{m,C}^X,
\qquad
\widetilde T_C q_{\pi(m),\eta(C)}^Y=q_{m,C}^X,
\]
and the linear boundary-non-creation inequalities for $T$ and $\widetilde T_C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Choi--Jamio\l{}kowski representation is a bijection between CPTP maps and PSD Choi matrices obeying the trace-preserving constraint. State action is affine in $J_\Lambda$. Covariance is affine in $(J_\Lambda,T)$. Stochasticity, recovery, and support constraints are linear. The only discrete data are the finite mode/boundary/collar correspondences. Each fixed branch is therefore a convex semidefinite/linear feasibility problem. Conversely any feasible branch defines a free morphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete branchwise conversion witnesses]
If a fixed branch is infeasible, semidefinite/linear separation yields a dual affine witness separating the target constraints from the attainable image of the source atlas. Since there are finitely many correspondence branches, exact non-convertibility has a finite collection of branchwise dual certificates.
\end{corollary}

\section{Physical NHSE and universal faithfulness}
\label{sec:physical}

The physical criterion used here is stated independently of any resource quantity. It is the
operational boundary-sensitive content of the NHSE: a positive-density family of spectral states
has anomalous boundary accumulation under an active boundary condition, is neutralized in a
boundary-calibration experiment on the same asymptotic scale, and has a nonzero statistical
response to changing the boundary condition. This formulation is representation-independent
and does not require Bloch momentum, a generalized Brillouin zone, point-gap topology,
reciprocity, one-dimensionality, or translation invariance.\cite{AnatomySkin,Universal2D,UnifiedLocalization,QuantumGeometry,NonAbelianSkin}

\begin{definition}[Physical boundary-sensitive NHSE]
Fix an observation pattern $\tau$ (right, left, paired, or any finite experimentally resolved
sector), an asymptotic scale $a(w)\to\infty$, and constants $\kappa,f,\chi_0>0$. A generator-derived
atlas sequence has physical boundary-sensitive NHSE of pattern $\tau$ if there is a family
$S_L$ with $|S_L|/M\ge f-o(1)$, active boundary conditions $B_L$, and admissible collars
$A_{B_L}(\Sigma_{m,L},w)$ such that, for every $m\in S_L$,
\[
\liminf_{\substack{w\to\infty\\w=o(\ell_L)}}
\frac{\Gamma_{m,B_L}^{\tau}(w)}{a(w)}\ge\kappa,
\]
while
\[
\limsup_{\substack{w\to\infty\\w=o(\ell_L)}}
\sup_{C\in\calC_L}
\frac{\Gamma_{m,C}^{\tau}(w)}{a(w)}=0.
\]
In addition, at least a positive-density subfamily obeys
\[
\liminf_{L\to\infty}\frac1M
\#\{m\in S_L:\chi_{m,\mathsf M}(B_L,C_L)\ge\chi_0\}>0
\]
for some admissible finite observation channel $\mathsf M$ and calibration sequence $C_L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universal physical faithfulness]
For every finite observation pattern $\tau$ and every asymptotic scale $a(w)$, physical
boundary-sensitive NHSE is equivalent to positive calibrated QRT threshold content on the same
scale together with positive boundary-response content. In particular, for every
$\kappa,f,\chi_0>0$, the physical criterion above holds if and only if there is a positive-density
subfamily whose calibrated tail excess obeys
\[
\sup_{B,C}\Delta_{m,\tau}(B|C;w)\ge(\kappa-o(1))a(w)
\]
and whose boundary susceptibility exceeds $\chi_0$ asymptotically.
For the exponential scale $a(w)=w$, if the per-mode profile is uniformly $O(w)$, then
\[
\boxed{r_{\rm skin,\tau}>0\iff
\text{positive-density exponential physical NHSE of pattern $\tau$}.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Physical localization and calibration neutrality imply
$\Gamma_{m,B}^{\tau}(w)-\sup_C\Gamma_{m,C}^{\tau}(w)
\ge(\kappa-o(1))a(w)$ on the positive-density family, hence the calibrated resource exceeds the
same asymptotic threshold. The susceptibility condition is independent and is preserved in the
same family. Conversely, a positive calibrated threshold implies that some admissible active
boundary collar has exponentially (or $a(w)$-scale) small leakage, because $\Gamma$ is a supremum
of negative logarithmic leakages. The calibration term is nonpositive on the leading scale by the
neutrality assumption, so the physical boundary-localization criterion follows. The positive
susceptibility condition supplies boundary sensitivity. Integrating the exponential threshold
spectrum under the uniform $O(w)$ bound gives the rate statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No false positive from boundary-insensitive localization]
If a localization mechanism has the same asymptotic boundary leakage profile in every physical
boundary condition and in the calibration family, then all calibrated skin excesses vanish at
leading order. Hence boundary-insensitive non-Hermitian localization is not assigned NHSE resource.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why this is the universal bridge]
The QRT resource is not defined by calling a state ``skin''. It is defined by a directly
measurable boundary-tail profile and a calibration subtraction. The physical theorem proves
that the resulting positive slope is exactly the standard exponential boundary-localization
criterion. Because the theorem is written on a metric cell space, it does not require Bloch
momenta, point gaps, one-dimensionality, or diagonalizability.
\end{remark}

\section{Universal scaling spectrum}

\begin{definition}[Complete tail-scaling spectrum]
For any diverging scale function $a(w)$ define
\[
\mathcal A_\tau[a,\kappa]
=
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
A_{L,\tau}[a,\kappa].
\]
The complete skin spectrum is the family
\[
\{\mathcal A_\tau[a,\kappa]
:\tau\in\{R,L,RL\},\ a(w)\to\infty,\ \kappa>0\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Scaling universality]
The same QRT detects:
\[
\mathcal S(w)\sim\kappa w
\]
for conventional exponential skin,
\[
\mathcal S(w)\sim\kappa w^\alpha
\quad(0<\alpha<1)
\]
for stretched-exponential/critical stretched localization, and
\[
\mathcal S(w)\sim c\log w
\]
for algebraic tails, simply by choosing the corresponding scale function $a(w)$. Thus the
QRT does not hard-code an extensive-in-system-size scaling law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The resource is the full tail profile $w\mapsto\Gamma(w)$, while scale functions only
specify which asymptotic sector of that profile is interrogated. The physical-faithfulness
argument applies verbatim to every diverging $a(w)$ for which a leakage asymptotic exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Layer-cake identity at fixed collar width]
If $0\le\Delta_{m,\tau}(B|C;w)\le Cw$ uniformly, then
\[
\frac{R_{\rm skin,\tau}^{(L)}(w)}{w}
=\int_0^C A_{L,\tau}[a(w),\kappa]\,d\kappa.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the elementary layer-cake identity to the nonnegative modewise excess at fixed $w$ and
then average over modes.
\end{proof}

\section{Representation independence and probe completeness}

\begin{theorem}[Cell-isometric representation invariance]
Let two atlas realizations be related by cell-local isometries preserving the cell metric, boundary
family, calibration family, and admissible observation channels. Then all quantum, geometric,
boundary-tail, and boundary-response resource profiles coincide exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Isometric invariance of fidelity preserves the quantum profiles. Cell-locality preserves cell
probabilities and the boundary regions, while the induced boundary bijection preserves every
leakage exponent and susceptibility optimization.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite operational probe completeness]
Let $\mathfrak M_L$ contain all finite cell-local CPTP observation channels followed by finite
classical readout. Every finite local statistical decision experiment is a classical post-processing
of some member of $\mathfrak M_L$. Thus the full family of quantum fidelity, cell-shadow,
boundary-tail, and boundary-response profiles is complete for finite local operational tests.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every finite local POVM or instrument is a CPTP map followed by classical readout. Classical
post-processing cannot increase fidelity divergence, so agreement of the complete probe family
implies agreement for every finite local statistical test generated from it.\end{proof}

\section{Known NHSE sectors inside the universal theory}

\subsection{One-dimensional and multiband lattices}
The cell metric is the real-space graph metric and $A$ is an end or boundary collar. No Bloch representation is needed.

\subsection{Higher-dimensional, corner, and hybrid skin}
Take $X_L$ to be the lattice graph and choose collars around edges, corners, or unions of boundary components. The same leakage theorem applies. Higher-dimensional universal skin effects are therefore represented without reducing the theory to one-dimensional pumping. \cite{Universal2D}

\subsection{Quasiperiodic and disordered NHSE}
No translation invariance enters the atlas definition. This is essential for systems in which NHSE exists without point-gap topology, including two-dimensional quasicrystals. \cite{QuasicrystalNHSE}

\subsection{Many-body and Fock-space skin}
The cell set may be a finite configuration graph. Right/left spectral sectors are then probability distributions on configurations, and a boundary collar is a chosen macroscopic boundary region of that graph. Many-body critical skin effects are therefore admitted as a genuine sector rather than as an afterthought. \cite{ManyBodyCritical}

\subsection{Floquet and harmonic NHSE}
For finite Sambe truncations the harmonic index is absorbed into the internal cell degree, while the physical-space boundary remains part of the boundary collar geometry. Harmonic NHSE, including different harmonic localization morphologies, is thereby represented in the same atlas. \cite{HarmonicNHSE}

\subsection{Boundary-insensitive localization}
If a non-Hermitian mechanism produces the same asymptotic boundary-tail profile in the physical and calibration experiments, then
\[
\Gamma_{m,B}^{\tau}(w)-\Gamma_{m,C}^{\tau}(w)=o(w),
\]
so the calibrated exponential NHSE resource vanishes. This is precisely the desired behavior for boundary-insensitive localization, which should not be conflated with boundary-sensitive NHSE. \cite{UnifiedLocalization}

\section{Reciprocity, unitary covariance, and the spatial no-go theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocity is geometrically free]
If an on-site unitary $V_B$ obeys
\[
V_B H_B^T V_B^\dagger=H_B,
\]
then right and left cell probabilities coincide up to the induced on-site transformation, implying
\[
\Phi_X^B=0.
\]
For boundary-paired reciprocal experiments with no boundary concentration relative to the calibration, the skin resource also vanishes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Arbitrary unitary invariance is impossible for a faithful spatial NHSE resource]
There exist non-Hermitian matrices that are unitarily similar to complex-symmetric matrices. Hence a resource that were invariant under all unitaries could not faithfully encode cell-localized skin structure. The free unitary subgroup must therefore preserve the cell decomposition, e.g. cell permutations followed by on-site unitaries.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A cell-preserving unitary acts only by relabeling cells and on-site basis changes, which do not alter cell probabilities. A general global unitary can arbitrarily redistribute support between cells and can therefore change $\Gamma_{\rm skin}$.
\end{proof}

\section{HN exact battery}

For $g\in\R$, define
\[
H_{g,L}^O=m\id+e^gT_++e^{-g}T_-,
\]
where $T_+=\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}|n+1\rangle\langle n|$.
For $g>0$,
\[
r_j(n)=\frac{e^{g(n-1)}\sin(k_jn)}{R_j},
\qquad
\ell_j(n)=\frac{e^{-g(n-1)}\sin(k_jn)}{L_j},
\qquad
k_j=\frac{\pi j}{L+1}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[HN fidelity battery]
For every mode,
\[
R_j=e^{g(L-1)}L_j,
\qquad
q_j(L+1-n)=p_j(n),
\]
and
\[
\beta_j=b_j
=g(L-1)+\log\frac{2L_j^2}{L+1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\Phi_X^O=\Phi_Q^O
\]
mode by mode. With the PBC reference,
\[
R_{\rm skin,fidelity}^{(L)}
=
|g|(L-1)-\log(L+1)
+\log\!\left(\frac12\coth|g|\right)+o(1),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{r_{\rm skin,fidelity}=|g|}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The normalization identity follows by the substitution $n\mapsto L+1-n$. The equality of right/left Bhattacharyya overlaps then follows from the reflection relation. The large-$L$ expression follows from the exact geometric-trigonometric sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[HN boundary-tail battery]
Let $g\ne0$. For each mode $j$ let $A_R(w)$ be the last $w$ sites and $A_L(w)$ the first
$w$ sites. Then, with the right/left HN eigenstate densities,
\[
\Gamma_{{\rm skin},j}^{RL}(O;w)
=|g|w+o(w)
\]
whenever $w\to\infty$ and $w=o(L)$, uniformly up to sublinear boundary-mode corrections.
For the PBC reference, every Fourier mode is cell-uniform and
\[
\Gamma_{{\rm skin},j}^{RL}(P;w)
=-\frac12\log\left(1-\frac{w}{L}\right)=o(w).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{r_{\rm skin,RL}=|g|}
\]
for the exponential boundary-tail resource.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $g>0$, the right density has weights proportional to $e^{2g(n-1)}\sin^2(k_jn)$ and the left density is its reflected partner. Summing outside the last (respectively first) $w$ sites gives a geometric tail whose logarithm is $-2gw+o(w)$; the trigonometric factors contribute at most sublinear corrections in the regime $1\ll w\ll L$. Hence $-\tfrac12\log P_{\rm leak}=gw+o(w)$, and reflection gives the same result for the left state. On the PBC ring each Fourier mode is cell-uniform, so the mass outside a width-$w$ collar is $1-w/L$, and its leakage score is $-\tfrac12\log(1-w/L)=o(w)$. The case $g<0$ follows by reflection.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The fidelity resource and leakage-exponent resource are distinct monotones in the same QRT. Both return the exact HN nonreciprocity scale $|g|$ in the thermodynamic density, but they probe different operational tasks.
\end{remark}

\section{Operational interpretation}

The quantum budget $\Phi_Q$ governs distinguishability of the paired right/left statistical states. The geometric budget $\Phi_X$ is the same task after local cell measurement. The boundary-skin resource is estimated directly from survival probabilities in boundary collars:
\[
\gamma=-\frac12\log P(\text{outside collar}).
\]
A positive extensive value therefore means that the probability of finding the state away from a boundary collar is exponentially small in the system's macroscopic scale. This makes the resource experimentally estimable without reconstructing a Bloch band, GBZ, or point-gap invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Perturbative sensitivity]
For a simple eigenpair,
\[
\delta\lambda
=
\frac{\langle\ell|\delta H|r\rangle}{\langle\ell|r\rangle}
+o(\|\delta H\|),
\]
so the eigenvalue condition number is proportional to $e^{b}$ with
\[
b=-\log|\langle\ell|r\rangle|.
\]
For a perturbation supported between cell sets $A$ and $B$,
\[
|\delta\lambda|
\le
\|\delta H\|e^b\sqrt{q(A)p(B)}
+o(\|\delta H\|).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is first-order non-Hermitian perturbation theory followed by Cauchy--Schwarz on the restricted supports.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Chernoff normalization]
For pure right/left states,
\[
\xi_Q=-\log|\langle\ell|r\rangle|^2=2b.
\]
For the position measurement,
\[
\xi_X
=-\log\min_{0\le s\le1}\sum_xp_x^sq_x^{1-s}
\ge\beta,
\]
where $\beta=-\log\BC(p,q)$. In the HN calibration, symmetry forces $s=1/2$, hence $\xi_X=\beta$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Similarity dressing and resource cost}

For $H'=SHS^{-1}$ with invertible $S$, a non-unitary similarity can change spatial skin resource because it changes the cell probability embedding. Such operations are not declared free unless they admit a free CPTP implementation satisfying the boundary conditions. For the HN ray, the explicit diagonal similarity
\[
S_\delta=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-\delta(n-1)})
\]
changes $g$ by $g\mapsto g-\delta$ and requires conditioning growing as
\[
\log\operatorname{cond}S_\delta=|\delta|(L-1).
\]
Thus extensive changes in the HN resource carry an extensive non-unitary dressing cost.

\section{Quantum-computing interface}

The atlas layer is compatible with ancilla-assisted dilations and postselected implementations of non-unitary maps. NHSE and Fermi-skin effects have already been observed on a digital quantum computer, making the resource observables experimentally relevant rather than purely formal. \cite{DigitalNHSE} A hardware implementation can estimate $p_{m,B}$ and $q_{m,B}$ from repeated cell measurements, while $F(\rho^R,\rho^L)$ can be accessed by standard quantum-fidelity or purification-based protocols.

\section{Conclusion}

V17 establishes a universal finite-dimensional quantum resource theory for the boundary-sensitive non-Hermitian skin effect with a scale-complete boundary-tail resource profile. The universal carrier is a boundary-conditioned quantum statistical atlas; the free operations are common CPTP morphisms that preserve the calibration experiment and obey spatial boundary non-creation; exact conversion is an SDP/linear feasibility relation in Choi variables; the QRT has quantum, geometric, one-sided, paired-boundary, and boundary-response monotones; and the full tail profile and strength-abundance spectrum resolve both localization strength and the density of modes carrying it.

The decisive result is the physical faithfulness theorem. Boundary-sensitive NHSE is not inserted into the theory by naming a state ``skin''. It is characterized independently through measurable leakage probabilities in macroscopic boundary collars relative to a boundary-neutral calibration. The full logarithmic boundary-tail profile is then proved equivalent to that physical boundary-localization criterion on any asymptotic scale. The extensive specialization gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{NHSE}_{\rm macroscopic}
\Longleftrightarrow
r_{\rm skin}>0
}
\]
under exactly the calibration condition that excludes boundary-insensitive background localization. The HN chain supplies an exact battery with resource-density coefficient $|g|$.

This separation also prevents two opposite errors: the theory does not reduce NHSE to point-gap topology, and it does not label every non-Hermitian localized state as NHSE. The result is a universal quantum-statistical resource theory whose physical NHSE sector is faithful to the operational boundary-sensitive phenomenon across the broad classes of realizations encoded by the common atlas language.
