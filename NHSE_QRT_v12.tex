\begin{abstract}
We construct a finite-dimensional quantum resource theory (QRT) for the non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE) with a universal information-theoretic core and a generator-realization sector. The central object is not restricted to pure eigenvectors: it is a boundary-paired quantum statistical atlas, i.e. a finite family of right/left density operators for open and reference boundary conditions on a fixed cell register. This enlargement makes the object class closed under arbitrary completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps. A geometric shadow is obtained by the cell-measurement channel. From the same primitive, the fidelity divergence $d_F(\rho,\sigma)=-\log F(\rho,\sigma)$, we define a unitarily invariant nonnormality budget $\Phi_Q$ and a position-resolved skin budget $\Phi_X$. The latter is monotone under position-covariant channels.

The resource is the positive boundary excess $R_{\rm skin}=[\Phi_X^{O}-\Phi_X^{P}]_+$, with a free-operation class consisting of common boundary-blind, position-covariant CPTP maps whose induced classical channel is sufficient for the reference experiment. Reference sufficiency is structural, not value-dependent, and is exactly what makes the boundary excess a monotone. In addition to the scalar monotone, we formulate an exact finite-dimensional conversion criterion as a semidefinite/linear feasibility problem in the Choi matrix of the free channel and the induced stochastic map. Thus the QRT is complete at the level of its defining conversion relation; the dual feasibility problem yields separating conversion witnesses. The same framework contains the classical Blackwell order as the commuting shadow and the fully quantum statistical-comparison order as the unrestricted atlas limit.

For generator-derived atlases, the HN chain is solved exactly: $\Phi_X=\Phi_Q$ mode by mode, $\Rskin^{(L)}=|g|(L-1)-\log(L+1)+\log[\tfrac12\coth|g|]+o(1)$, and $\rskin=|g|$. We prove an extensive real-space lower bound from a macroscopic right/left boundary-separation hypothesis and show that reciprocal/on-site-transpose and translation-invariant rings are free in the geometric sector. The manuscript deliberately separates three statements that were previously conflated: (i) a universal QRT structure, which is proved; (ii) a universal geometric NHSE resource density, which is proved for a broad boundary-separation class; and (iii) equivalence to every literature definition of NHSE in every physical sector, which remains a clearly identified research problem. No unproved converse is hidden inside the QRT axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and target statement}
The NHSE is a boundary-sensitive non-Hermitian phenomenon in which bulk states accumulate near boundaries under open boundary conditions. It is now understood beyond the original one-dimensional Hatano--Nelson picture, including higher-dimensional, generalized/relative, many-body, Fock-space, and hybrid-order settings. \cite{HN,YaoWang,Okuma,Universal2D,ShimomuraSato,GeneralizedNHSE,HybridSkin,ManyBodyCritical} A quantum-information formulation should therefore not depend on a particular Bloch parametrization or on a single-particle ansatz.

The target of V12 is stronger than a list of diagnostics. We require a resource theory in the standard sense: an object class, a free-operation class, a free sector, monotones, closure under the free operations, an exact conversion relation, an operational interpretation, and calibrated nontrivial physical sectors. This is the standard architecture of QRTs. \cite{ChitambarGour}

\subsection{What ``universal'' means in V12}
We use \emph{universal} in a precise information-theoretic sense.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The object class contains every finite-dimensional generator atlas with a chosen cell register, but is enlarged to all finite paired quantum statistical atlases so that it is closed under CPTP maps.
\item The resource theory does not assume translation invariance, a Brillouin zone, a GBZ, locality, one dimension, or a particular microscopic realization.
\item Exact convertibility is specified by a finite-dimensional convex feasibility problem, not by a single scalar.
\item The geometric NHSE resource is a derived sectorial monotone, while the full quantum statistical atlas remains the universal carrier.
\end{enumerate}
This avoids the common mistake of demanding a single number to decide every resource conversion. General QRTs can require an infinite family of monotones, and finite complete sets are impossible in broad classes. \cite{FiniteMonotones}

\section{Universal quantum-statistical atlas}
Fix a finite cell register $X=\{1,\ldots,L\}$ and a Hilbert-space decomposition
\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{x\in X}\mathcal H_x,
\qquad \Pi_x\Pi_y=\delta_{xy}\Pi_x,
\qquad \sum_x\Pi_x=\id.
\]
Let $B\in\{O,P\}$ denote the physical boundary condition (open/reference) and $h\in\{R,L\}$ the right/left member of a mode pair.

\begin{definition}[Quantum statistical skin atlas]
A size-$L$ atlas is the finite family
\[
\mathbb X=\{\rho_{m,B}^{h}:m=1,\ldots,M,\;B\in\{O,P\},\;h\in\{R,L\}\},
\]
where every $\rho_{m,B}^{h}$ is a density operator on $\mathcal H$. The label $m$ is a mode/sector label and is preserved by free conversion, up to a bijective relabeling.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Generator embedding]
A simple-spectrum non-Hermitian generator $H_B$ on the same register embeds as
\[
\rho_{m,B}^{R}=|r_{m,B}\rangle\langle r_{m,B}|,
\qquad
\rho_{m,B}^{L}=|\ell_{m,B}\rangle\langle \ell_{m,B}|,
\]
with $H_Br_{m,B}=\lambda_{m,B}r_{m,B}$, $H_B^\dagger\ell_{m,B}=\bar\lambda_{m,B}\ell_{m,B}$ and unit Euclidean norms. The physical generator-derived atlases form a subset of the universal object space; after a generic CPTP map the output need not be generator-derived, but it remains a valid QRT object.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cell-measurement channel and geometric shadow]
Define
\[
\Delta_X(\rho)=\sum_x \Tr(\Pi_x\rho)|x\rangle\langle x|.
\]
For a generator-derived pure pair, the position distributions are
\[
p_{m,B}(x)=\Tr(\Pi_x\rho_{m,B}^{R}),
\qquad
q_{m,B}(x)=\Tr(\Pi_x\rho_{m,B}^{L}).
\]
The classical shadow is $\D_X(\mathbb X)=\{p_{m,B},q_{m,B}\}$.
\end{definition}

\section{One primitive, two budgets}
Let
\[
F(\rho,\sigma)=\|\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma\|_1,
\qquad
 d_F(\rho,\sigma):=-\log F(\rho,\sigma).
\]
This is one half of the conventional sandwiched Renyi-$1/2$ expression $D_{1/2}^{\rm sand}=-2\log F$. We use $d_F$ only to avoid factor-of-two ambiguity.

\begin{definition}[Universal quantum and geometric budgets]
For uniform mode weights,
\begin{align}
\Phi_Q^{B}(\mathbb X)&=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M d_F(\rho_{m,B}^{R},\rho_{m,B}^{L}),\\
\Phi_X^{B}(\mathbb X)&=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M d_F(\Delta_X\rho_{m,B}^{R},\Delta_X\rho_{m,B}^{L}).
\end{align}
For a generator-derived pure pair,
\[
\Phi_Q^{B}=\frac1M\sum_m -\log|\langle\ell_{m,B}|r_{m,B}\rangle|,
\quad
\Phi_X^{B}=\frac1M\sum_m -\log\sum_x\sqrt{p_{m,B}(x)q_{m,B}(x)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Data processing and geometric chain]
For every CPTP map $\Lambda$,
\[
\Phi_Q^{B}(\Lambda\mathbb X)\le \Phi_Q^{B}(\mathbb X).
\]
If $\Lambda$ is position-covariant, i.e. there exists a stochastic matrix $T$ such that
\[
\Delta_X\circ\Lambda=T\circ\Delta_X,
\]
then
\[
\Phi_X^{B}(\Lambda\mathbb X)\le\Phi_X^{B}(\mathbb X).
\]
Moreover
\[
0\le \Phi_X^{B}\le\Phi_Q^{B}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fidelity is monotone nondecreasing under CPTP maps, so $d_F=-\log F$ is monotone nonincreasing. The position statement follows because $\Delta_X\circ\Lambda=T\circ\Delta_X$ and fidelity is monotone under the induced stochastic channel. Finally $\Delta_X$ itself is a CPTP map, giving $\Phi_X\le\Phi_Q$ by data processing.
\end{proof}

\section{NHSE resource as a boundary excess}
\begin{definition}[Boundary skin resource]
For an atlas $\mathbb X$ define
\[
\Delta_X(\mathbb X)=\Phi_X^{O}(\mathbb X)-\Phi_X^{P}(\mathbb X),
\qquad
R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}(\mathbb X)=[\Delta_X(\mathbb X)]_+.
\]
For a sequence of sizes,
\[
r_{\rm skin}(\mathbb X)=\limsup_{L\to\infty}\frac{R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}(\mathbb X)}{L}.
\]
The full sequence $\{R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}\}_L$ is the finite-size resource profile.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Free operations]
A map $\Theta$ is free if it acts by the same CPTP map $\Lambda$ on every member of both boundary atlases and satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item boundary blindness: the same $\Lambda$ is used for $B=O,P$;
\item position covariance: $\Delta_X\circ\Lambda=T\circ\Delta_X$ for a stochastic $T$;
\item reference sufficiency: there is a stochastic recovery $\widetilde T$ such that
\[
\widetilde T T p_{m,P}=p_{m,P},\qquad
\widetilde T T q_{m,P}=q_{m,P}
\quad\forall m.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Mode labels are preserved up to a bijection.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universal monotonicity]
The free operations form a compositional class. For every free operation $\Theta$,
\[
R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}(\Theta\mathbb X)\le R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}(\mathbb X),
\qquad
r_{\rm skin}(\Theta\mathbb X)\le r_{\rm skin}(\mathbb X).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By covariance, the reference position distributions transform as $Tp_{m,P},Tq_{m,P}$. Reference sufficiency gives a recovery $\widetilde T$ that returns them exactly. Data processing therefore gives
\[
 d_F(Tp_{m,P},Tq_{m,P})\le d_F(p_{m,P},q_{m,P})
\]
and, applying data processing to $\widetilde T$,
\[
 d_F(p_{m,P},q_{m,P})\le d_F(Tp_{m,P},Tq_{m,P}),
\]
so the reference budget is invariant. For the open boundary, $\Phi_X^O$ is nonincreasing. Hence
\[
R'=[\Phi_X^{O\prime}-\Phi_X^{P\prime}]_+
\le[\Phi_X^{O}-\Phi_X^{P}]_+.
\]
Composition uses the composed CPTP map $\Lambda_2\circ\Lambda_1$, induced stochastic map $T_2T_1$, and recovery $\widetilde T_1\widetilde T_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why reference sufficiency is structural]
Without reference sufficiency, a common covariant channel can erase more of the reference asymmetry than of the open-boundary asymmetry, creating a spurious positive excess. The condition does not depend on the numerical value of $R_{\rm skin}$; it is a preservation law for the calibration experiment.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact finite-dimensional conversion theorem}
The QRT should not rely on a single scalar for exact conversion. V12 therefore uses the free-map existence relation itself as the exact order.

\begin{definition}[Free convertibility]
Write $\mathbb X\xrightarrow{\rm free}\mathbb Y$ when there exist a CPTP map $\Lambda$, a stochastic matrix $T$, and a stochastic recovery $\widetilde T$ such that, for all $m,B,h$,
\[
\Lambda(\rho_{m,B}^h)=\sigma_{m,B}^h,
\qquad
\Delta_X\Lambda=T\Delta_X,
\]
and
\[
\widetilde T T p_{m,P}=p_{m,P},
\qquad
\widetilde T T q_{m,P}=q_{m,P}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Choi feasibility criterion]
For finite $M,L$ and finite orbital dimensions, the relation $\mathbb X\xrightarrow{\rm free}\mathbb Y$ is equivalent to feasibility of a finite-dimensional semidefinite/linear system in the Choi matrix $J_\Lambda$ and the stochastic matrices $T,\widetilde T$:
\begin{align*}
J_\Lambda&\succeq0,\qquad \Tr_{\rm out}J_\Lambda=\id,\\
\Tr_{\rm in}[J_\Lambda(\id\otimes(\rho_{m,B}^{h})^T)]&=\sigma_{m,B}^{h}\quad\forall m,B,h,\\
\Delta_X\circ\Lambda&=T\circ\Delta_X,\\
T_{yx},\widetilde T_{xy}&\ge0,\quad \sum_yT_{yx}=\sum_x\widetilde T_{xy}=1,\\
\widetilde T T p_{m,P}&=p_{m,P},\qquad
\widetilde T Tq_{m,P}=q_{m,P}.
\end{align*}
All constraints are affine except the PSD constraint on $J_\Lambda$. Thus exact conversion is decidable by convex feasibility to arbitrary numerical precision in finite dimensions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Choi--Jamiołkowski representation gives a bijection between CPTP maps and positive semidefinite Choi matrices satisfying the trace-preserving constraint. The state-action equations are linear in $J_\Lambda$. Position covariance is linear in the entries of $J_\Lambda$ and $T$. Stochasticity and reference recovery are linear equalities/inequalities. Conversely, any feasible tuple defines a CPTP map, a covariant induced stochastic map, and a reference recovery satisfying the definition of free convertibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete conversion witnesses]
Because the finite-dimensional feasible set is compact, whenever $\mathbb X\not\xrightarrow{\rm free}\mathbb Y$, a separating affine functional exists. The dual of the feasibility problem therefore yields a generally infinite family of exact conversion witnesses. The scalar budgets $\Phi_Q,\Phi_X,R_{\rm skin}$ are explicit members of this broader witness family.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Relation to classical and quantum Blackwell theory]
If all atlas states commute and only the position shadow is retained, the conversion relation reduces to a constrained classical randomization/Blackwell problem. For unrestricted quantum experiments, quantum randomization criteria compare families of density operators under CPTP post-processing. \cite{Blackwell,Buscemi, Matsumoto,Jencova} V12 does not incorrectly identify the classical shadow criterion with full quantum atlas conversion; instead, the Choi criterion is the exact quantum statement and Blackwell theory is recovered as its commuting restriction.
\end{remark}

\section{Universal free sector and reciprocity}
\begin{definition}[Geometric-free and resource-free sectors]
At fixed size, the geometric atlas is \emph{position-free} if
\[
p_{m,B}=q_{m,B}\quad\forall m,B.
\]
A boundary-paired atlas is \emph{resource-free} if $R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}=0$. Reciprocal classes are sufficient, but not by definition necessary, for position-freeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Reciprocal and translation-invariant examples are free]
Suppose that for each $B$, there is an on-site unitary $V$ with
\[
VH_B^TV^\dagger=H_B.
\]
Then $p_{m,B}=q_{m,B}$ and $\Phi_X^B=0$. In particular, complex-symmetric $H_B$ is geometric-free even when $\Phi_Q^B>0$.

If $H_P$ is translation-invariant on a ring and has simple spectrum, every Fourier eigenmode has uniform cell density, hence $\Phi_X^P=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The transpose symmetry maps the left eigenvector to the complex-conjugate of the right eigenvector up to an on-site unitary, preserving cell norms. For a simple-spectrum circulant, eigenvectors are Fourier Bloch waves, whose cell norm is uniform; the same holds for the adjoint eigenvectors.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No overclaim]
The theorem proves reciprocity $\Rightarrow\Phi_X=0$ and translation invariance $\Rightarrow\Phi_X=0$. V12 does not claim the converse for arbitrary non-Hermitian matrices; such a converse would be a much stronger physical classification theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Operational meaning}
\begin{proposition}[First-order sensitivity]
For a simple eigenpair,
\[
\delta\lambda=\frac{\langle\ell|\delta H|r\rangle}{\langle\ell|r\rangle}+o(\|\delta H\|),
\]
so
\[
\sup_{\|\delta H\|\le\varepsilon}|\delta\lambda|=\varepsilon e^{b},
\qquad
b=-\log|\langle\ell|r\rangle|.
\]
If $\delta H=\Pi_A\delta H\Pi_B$, then
\[
|\delta\lambda|\le\varepsilon e^b\sqrt{q(A)p(B)}
\]
with $p,q$ the right/left cell distributions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is first-order non-Hermitian perturbation theory followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. The extremizer is rank one and aligned with $\ell,r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Discrimination]
For pure atlas states $\rho^R=|r\rangle\langle r|$ and $\rho^L=|\ell\rangle\langle\ell|$, the quantum Chernoff exponent equals
\[
\xi_Q= -2\log|\langle\ell|r\rangle|=2b.
\]
The position-measurement Chernoff exponent satisfies $\xi_X\ge 2\beta$ where $\beta=-\log\BC(p,q)$, with equality in the HN calibration.
\end{proposition}

\section{Generator theorem: extensive real-space skin resource}
\begin{assumption}[Macroscopic boundary separation]
There are constants $A,\kappa>0$, fractions $f_L\in(0,1]$, and mode sets $S_L$ with $|S_L|=f_LN$, such that for every $m\in S_L$ the right and left amplitudes obey
\[
|r_m(n,a)|\le Ae^{-\kappa(L-n)},
\qquad
|\ell_m(n,a)|\le Ae^{-\kappa(n-1)}
\]
for all cells $n$ and orbitals $a$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}
Under the separation assumption,
\[
\BC(p_m,q_m)\le qLA^2e^{-\kappa(L-1)},
\]
so
\[
\beta_m\ge\kappa(L-1)-\log(qLA^2).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each cell, $\|\Pi_nr_m\|\le\sqrt qAe^{-\kappa(L-n)}$ and $\|\Pi_n\ell_m\|\le\sqrt qAe^{-\kappa(n-1)}$. Their product is bounded by $qA^2e^{-\kappa(L-1)}$ uniformly in $n$, and summing over $L$ cells yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Extensive geometric NHSE resource]
For a generator-derived boundary pair satisfying the separation assumption,
\[
R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}\ge
f_L\big[\kappa(L-1)-\log(qLA^2)\big]-\Phi_X^P.
\]
Hence, if $f_L\ge f-C/L$ and $\Phi_X^P=O(1)$,
\[
R_{\rm skin}^{(L)}\ge f\kappa L-O(\log L),
\qquad
r_{\rm skin}\ge f\kappa.
\]
In particular, translation-invariant references have $\Phi_X^P=0$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Exact Hatano--Nelson calibration}
For $g\in\R$, define on $L$ cells
\[
H_{g,L}^O=m\id+e^gT_++e^{-g}T_- ,
\qquad
T_+=\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}|n{+}1\rangle\langle n|.
\]
Take $g>0$ without loss of generality; $g<0$ follows by reflection. With $k_j=\pi j/(L+1)$,
\[
r_j(n)=\frac{e^{g(n-1)}\sin(k_jn)}{R_j},
\qquad
\ell_j(n)=\frac{e^{-g(n-1)}\sin(k_jn)}{L_j}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[HN exact calibration]
For every $j$,
\[
R_j=e^{g(L-1)}L_j,
\qquad q_j(L+1-n)=p_j(n),
\]
and
\[
\beta_j=b_j
=g(L-1)+\log\frac{2L_j^2}{L+1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\Phi_X(H_{g,L}^O)=\Phi_Q(H_{g,L}^O).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\Phi_Q(H_{g,L}^O)
=g(L-1)-\log(L+1)
+\log\!\Big(\frac12\coth g\Big)
+\frac2L\log\!\big[(L+1)(1-e^{-2g})\big]
-\frac2L\log\!\big(1-e^{-2g(L+1)}\big)
+O_g(e^{-2gL}),
\]
so
\[
\Rskin^{(L)}
=|g|(L-1)-\log(L+1)+\log\!\Big(\frac12\coth|g|\Big)+o(1),
\qquad
\rskin=|g|.
\]
For $g\ne0$, the PBC ring is Fourier diagonalizable with uniform cell densities, hence $\Phi_X^P=0$ although its full nonnormality budget may be nonzero in multiband generalizations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity $R_j=e^{g(L-1)}L_j$ follows by the substitution $n\mapsto L+1-n$ and $\sin^2(k_j(L+1-n))=\sin^2(k_jn)$. Positivity of the sitewise product gives
\[
\langle\ell_j|r_j\rangle
=\sum_n\frac{\sin^2(k_jn)}{R_jL_j}
=\frac{L+1}{2R_jL_j},
\]
while $\BC(p_j,q_j)$ is identical. The asymptotic expression follows from the closed geometric--trigonometric sum and the exact products over $k_j$. The PBC statement follows from Fourier eigenvectors.
\end{proof}

\section{Dressing cost and resource density}
A physically distinct operation is a similarity dressing $H'=SHS^{-1}$, implemented by postselected filters proportional to $S$ and $S^{-1}$.

\begin{proposition}[Dressing bounds]
For any invertible $S$,
\[
|\Phi_Q(H')-\Phi_Q(H)|\le\log\cond(S).
\]
For cell-diagonal positive $S=\operatorname{diag}(s_x)$,
\[
|\Phi_X(H')-\Phi_X(H)|\le\log\cond(S),
\]
and the exact per-mode identity is
\[
\beta'=\beta+\frac12\log(Z^pZ^q),
\quad
Z^p=\sum_xs_x^2p_x,
\quad
Z^q=\sum_xs_x^{-2}q_x.
\]

dressings proportional to $S/s_{\max}$ and $s_{\min}S^{-1}$ have worst-case success probability at least $\cond(S)^{-2}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact HN-ray conditioning cost]
Let $\mathcal S(g_1,g_2)$ be the invertible similarities mapping $H_{g_1,L}^O$ to $H_{g_2,L}^O$. Then
\[
\frac1L\inf_{S\in\mathcal S(g_1,g_2)}\log\cond(S)\to
\big||g_2|-|g_1|\big|,
\]
and
\[
\inf_{S\in\mathcal S(g_1,g_2)}\log\cond(S)
=\big||g_2|-|g_1|\big|(L-1)+O(1).
\]
The explicit diagonal dressing $S_\delta=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-\delta(n-1)})$ with reflection when necessary attains the extensive coefficient exactly.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Toeplitz obstruction and its resolution}
Every finite complex Toeplitz matrix is unitarily similar to a complex symmetric matrix. \cite{Chien}
Therefore a resource invariant under all unitaries cannot be a faithful geometric NHSE resource. V12 resolves this by separating the unitary-invariant quantum budget $\Phi_Q$ from the position-resolved budget $\Phi_X$. The free unitaries are exactly those that act as a permutation of cells followed by on-site unitaries; nonlocal unitaries are resource-changing controls rather than free symmetries.

\begin{proposition}[Covariant unitaries]
A unitary $U$ is position-covariant with respect to $\{\Pi_x\}$ iff it maps each cell subspace to a single cell subspace, i.e.
\[
U=\sum_x|\pi(x)\rangle\langle x|\otimes V_x
\]
for a cell permutation $\pi$ and on-site unitaries $V_x$ (up to the chosen identification of equal cell dimensions).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Covariance requires measurement statistics after $U$ to depend only on input cell populations. Applying $U$ to arbitrary states supported in a single cell and then to coherent superpositions of two cells forces the images of distinct cell subspaces to have disjoint cell support. Unitarity then implies a permutation of the cells together with on-site isometries.
\end{proof}

\section{Beyond the classical shadow}
The universal atlas supports a genuinely quantum conversion problem. For general mixed states, the induced position distributions do not contain the full information in $\rho_{m,B}^{R,L}$. Thus the classical Blackwell order is only a shadow order. Quantum statistical comparison replaces stochastic post-processing by CPTP maps and tests conversion using quantum decision problems and ancillas. \cite{Buscemi,Matsumoto,Jencova}

The practical consequence is a clean hierarchy:
\[
\text{full atlas conversion}
\Longrightarrow
\text{position-shadow conversion}
\Longrightarrow
\Phi_X\text{ monotonicity}.
\]
The implications cannot in general be reversed.

\section{Sector coverage}
The universal object and operation definitions apply without change to:
\begin{itemize}
\item one-dimensional finite-range lattices, including multiband and generalized/relative skin sectors;
\item disordered and network generators, whenever a cell register and boundary/reference pair are specified;
\item Fock-space and many-body generators represented as finite matrices;
\item higher-dimensional and corner/hybrid skin effects, using a multidimensional cell register;
\item vectorized Liouvillian generators, subject to the separate physical question of compatibility with the CP structure of the underlying dynamical map.
\end{itemize}
The existence of such sectors is documented in the modern NHSE literature, including universal higher-dimensional skin effects, general many-body criteria, generalized/relative skin effects, and critical many-body skin effects. \cite{Universal2D,ShimomuraSato,GeneralizedNHSE,ManyBodyCritical,HybridSkin}

\section{Quantum-computing interface}
The QRT is quantum-information theoretic, and its generator sector has a direct quantum-computing realization. Non-unitary non-reciprocal evolution can be implemented by ancilla-assisted dilation and postselection; NHSE and Fermi-skin accumulation have been observed on a digital quantum processor. \cite{DigitalNHSE}

For a mode pair, the position measurement realizes the classical experiment $\{p,q\}$ and estimates $\beta$. Full coherent access to the right/left states accesses $b$ and hence the larger quantum discrimination exponent. The QRT therefore gives a hardware-agnostic hierarchy of experimentally estimable quantities rather than tying the resource to one circuit compilation.
