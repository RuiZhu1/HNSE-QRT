\title{A Quantum Resource Theory for the Transpose-Reciprocity-Breaking Sector of the Non-Hermitian Skin Effect via Quantum Channels}

\author{Ann Author}
\affiliation{Authors' institution and/or address}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We construct a fixed-time channel quantum resource theory for the
transpose-reciprocity-breaking sector associated with conventional non-Hermitian skin effect
(NHSE) physics. The primitive objects are quasi-local CPTP bistochastic channels, with
non-Hermitian Hamiltonian evolutions entering through normalized propagators and a canonical
Julia unitary completion. The Julia completion is a representation convention of the present
QRT, not a uniqueness claim about physical open-system realizations. The free objects are
transpose-reciprocal channels, and the free transformations are physically realizable quantum
superchannels that preserve the chosen object class and commute with channel transpose on that
class. The theory has two faithful resource monotones, the channel asymmetry $A_\diamond$ and
the generalized robustness $\Rrob$. The asymmetry has the exact operational interpretation
$A_\diamond=2p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}-1$ for discrimination of a channel from its reciprocal
transpose.

The exactly solvable calibration sector is the uniform Hatano--Nelson gauge orbit. At a fixed
two-site calibration time, $A_\diamond$ has a closed form and is strictly increasing in $|g|$
under a common strict normalization. Thus $|g|$ and $A_\diamond$ induce the same ordering on
that orbit, yielding a rigorous necessary condition for every free conversion. We do not claim
that the converse deterministic attenuation law holds for the full ancilla-assisted superchannel
class. For an explicitly defined no-ancilla unitary-congruence subclass, a phase-invariant
cosquare-trace scalar makes $|g|$ rigid, so neither attenuation nor amplification occurs there.

Normalization is treated as an operational gauge. The fixed-time resource support,
$A_\diamond=0$ versus $A_\diamond>0$, is independent of finite strict scalar normalization,
while quantitative values are uniformly comparable inside a common strict-contraction ball.
A per-Hamiltonian tight normalization gives an exactly energy-rescaling-invariant
trajectory-derived quantity $A_\diamond^{\sup}$. A uniform thermodynamic short-time estimate
with an $L$-independent remainder gives a non-vacuous lower bound for the thermodynamic resource
on the Hatano--Nelson family.

The thermodynamic locality statement is formulated at the family level. At finite $L$, the
quasi-locality definition is only a compatibility structure; genuinely non-vacuous locality
requires $L$-uniform constants. Likewise, the buffered local-channel reduction is explicitly
restricted to a tensor-product local realization of the channel. This is necessary because the
single-particle spatial Hilbert space used for the primary QRT is a direct sum over sites and does
not itself carry a canonical spatial tensor-product factorization.

The exact scope of the NHSE correspondence is class dependent. For any finite-dimensional
Hamiltonian, positivity of the trajectory-derived asymmetry is equivalent to
transpose-reciprocity breaking under the stated time-domain and strict-normalization assumptions.
Consequently, transpose-reciprocity-breaking conventional NHSE always carries positive resource.
An exact biconditional with conventional NHSE is obtained only on model classes in which
conventional NHSE is itself equivalent to transpose-reciprocity breaking. The uniform
Hatano--Nelson family is an explicit exact example. Reciprocal non-Abelian $\mathbb Z_2$ skin
physics and genuinely Lindbladian skin topology are outside the universal scope of the present
channel QRT.
\end{abstract}

\section{Operational starting point: fixed-time NHSE channels as resources}
\label{sec:operational}

A quantum resource theory is specified by an object class, a free subset, admissible free
transformations, and the induced conversion preorder; resource monotones are nonincreasing under
the free transformations \cite{ChitambarGour,LiuYuan}. In channel resource theories, the primitive
objects are quantum channels and the transformations can be represented by quantum superchannels
\cite{LiuYuan,ChiribellaSupermap}.

Our aim is to formulate a QRT in which transpose-reciprocity breaking is the channel resource
associated with the transpose-reciprocity-breaking sector of conventional NHSE. The primitive
resource object is a CPTP bistochastic channel at a fixed operational time $t$. A time-dependent
non-Hermitian evolution gives a family of such objects, but the family is a derived trajectory
rather than a new primitive process tensor or comb object.

A non-Hermitian Hamiltonian by itself does not define a CPTP map. We therefore normalize its
propagator to a strict contraction and embed it into a canonical Julia unitary dilation. This
completion is an operational representation of the normalized propagator; it is not a claim that
every open-system realization of an NHSE is equivalent to a postselected non-Hermitian Hamiltonian,
nor that the Julia dilation is the unique physical realization of the contraction.

\begin{definition}[Single-particle Hilbert space]
For a chain of length $L$ with internal dimension $M$, let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_L
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
|n,\alpha\rangle:
1\le n\le L,
1\le\alpha\le M
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The spatial basis is fixed once and for all. All reciprocity statements below refer to this
basis. When the canonical Julia dilation is formed, the environmental copy is equipped with the
corresponding copied spatial basis.
\end{definition}

Throughout, $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$ denotes the operator (spectral) norm.

\begin{definition}[Canonical dilation lattice]
For the canonical Julia completion define
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L
=
\mathcal H_L\oplus\mathcal H_L^{\rm env},
\qquad
\mathcal H_L^{\rm env}\simeq\mathcal H_L,
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}
\widetilde P_n=P_n\oplus P_n^{\rm env}
\end{equation}
be the spatial projector onto the physical and environmental degrees of freedom associated with
cell $n$. The fixed-time channel QRT below acts on $\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Admissible normalized propagator]
\label{def:normcontr}
Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional non-Hermitian Hamiltonian and
\begin{equation}
U_H(t)=e^{-iHt}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal H_{\rm adm}$ be a Hamiltonian class closed under transpose,
$H\mapsto H^T$, and define
\begin{equation}
\kappa_*(t)
:=
\sup_{H\in\mathcal H_{\rm adm}}
\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty^2
<\infty
\end{equation}
for every operational time $t$. Since
\begin{equation}
\norm{U_{H^T}(t)}_\infty
=
\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty,
\end{equation}
this normalization envelope is itself transpose invariant. Choose a strictly positive function
$\varepsilon(t)>0$ and define the common admissible normalization
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_\varepsilon(t)
=
\bigl[1+\varepsilon(t)\bigr]\kappa_*(t).
}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
K_{H,t}
=
\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa_\varepsilon(t)}}
\end{equation}
is a strict contraction:
\begin{equation}
K_{H,t}^\dagger K_{H,t}
\le
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon(t)}\id.
\end{equation}
When short-time normalization information is needed, we assume
$\varepsilon(t)\to0$ as $t\to0^+$. If
$\norm{H}_\infty\le h_{\max}$ throughout the admissible class, then
\begin{equation}
\kappa_*(t)
\le
e^{2h_{\max}t},
\qquad
\kappa_\varepsilon(t)
\le
[1+\varepsilon(t)]e^{2h_{\max}t}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
K_{H^T,t}=K_{H,t}^T.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The success branch associated with the normalized propagator is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{H,t}^{\rm succ}(\rho)
=
K_{H,t}\rho K_{H,t}^\dagger.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocity-compatible Julia dilation]
\label{prop:dilation}
For every contraction $K$ on $\mathcal H_L$, define
\begin{equation}
D_K=(\id-K^\dagger K)^{1/2},
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}=(\id-KK^\dagger)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L
=
\mathcal H_L\oplus\mathcal H_L^{\rm env}.
\end{equation}
The Julia operator
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\widetilde U_K
=
\begin{pmatrix}
K & D_{K^\dagger}\\
D_K & -K^\dagger
\end{pmatrix}
}
\end{equation}
is unitary. If $K^T=K$, then
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_K^T=\widetilde U_K.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_K(X)
=
\widetilde U_K X\widetilde U_K^\dagger
\end{equation}
is CPTP, unital, and reciprocal whenever $K^T=K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Julia operator is unitary for every contraction. The standard defect identities
\begin{equation}
D_{K^\dagger}K=KD_K,
\qquad
D_KK^\dagger=KD_{K^\dagger}
\end{equation}
together with
\begin{equation}
D_K^2=\id-K^\dagger K,
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}^2=\id-KK^\dagger
\end{equation}
give
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_K^\dagger\widetilde U_K
=
\widetilde U_K\widetilde U_K^\dagger
=
\id.
\end{equation}
If $K^T=K$, then
\begin{equation}
D_K^T=D_{K^\dagger},
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}^T=D_K,
\end{equation}
by functional calculus and $K^*=K^\dagger$ in this case. Hence the Julia block matrix
is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[General transpose covariance of the Julia dilation]
\label{cor:julia-transpose-general}
For every contraction $K$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\widetilde U_K^T
=
\widetilde U_{K^T},
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_K^{\rev}
=
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{K^T}.
}
\end{equation}
Hence, for the transpose-closed scalar normalization of Definition~\ref{def:normcontr},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{H,t}^{\rev}
=
\mathcal N_{H^T,t}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a general matrix $K$, functional calculus gives the exact identities
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
D_K^T=D_{K^*},
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}^T=D_{K^T}.
}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_K^T
=
\begin{pmatrix}
K^T & D_{K^*}\\
D_{K^T} & -K^*
\end{pmatrix}
=
\widetilde U_{K^T},
\end{equation}
which proves the first identity. For a unitary channel, channel transpose is generated by the
transposed implementing unitary, proving the second. The final statement follows from
$K_{H^T,t}=K_{H,t}^T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Canonical representation choice]
The map
\begin{equation}
K\longmapsto\widetilde U_K\longmapsto\widetilde{\mathcal E}_K
\end{equation}
is fixed as the canonical representation convention of the present QRT. The results below
therefore concern the chosen Julia-completed channel representative. They do not assert that all
Stinespring dilations of the same contraction, or all physical open-system realizations of the
same non-Hermitian evolution, are resource-equivalent.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Quasi-locality of the Julia dilation]
\label{lem:quasi-julia}
Suppose $H$ is uniformly bounded and finite range in one dimension, and the normalization
is chosen so that $\norm{K_{H,t}}_\infty\le q_t<1$ uniformly in system size. Then, at each fixed
finite time, $U_H(t)$, $K_{H,t}$, $D_{K_{H,t}}$, $D_{K_{H,t}^\dagger}$, and
$\widetilde U_{K_{H,t}}$ are exponentially quasi-local. In particular, there exist
$C_t,\mu_t>0$, independent of $L$, such that
\begin{equation}
\norm{\widetilde P_n A\widetilde P_m}
\le
C_t e^{-\mu_t|n-m|}
\end{equation}
for each operator $A$ in the list.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $H$ has range $R$, then $H^r$ has range at most $rR$, and
\begin{equation}
P_nU_H(t)P_m
=
\sum_{r\ge |n-m|/R}
\frac{(-it)^r}{r!}P_nH^rP_m.
\end{equation}
The resulting Poisson tail is exponentially small at fixed $t$. The same is true of
$K_{H,t}$ and $K_{H,t}^\dagger K_{H,t}$. Since
$\norm{K_{H,t}^\dagger K_{H,t}}_\infty\le q_t^2<1$, the binomial series
\begin{equation}
(\id-X)^{1/2}
=
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}c_rX^r,
\qquad
X=K_{H,t}^\dagger K_{H,t},
\end{equation}
converges absolutely in operator norm and preserves exponential quasi-locality under the
convolution of exponentially decaying kernels. The same applies to $D_{K^\dagger}$, and the
block form of $\widetilde U_K$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Quasi-locality, rather than strict finite range, is the natural class because the defect
operators are generally not finite range even when $K$ is.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Fixed-time resource channel]
\label{def:fixed-time-resource}
For Hamiltonian $H$ and fixed operational time $t$, define the primitive resource object
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{H,t}
:=
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{K_{H,t}}.
}
\end{equation}
This definition uses the canonical Julia-completed representative fixed above. The resource is
therefore defined relative to the chosen normalization convention and canonical representation
choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory-derived channel resource]
\label{def:process-trajectory}
For a time window $\mathcal T$, define the derived trajectory
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathfrak N_H:
\mathcal T\to\mathsf{Obj},
\qquad
t\mapsto\mathcal N_{H,t}.
}
\end{equation}
This map is not treated as a primitive process tensor or quantum comb.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional spatial transition probabilities]
\label{lem:conditional}
For a localized physical input $|m\rangle$ and conditioning on successful return to the
physical sector,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P_t(n|m)
=
\frac{|U_H(t)_{nm}|^2}
{\sum_r|U_H(t)_{rm}|^2}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The physical success probability at site $n$ is proportional to $|K_{H,t}(n,m)|^2$.
The scalar normalization cancels upon conditioning.
\end{proof}

\section{Reciprocity, locality, free channels, and free superchannels}
\label{sec:free}

\begin{definition}[Finite-size exponentially quasi-local channel class]
\label{def:qexp-fixed-L}
For fixed $L$, let $\mathfrak Q_{\exp,L}$ be the set of channels $\mathcal N_L$ on
$\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L$ for which there exist constants $C,\mu>0$ and a Kraus representation
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N_L(X)
=
\sum_aK_{a,L}XK_{a,L}^\dagger
\end{equation}
satisfying
\begin{equation}
\sum_a\norm{\widetilde P_nK_{a,L}\widetilde P_m}^2
\le
C^2e^{-2\mu|n-m|}
\end{equation}
for all sites $n,m$. At fixed $L$, the constants may depend on the channel and on $L$.
Uniformity of $C,\mu$ across a thermodynamic family is imposed separately below.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bistochastic channel class]
\label{def:bistochastic}
For finite $L$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Chan_L^{\rm bi}
=
\left\{
\mathcal N\in\Chan(\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L):
\mathcal N(\id)=\id
\right\}.
}
\end{equation}
Because all maps in $\Chan_L^{\rm bi}$ are CPTP by definition, this is precisely the
finite-dimensional class of CPTP bistochastic channels.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quasi-local bistochastic object class]
\label{def:obj-fixed-L}
For each finite $L$, let
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathsf{Obj}_L
=
\overline{\mathfrak Q_{\exp,L}\cap\Chan_L^{\rm bi}}^{\|\cdot\|_\diamond}.
}
\end{equation}
The primitive QRT objects are elements of $\mathsf{Obj}_L$. Thermodynamic statements refer
to sequences drawn from these fixed-size classes with additional $L$-uniform locality bounds.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Finite-size locality is only a compatibility structure]
At fixed finite $L$, allowing the locality constants to depend on $L$ makes the quasi-locality
condition largely a compatibility requirement: finite-dimensional channels can always be bounded
by sufficiently large constants. The substantive thermodynamic locality restriction is therefore
imposed at the family level. No thermodynamic locality statement below relies on the finite-size
condition alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Thermodynamic object family]
\label{def:thermo-object-family}
A sequence $\{\mathcal N_L\}_{L\ge1}$ is a thermodynamically admissible object family if, for
every compact operational time interval $\mathcal T_0$, there are constants
$C_{\mathcal T_0},\mu_{\mathcal T_0}>0$ independent of $L$ and $t\in\mathcal T_0$ and Kraus
representations of $\mathcal N_{L,t}$ such that
\begin{equation}
\sum_a\norm{\widetilde P_nK_{a,L,t}\widetilde P_m}^2
\le
C_{\mathcal T_0}^2e^{-2\mu_{\mathcal T_0}|n-m|}
\end{equation}
for all $n,m$, while $\mathcal N_{L,t}\in\mathsf{Obj}_L$ for every $L,t$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Channel transpose]
For $\mathcal N\in\Chan_L^{\rm bi}$ with Kraus operators $\{K_a\}$ in the fixed spatial and
environmental basis, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N^{\rev}(X)
=
\sum_aK_a^TXK_a^*.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Basic properties of channel transpose]
\label{lem:transpose-props}
For every $\mathcal N\in\Chan_L^{\rm bi}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal N^{\rev}\in\Chan_L^{\rm bi}$;
\item $(\mathcal N^{\rev})^{\rev}=\mathcal N$;
\item if $\mathcal U(X)=UXU^\dagger$, then $\mathcal U^{\rev}(X)=U^TXU^*$;
\item the definition is independent of the chosen Kraus representation;
\item transpose preserves $\mathsf{Obj}_L$;
\item the map $\mathcal N\mapsto\mathcal N^{\rev}$ is linear and continuous in finite dimension.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $L_a=K_a^T$. Complete positivity is immediate. Trace preservation follows from
bistochasticity:
\begin{equation}
\sum_aL_a^\dagger L_a
=
\sum_aK_a^*K_a^T
=
\left(\sum_aK_aK_a^\dagger\right)^T
=
\id.
\end{equation}
Unitality follows from the original trace-preserving condition:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^{\rev}(\id)
=
\sum_aK_a^TK_a^*
=
\left(\sum_aK_a^\dagger K_a\right)^T
=
\id.
\end{equation}
Double transpose returns the original Kraus operators. If
$L_b=\sum_au_{ba}K_a$ is another Kraus representation, then
$L_b^T=\sum_au_{ba}K_a^T$ and
\begin{equation}
\sum_bL_b^T X L_b^*
=
\sum_{a,a'}
\left(\sum_bu_{ba}u_{ba'}^*\right)
K_a^TXK_{a'}^*
=
\sum_aK_a^TXK_a^*,
\end{equation}
so the channel is representation independent.

Finally,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde P_nK_a^T\widetilde P_m
=
(\widetilde P_mK_a\widetilde P_n)^T,
\end{equation}
so the same locality bound holds after exchanging the two sites; diamond-norm closure is
preserved by continuity. Linearity and continuity are immediate in finite dimension because
$\rev$ is a fixed linear involution on the finite-dimensional vector space of superoperators.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Transpose is not assumed to be a diamond isometry]
The channel-transpose involution is not used anywhere below as a diamond-norm isometry. In
particular, in general one cannot assert
\begin{equation}
\norm{\mathcal A^{\rev}-\mathcal B^{\rev}}_\diamond
=
\norm{\mathcal A-\mathcal B}_\diamond.
\end{equation}
Whenever continuity of $\rev$ is needed, it is stated either as a separate finite-dimensional
continuity bound or directly as an assumption on both a channel and its transpose.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Convexity and anti-homomorphism]
\label{lem:rev-algebra}
For $\mathcal A,\mathcal B\in\Chan_L^{\rm bi}$ and $\alpha+\beta=1$,
\begin{equation}
(\alpha\mathcal A+\beta\mathcal B)^{\rev}
=
\alpha\mathcal A^{\rev}
+
\beta\mathcal B^{\rev},
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(\mathcal A\circ\mathcal B)^{\rev}
=
\mathcal B^{\rev}\circ\mathcal A^{\rev}.
}
\end{equation}
Also,
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal N\otimes\id_E)^{\rev}
=
\mathcal N^{\rev}\otimes\id_E,
\qquad
\left(\bigotimes_k\mathcal N_k\right)^{\rev}
=
\bigotimes_k\mathcal N_k^{\rev}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For composition, the Kraus operators are $A_aB_b$, and
\begin{equation}
(A_aB_b)^T=B_b^TA_a^T.
\end{equation}
The tensor identities follow from tensor-product Kraus representations.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why covariance is imposed on the complete superchannel]
Because channel transpose reverses composition order, a reciprocal pre-processing map and a
reciprocal post-processing map do not by themselves imply covariance. The correct free-operation
condition is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Theta(\mathcal N^{\rev})
=
[\Theta(\mathcal N)]^{\rev}
\end{equation}
for the complete superchannel. This is stronger and better typed than imposing a separate
reciprocity condition on the individual pre- and post-processing components.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Free channel set]
For each finite $L$, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\F_L
=
\left\{
\mathcal N\in\mathsf{Obj}_L:
\mathcal N^{\rev}=\mathcal N
\right\}.
}
\end{equation}
Thus the free channels are precisely the reciprocal subset of the allowed quasi-local
bistochastic channels.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Hamiltonian reciprocity implies channel reciprocity]
\label{prop:H-to-channel}
If $H^T=H$, then
\begin{equation}
U_H(t)^T=U_H(t),
\qquad
K_{H,t}^T=K_{H,t},
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{H,t}^{\rev}
=
\mathcal N_{H,t}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Transpose commutes with the exponential:
\begin{equation}
U_H(t)^T
=
e^{-iH^Tt}
=
e^{-iHt}
=
U_H(t),
\end{equation}
and the normalization is a transpose-invariant positive scalar. Proposition~\ref{prop:dilation}
then gives reciprocity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Free superchannels and a thermodynamic locality certificate}
\label{sec:superchannels}

\begin{definition}[Physical finite-size free superchannel]
\label{def:free-superchannel}
A deterministic quantum superchannel has the physical realization
\begin{equation}
\Theta(\mathcal N)
=
\Gamma_{\rm post}
\circ
(\mathcal N\otimes\id_E)
\circ
\Gamma_{\rm pre},
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma_{\rm pre},\Gamma_{\rm post}$ are CPTP maps and $E$ is an auxiliary memory
system \cite{ChiribellaSupermap}.

A finite-size superchannel is free if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it is physically realizable;
\item it preserves the object class,
\begin{equation}
\Theta(\mathsf{Obj}_L)\subseteq\mathsf{Obj}_L;
\end{equation}
\item it is reciprocity-covariant,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Theta(\mathcal N^{\rev})
=
[\Theta(\mathcal N)]^{\rev}
\qquad
\forall\mathcal N\in\mathsf{Obj}_L.
}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The covariance and object-preservation requirements are conditions on the complete superchannel,
not on the individual pre- and post-processing maps separately. We denote this finite-size class
by $\FreeOps_L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quasi-local CPTP preprocessing/postprocessing certificate]
\label{def:ql-cptp}
Let $\Gamma_L$ be a CPTP map whose input and output lattices carry the fixed spatial basis.
We say that $\Gamma_L$ is $(C_\Gamma,\mu_\Gamma)$ quasi-local in the Heisenberg sense if, for
every finite interval $V$, every $r\ge0$, and every observable $O_V$ supported on $V$, there exists
an observable $O_{V^{(+r)}}$ supported on the $r$-neighborhood of $V$ such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{\Gamma_L^\dagger(O_V)-O_{V^{(+r)}}}_\infty
\le
C_\Gamma |V|e^{-\mu_\Gamma r}\norm{O_V}_\infty.
}
\end{equation}
A finite-depth bounded-range circuit channel is a special case, with an error that vanishes
exactly beyond a finite light cone.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Locality certificate for a superchannel family]
\label{def:locality-certificate}
A thermodynamic family $\{\Theta_L\}_{L\ge1}$ admits a locality certificate if there are
ancillary memories $E_L$ decomposed into spatial cells with uniformly bounded local dimension,
and physical realizations
\begin{equation}
\Theta_L(\mathcal N)
=
\Gamma_{{\rm post},L}
\circ
(\mathcal N\otimes\id_{E_L})
\circ
\Gamma_{{\rm pre},L},
\end{equation}
for which both $\Gamma_{{\rm pre},L}$ and $\Gamma_{{\rm post},L}$ satisfy the Heisenberg
quasi-locality condition in Definition~\ref{def:ql-cptp} with constants bounded independently
of $L$. This certificate is required only at the family level; no covariance is imposed on either
component separately.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Thermodynamic free-superchannel family]
\label{def:thermo-free-superchannels}
A sequence $\{\Theta_L\}_{L\ge1}$ is a thermodynamically free superchannel family if each
$\Theta_L\in\FreeOps_L$ and the family admits a locality certificate with constants uniform in
$L$ on every compact operational time interval. We denote this family class by
$\FreeOps_\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Non-vacuous thermodynamic locality]
The family-level definition is the actual thermodynamic locality condition used in all
thermodynamic monotonicity statements. Thus an arbitrary finite-size global channel is not
automatically promoted to a thermodynamically free transformation merely because it happens to
belong to $\FreeOps_L$ at every fixed $L$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Fixed-time conversion preorder]
For $\mathcal N,\mathcal M\in\mathsf{Obj}_L$, write
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N\succeq_{\FreeOps}\mathcal M
}
\end{equation}
if there exists a finite-size free superchannel $\Theta\in\FreeOps_L$ such that
$\Theta(\mathcal N)=\mathcal M$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Basic closure properties of the free transformations]
The identity superchannel is free, the composition of two free superchannels is free, and every
free superchannel preserves the free set:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N\in\F_L
\Longrightarrow
\Theta(\mathcal N)\in\F_L
\qquad
(\Theta\in\FreeOps_L).
}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\succeq_{\FreeOps}$ is a preorder on $\mathsf{Obj}_L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity superchannel is physical, preserves the object class, and trivially commutes with
$\rev$. For $\Theta_1,\Theta_2\in\FreeOps_L$,
\begin{equation}
(\Theta_2\circ\Theta_1)(\mathcal N^{\rev})
=
\Theta_2(\Theta_1(\mathcal N)^{\rev})
=
\Theta_2(\Theta_1(\mathcal N))^{\rev},
\end{equation}
so covariance is preserved by composition; object preservation and physical realizability
compose as well. Finally, if $\mathcal N=\mathcal N^{\rev}$, covariance gives
$\Theta(\mathcal N)=\Theta(\mathcal N^{\rev})=\Theta(\mathcal N)^{\rev}$, hence
$\Theta(\mathcal N)\in\F_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Formal reciprocity projection]
The affine map
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\rm rec}(\mathcal N)
=
\frac12(\mathcal N+\mathcal N^{\rev})
\end{equation}
always produces a reciprocal bistochastic channel. It is a formal proof device and is not
assumed to be an operationally free superchannel on an unknown input.
\end{remark}

\section{Faithful fixed-time channel resource monotones}
\label{sec:monotones}

\begin{definition}[Channel asymmetry]
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)
=
\frac12\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Operational channel-reciprocity discrimination]
\label{prop:operational-Adiamond}
Suppose one receives a black-box channel that is promised to be either $\mathcal N$ or
$\mathcal N^{\rev}$ with equal prior probabilities. Then the optimal one-shot discrimination
success probability is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}
=
\frac12
+
\frac14\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond
=
\frac{1+A_\diamond(\mathcal N)}2.
}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)
=
2p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}-1
=
2\left(p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}-\frac12\right).
}
\end{equation}
Thus $A_\diamond$ is exactly twice the optimal one-shot advantage over random guessing for
detecting transpose-reciprocity breaking.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Helstrom theorem for discrimination of quantum channels gives
\begin{equation}
p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}(\mathcal N,\mathcal M)
=
\frac12+\frac14\|\mathcal N-\mathcal M\|_\diamond.
\end{equation}
Set $\mathcal M=\mathcal N^{\rev}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Operational meaning]
The diamond norm optimizes over reference systems:
\begin{equation}
\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond
=
\sup_{\rho_{RE}}
\left\|
(\mathcal N\otimes\id_R)(\rho_{RE})
-
(\mathcal N^{\rev}\otimes\id_R)(\rho_{RE})
\right\|_1.
\end{equation}
Thus $A_\diamond$ compares the coherent quantum processes themselves rather than only classical
transition probabilities.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity and faithfulness of channel asymmetry]
\label{thm:Adiamond}
For every free superchannel $\Theta\in\FreeOps_L$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\Theta(\mathcal N))
\le
A_\diamond(\mathcal N).
}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)=0
\iff
\mathcal N\in\F_L.
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Covariance gives
\begin{equation}
[\Theta(\mathcal N)]^{\rev}
=
\Theta(\mathcal N^{\rev}),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\Theta(\mathcal N))
=
\frac12
\|\Theta(\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev})\|_\diamond.
\end{equation}
Any physical superchannel is contractive for the diamond norm, giving the inequality.
Faithfulness is immediate from the definition of $\F_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Generalized channel robustness]
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Rrob(\mathcal N)
=
\inf
\left\{
s\ge0:
\begin{array}{l}
\exists\,\mathcal M\in\mathsf{Obj}_L,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{\mathcal N+s\mathcal M}{1+s}\in\F_L
\end{array}
\right\}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Faithfulness and monotonicity of robustness]
\label{thm:robustness}
For every free superchannel $\Theta\in\FreeOps_L$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Rrob(\Theta(\mathcal N))
\le
\Rrob(\mathcal N),
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Rrob(\mathcal N)=0
\iff
\mathcal N\in\F_L.
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F
=
\frac{\mathcal N+s\mathcal M}{1+s}\in\F_L,
\end{equation}
then covariance and linearity give
\begin{equation}
\Theta(\mathcal F)
=
\frac{\Theta(\mathcal N)+s\Theta(\mathcal M)}{1+s}
\in\F_L.
\end{equation}
Thus the same $s$ is feasible after $\Theta$.

Conversely, if $\Rrob(\mathcal N)=0$, there is a sequence $s_k\to0$ and
$\mathcal F_k\in\F_L$ with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_k
=
\frac{\mathcal N+s_k\mathcal M_k}{1+s_k}.
\end{equation}
The finite-dimensional channel set is compact, so the feasible $\mathcal M_k$ are uniformly
bounded in diamond norm. Hence
$\|\mathcal F_k-\mathcal N\|_\diamond\to0$; closedness of $\F_L$ then implies
$\mathcal N\in\F_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Global boundedness and tensor-power collapse]
\label{prop:bounded-global}
For every finite-size channel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
0\le A_\diamond(\mathcal N)\le\Rrob(\mathcal N)\le1.
}
\end{equation}
For every $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})\le1,
\qquad
\Rrob(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})\le1,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{A_\diamond(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})}{n}
=
0,
\qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\Rrob(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})}{n}
=
0.
}
\end{equation}
Thus the global fixed-time channel measures in this normalization are persistence-type
quantities rather than extensive tensor-power rates.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The diamond distance between two channels is at most $2$, hence $A_\diamond\le1$. For robustness,
choose $\mathcal M=\mathcal N^{\rev}$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal N+\mathcal N^{\rev}}2\in\F_L.
\end{equation}
This gives $\Rrob\le1$. The same argument applies to tensor powers using
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})^{\rev}
=
(\mathcal N^{\rev})^{\otimes n}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Robustness dominates channel asymmetry]
For every channel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)\le\Rrob(\mathcal N).
}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $s,\mathcal M$ are feasible, then the free mixture obeys
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}
=
-s(\mathcal M-\mathcal M^{\rev}),
\end{equation}
and the diamond distance between two channels is at most $2$. Hence
$A_\diamond(\mathcal N)\le s$, and infimization gives the result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Thermodynamic persistence}
\label{sec:thermodynamic-resource}

\begin{definition}[Thermodynamic channel family]
A fixed-time family $\{\mathcal N_{L,t}\}_{L\ge1}$ is thermodynamically admissible if, for every
compact operational time interval $\mathcal T_0$, the family admits quasi-local Kraus
representations with constants $C_{\mathcal T_0},\mu_{\mathcal T_0}>0$ independent of $L$ and
$t\in\mathcal T_0$, and each $\mathcal N_{L,t}$ belongs to $\mathsf{Obj}_L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Thermodynamic persistence]
Define
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
=
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{L,t}),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
R_{{\rm rob},\infty}(t)
=
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
\Rrob(\mathcal N_{L,t}).
\end{equation}
These are persistence quantities, not extensive densities.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Thermodynamic monotonicity]
For a thermodynamically free superchannel family
$\{\Theta_L\}_{L\ge1}\in\FreeOps_\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\infty}[\Theta(\mathcal N)]
\le
A_{\diamond,\infty}(\mathcal N),
\qquad
R_{{\rm rob},\infty}[\Theta(\mathcal N)]
\le
R_{{\rm rob},\infty}(\mathcal N).
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the finite-size monotonicity inequalities at every $L$ and take the thermodynamic
limsup. The family-level locality certificate is what makes the resulting sequence an element of
the thermodynamic free-operation class rather than merely a list of unrelated finite-size maps.
\end{proof}

\section{Normalization gauge as part of the operational specification}
\label{sec:normalization-gauge}

The channel resource defined in this work depends on a scalar normalization of the
non-Hermitian propagator. This dependence is not an ambiguity to be hidden: the normalization
convention is part of the operational specification of the present channel QRT. What is intrinsic
within the strict scalar class is the reciprocity support $A_\diamond=0$ versus $A_\diamond>0$,
whereas the numerical value of $A_\diamond$ depends quantitatively on the chosen normalization
gauge.

\begin{definition}[Admissible scalar normalization gauge]
\label{def:normalization-gauge}
Let $\mathcal H_{\rm adm}$ be a Hamiltonian class closed under transpose,
$H\mapsto H^T$. An \emph{admissible scalar normalization gauge} is a positive
function
\begin{equation}
\kappa:\mathcal H_{\rm adm}\times\mathcal T\to(0,\infty),
\qquad
(H,t)\mapsto\kappa(H,t),
\end{equation}
satisfying the following conditions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Strict contraction:}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa(H,t)>\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty^2,
\qquad
U_H(t)=e^{-iHt}.
}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Transpose invariance:}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa(H^T,t)=\kappa(H,t).
}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Thermodynamic uniformity:}
for every thermodynamic family $\{H_L\}_{L\ge1}$ and every compact interval
$\mathcal T_0\ni0$ on which
\begin{equation}
\sup_L\sup_{t\in\mathcal T_0}
\norm{U_{H_L}(t)}_\infty<\infty,
\end{equation}
there are finite constants
$0<\kappa_-<\kappa_+<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_-
\le
\kappa(H_L,t)
\le
\kappa_+
}
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$ and $t\in\mathcal T_0$.

\item \textbf{Short-time regularity:}
when a short-time expansion is invoked, there exists
$\kappa_0>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa(H_L,t)
=
\kappa_0+O(t)
}
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$ within the thermodynamic family under consideration.
\end{enumerate}

Given such a gauge, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}
=
\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa(H,t)}},
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}
=
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}}
}
\end{equation}
be its canonical Julia-completed channel.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Operational status of the normalization gauge]
\label{rem:normalization-operational}
The scalar normalization gauge is part of the operational definition of the QRT. Accordingly,
the statement $A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)})$ refers to a channel resource evaluated
after the gauge $\kappa$ has been fixed. The present theory does not claim that the numerical
magnitude of $A_\diamond$ is a representation-independent invariant of the bare Hamiltonian $H$
under arbitrary changes of normalization. What is proved below is that the reciprocity support of
the resource is invariant within the admissible strict scalar class, and that quantitative values
are equivalent up to explicit continuity bounds inside a common strict-contraction region.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact reciprocity criterion for a strict Julia dilation]
\label{prop:exact-criterion}
Let $K$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\norm{K}_\infty\le q<1.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_K^{\rev}
=
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_K
\iff
K^T=K.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The forward implication from $K^T=K$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:dilation}. Conversely, equality
of the two unitary-conjugation channels implies
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_K^T=e^{i\phi}\widetilde U_K
\end{equation}
for some phase. Transposing again gives $e^{2i\phi}=1$. If $\phi=\pi$, the upper-right block
would satisfy
\begin{equation}
D_K^T=-D_{K^\dagger}.
\end{equation}
Taking traces gives
\begin{equation}
\Tr(D_K)=-\Tr(D_{K^\dagger}),
\end{equation}
which is impossible because both defect operators are positive definite and hence have strictly
positive traces. Thus $\phi=0$, and the $(1,1)$ block gives $K^T=K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gauge-invariant reciprocity support]
\label{prop:gauge-support}
Let $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$ be any two admissible strict scalar normalization gauges. Then
for every finite-dimensional $H$ and every operational time $t$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa_1)})=0
\iff
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa_2)})=0.
}
\end{equation}
More precisely,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)})=0
\iff
U_H(t)^T=U_H(t).
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an admissible scalar normalization,
\begin{equation}
K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}
=
\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa(H,t)}},
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\left(K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}\right)^T
=
K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}
\iff
U_H(t)^T=U_H(t).
\end{equation}
Because the contraction is strict, Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-criterion} applies and gives
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)})=0
\iff
\left(K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}\right)^T
=
K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}.
\end{equation}
Combining the two equivalences proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform quantitative equivalence of normalization gauges]
\label{prop:uniform-gauge-equivalence}
Let $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$ be two normalization gauges for the same propagator
$U_H(t)$, and suppose that on the domain of interest
\begin{equation}
\norm{K_j(H,t)}_\infty
\le q<1,
\qquad
K_j(H,t)
=
\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa_j(H,t)}},
\qquad
j=1,2.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\left|
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa_1)})
-
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa_2)})
\right|
\le
C(q)\,
\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty
\left|
\kappa_1(H,t)^{-1/2}
-
\kappa_2(H,t)^{-1/2}
\right|,
}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
C(q)
=
2\left(
1+\frac{q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\right).
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set
\begin{equation}
X_j=K_j^\dagger K_j,
\qquad
D_{K_j}=(\id-X_j)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\norm{X_1-X_2}_\infty
\le
\left(
\norm{K_1}_\infty+\norm{K_2}_\infty
\right)
\norm{K_1-K_2}_\infty
\le
2q\norm{K_1-K_2}_\infty.
\end{equation}
Since the spectra of $X_j$ lie in $[0,q^2]$, the square-root functional calculus on $1-X$
gives
\begin{equation}
\norm{D_{K_1}-D_{K_2}}_\infty
\le
\frac{q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\norm{K_1-K_2}_\infty.
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds for the complementary defect $D_{K_j^\dagger}$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\norm{\widetilde U_{K_1}-\widetilde U_{K_2}}_\infty
\le
\left(
1+\frac{q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\right)
\norm{K_1-K_2}_\infty.
\end{equation}
For unitary channels,
\begin{equation}
\|\mathcal U_1-\mathcal U_2\|_\diamond
\le
2\norm{U_1-U_2}_\infty,
\end{equation}
and the same estimate applies to the transposed unitary channels. Using the triangle inequality
in the definition of $A_\diamond$ gives
\begin{equation}
\left|
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_1)-A_\diamond(\mathcal N_2)
\right|
\le
2\left(
1+\frac{q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\right)
\norm{K_1-K_2}_\infty.
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
\norm{K_1-K_2}_\infty
=
\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty
\left|
\kappa_1(H,t)^{-1/2}
-
\kappa_2(H,t)^{-1/2}
\right|,
\end{equation}
which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Large-normalization degeneration]
\label{prop:large-kappa-degeneracy}
For fixed $H$ and $t$, let
\begin{equation}
K^{(\kappa)}
=
\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa}},
\qquad
\kappa>\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty^2.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
K^{(\kappa)}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
D_{K^{(\kappa)}}\longrightarrow\id,
\qquad
D_{K^{(\kappa)\dagger}}\longrightarrow\id,
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_{K^{(\kappa)}}
\longrightarrow
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\id\\
\id&0
\end{pmatrix}
=:S.
\end{equation}
Since $S^T=S$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}
A_\diamond
\left(
\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{K^{(\kappa)}}
\right)
=0.
}
\end{equation}
Thus the numerical value of the channel resource cannot be regarded as a normalization-
independent quantity under arbitrary scalar rescaling.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The convergence of $K^{(\kappa)}$ is immediate. Functional calculus gives
$D_{K^{(\kappa)}}\to\id$ and
$D_{K^{(\kappa)\dagger}}\to\id$, hence the Julia operator converges to $S$.
Continuity of unitary channels in the diamond norm then implies convergence of the corresponding
channel asymmetry.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Normalization-gauge principle]
\label{thm:normalization-gauge-principle}
Within the strict scalar normalization class, the present QRT has the following separation
between intrinsic and gauge-dependent information:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The fixed-time support statement
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t})=0
\iff
U_H(t)^T=U_H(t)
}
\end{equation}
is independent of the admissible scalar normalization gauge.

\item Numerical resource values are not invariant under arbitrary normalization changes, but are
quantitatively equivalent under a common strict-contraction bound according to
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-gauge-equivalence}.

\item The trajectory-derived resource based on the per-Hamiltonian tight normalization is
invariant under positive overall energy rescaling when the full positive-time domain is optimized.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items (1) and (2) are respectively Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-support} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-gauge-equivalence}. Item (3) follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:tight-scale-covariance}, introduced below.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
\label{rem:gauge-interpretation}
The physically invariant statement supplied by the present construction is therefore a statement
about transpose-reciprocity breaking and its operational detectability after a specified
normalization gauge has been fixed. Claims about absolute resource magnitudes are always
understood relative to that gauge.
\end{remark}

\section{Time-optimized trajectory-derived resource}
\label{sec:time-optimized}

\begin{definition}[Per-Hamiltonian tight normalization gauge]
\label{def:tight-normalization-gauge}
For a fixed finite-dimensional Hamiltonian $H$, fix $\varepsilon_0>0$ and define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_H^{\rm tight}(t)
=
(1+\varepsilon_0)\norm{U_H(t)}_\infty^2.
}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
K_{H,t}^{\rm tight}
=
\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa_H^{\rm tight}(t)}}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\norm{K_{H,t}^{\rm tight}}_\infty
=
\frac1{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_0}}
=:q_{\rm tight}<1.
\end{equation}
For the remainder of this manuscript, $\mathcal N_{H,t}^{\rm tight}$ denotes the canonical
Julia channel generated by this gauge.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory-derived resources]
\label{def:trajectory-resources}
For a Hamiltonian $H$ and an operational time domain $\mathcal T\subset(0,\infty)$, define
the gauge-dependent trajectory resources
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)
=
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}),
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{{\rm rob},\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)
=
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\Rrob(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}).
}
\end{equation}
When the tight gauge of Definition~\ref{def:tight-normalization-gauge} is used and
$\mathcal T=(0,\infty)$, we abbreviate
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)
=
A_{\diamond,{\rm tight}}^{\sup}(H;(0,\infty)),
\qquad
R_{\rm rob}^{\sup}(H)
=
R_{{\rm rob},{\rm tight}}^{\sup}(H;(0,\infty)).
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Exact scale covariance of the tight gauge]
\label{lem:tight-scale-covariance}
For every $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{\lambda H,t}^{\rm tight}
=
\mathcal N_{H,\lambda t}^{\rm tight}.
}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond^{\sup}(\lambda H)
=
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H),
\qquad
R_{\rm rob}^{\sup}(\lambda H)
=
R_{\rm rob}^{\sup}(H).
}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since
\begin{equation}
U_{\lambda H}(t)=U_H(\lambda t),
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
\norm{U_{\lambda H}(t)}_\infty
=
\norm{U_H(\lambda t)}_\infty,
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\lambda H}^{\rm tight}(t)
=
\kappa_H^{\rm tight}(\lambda t).
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
K_{\lambda H,t}^{\rm tight}
=
K_{H,\lambda t}^{\rm tight},
\end{equation}
which gives the first identity. The second follows by the bijection $s=\lambda t$ of
$(0,\infty)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory-level free transformation]
\label{def:trajectory-free-map}
Let $\mathcal T\subset(0,\infty)$ be an operational time domain and let
$\mathfrak N=\{\mathcal N_t\}_{t\in\mathcal T}$ be a trajectory with
$\mathcal N_t\in\mathsf{Obj}_L$. A deterministic trajectory-level free transformation is a family
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathfrak\Theta=\{\Theta_t\}_{t\in\mathcal T}
}
\end{equation}
with each $\Theta_t\in\FreeOps_L$ such that
\begin{equation}
(\mathfrak\Theta\mathfrak N)_t
:=
\Theta_t(\mathcal N_t),
\qquad
t\in\mathcal T,
\end{equation}
and such that the family preserves the relevant object class and has uniform locality bounds
on every compact subinterval of $\mathcal T$. No independent time reparametrization is included
in $\mathfrak\Theta$; time reparametrization is treated separately through the exact scale
covariance of Lemma~\ref{lem:tight-scale-covariance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Trajectory-level monotonicity]
\label{thm:trajectory-monotonicity}
For every trajectory-level free transformation $\mathfrak\Theta$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
A_\diamond(\Theta_t(\mathcal N_t))
\le
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_t),
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\Rrob(\Theta_t(\mathcal N_t))
\le
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\Rrob(\mathcal N_t).
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-time monotonicity theorems apply pointwise in $t$, and taking the supremum preserves
the inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No fixed-time resource persistence law from skin length alone]
\label{prop:persistence-no-law}
For the uniform Hatano--Nelson family, scaling $H_{\lambda,g}=\lambda H_g$ leaves
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\GBZ}(H_{\lambda,g})
=
\frac{|g|}{a},
\qquad
\ell_{\rm skin}
=
\frac{a}{|g|},
\end{equation}
unchanged, while
\begin{equation}
U_{\lambda,g}(t)=U_g(\lambda t).
\end{equation}
Under a common fixed normalization $\kappa(t)$ independent of $\lambda$, with uniform
thermodynamic bounds,
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\infty}(H_{\lambda,g};t)\to0
\qquad
(\lambda\to0)
\end{equation}
at fixed operational time. Hence a fixed-time persistence number cannot be a universal
quantitative function of skin length alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The GBZ radius is determined by the hopping ratio and is invariant under overall energy rescaling.
At fixed $t$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{U_{\lambda,g}(t)-\id}_\infty
\le
e^{\lambda\norm{H_g}_\infty t}-1
\to0.
\end{equation}
The associated normalized propagator converges to a scalar multiple of $\id$, whose Julia
channel is reciprocal. Continuity of the Julia completion and of $A_\diamond$ therefore gives
the stated vanishing limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Free superchannels, dilution, and the exact scope of conversion}
\label{sec:free-super}

\begin{proposition}[No-ancilla covariant realization]
\label{prop:noancilla-construction}
Let $\Gamma\in\mathsf{Obj}_L$ and define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Theta_\Gamma(\mathcal N)
=
\Gamma^{\rev}\circ\mathcal N\circ\Gamma.
}
\end{equation}
Then $\Theta_\Gamma$ is a physical free superchannel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Bistochasticity of $\Gamma$ implies bistochasticity of $\Gamma^{\rev}$. Quasi-locality and
object preservation follow from $\Gamma\in\mathsf{Obj}_L$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:transpose-props}.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rev-algebra},
\begin{align}
[\Theta_\Gamma(\mathcal N)]^{\rev}
&=
(\Gamma^{\rev}\circ\mathcal N\circ\Gamma)^{\rev}
\\
&=
\Gamma^{\rev}\circ\mathcal N^{\rev}\circ\Gamma
\\
&=
\Theta_\Gamma(\mathcal N^{\rev}).
\end{align}
Thus the covariance condition holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Covariant dilution by a reciprocal branch]
\label{prop:dilution}
Fix $\mathcal R_0\in\F_L$ and $p\in[0,1]$. Suppose the laboratory control model admits a
deterministic randomized switch that applies $\mathcal N$ with probability $p$ and the fixed
reciprocal channel $\mathcal R_0$ with probability $1-p$, with no object-class violation. Then
\begin{equation}
\Theta_p(\mathcal N)
=
p\mathcal N+(1-p)\mathcal R_0
\end{equation}
is a free superchannel and
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\Theta_p(\mathcal N))
=
pA_\diamond(\mathcal N),
\qquad
\Rrob(\Theta_p(\mathcal N))
\le
p\Rrob(\mathcal N).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The covariance and asymmetry identity follow by linearity. If
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal N+s\mathcal M}{1+s}
=
\mathcal F\in\F_L,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\Theta_p(\mathcal N)+ps\mathcal M
=
p(1+s)\mathcal F+(1-p)\mathcal R_0,
\end{equation}
which is free after division by $1+ps$. Hence $ps$ is feasible, and taking the infimum gives
the robustness bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Ancilla-free superchannel]
A superchannel is ancilla free if it has the form
\begin{equation}
\Theta(\mathcal N)
=
\Gamma_{\rm post}\circ\mathcal N\circ\Gamma_{\rm pre}
\end{equation}
with no memory system. The main QRT does not assume that arbitrary ancilla-free covariant
superchannels can be classified from covariance restricted to $\mathsf{Obj}_L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Unitary-congruence free subclass]
For a unitary $V$ let $\mathcal V(X)=VXV^\dagger$. Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Theta_V(\mathcal N)
=
\mathcal V^{\rev}\circ\mathcal N\circ\mathcal V,
\qquad
\mathcal V^{\rev}(X)=V^TXV^*.
}
\end{equation}
For each finite $L$, $\FreeOps_{\rm uc}(L)$ denotes the set of such $\Theta_V$ that preserve
$\mathsf{Obj}_L$; for thermodynamic families we additionally require uniform quasi-locality
of the implementing unitaries. Every member is a deterministic, ancilla-free, physical free
superchannel.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The full ancilla-assisted covariant superchannel class, and even the full ancilla-free covariant
class beyond $\FreeOps_{\rm uc}(L)$, are not classified here. In particular, deterministic
attenuation $\mathcal N_g\to\mathcal N_{g'}$ for $|g'|<|g|$ is not inferred from the monotones.
Any sufficiency statement for the full class requires an explicit physical protocol.
\end{remark}

\section{Concrete Hatano--Nelson channel calibration}
\label{sec:example}

Consider the two-site calibration Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H_0
=
m\id+
t_0(|1\rangle\langle2|+|2\rangle\langle1|),
\end{equation}
and the gauge family
\begin{equation}
H_g
=
S_gH_0S_g^{-1},
\qquad
S_g|1\rangle=|1\rangle,
\qquad
S_g|2\rangle=e^{-g}|2\rangle,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
H_g
=
m\id
+
t_0
\left(
e^g|1\rangle\langle2|
+
e^{-g}|2\rangle\langle1|
\right).
\end{equation}
Throughout this calibration, we assume $m,t_0\in\mathbb R$ with $t_0>0$.

At
\begin{equation}
\tau_*=\frac{\pi}{2t_0},
\end{equation}
one has, up to the global phase $e^{-im\tau_*}$,
\begin{equation}
U_g(\tau_*)
=
-i
\begin{pmatrix}
0&e^g\\
e^{-g}&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\norm{U_g(\tau_*)}_\infty=e^{|g|}.
\end{equation}
For a common normalization $\kappa>e^{2g_{\max}}$ on $|g|\le g_{\max}$, set
\begin{equation}
u=\kappa^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
K_g
=
-iu
\begin{pmatrix}
0&e^g\\
e^{-g}&0
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and the Julia defects are
\begin{equation}
D_K=\diag(a,b),
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}=\diag(b,a),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
a=\sqrt{1-u^2e^{-2g}},
\qquad
b=\sqrt{1-u^2e^{2g}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact two-site channel asymmetry]
\label{thm:gauge-nogo}
For the two-site Hatano--Nelson calibration at $\tau_*$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})
=
\sqrt{1-c(g)^2},
}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
c(g)
=
u^2+
\sqrt{
(1-u^2e^{2g})(1-u^2e^{-2g})
}.
}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $g>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dg}A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})>0.
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The relative unitary for discrimination of
$\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{K_g}$ and
$\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{K_g}^{\rev}$ is
\begin{equation}
W_g
=
\widetilde U_{K_g}^\dagger
\widetilde U_{K_g}^T.
\end{equation}
For the calibration unitary, direct block multiplication gives that the spectrum of $W_g$
consists of $e^{\pm i\theta(g)}$, each with multiplicity two, with
\begin{equation}
\cos\theta(g)=u^2+ab=c(g).
\end{equation}
For unitary channels, the exact discrimination formula gives
\begin{equation}
\frac12
\|\mathcal U-\mathcal V\|_\diamond
=
\sqrt{1-\nu(W)^2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\nu(W)
=
\min_{\|\psi\|=1}
|\langle\psi|W|\psi\rangle|.
\end{equation}
Because the spectrum is $e^{\pm i\theta}$ with $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$ in the strict calibration
regime, the numerical range is the line segment between these two eigenvalues and
$\nu(W)=\cos\theta=c(g)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond=\sqrt{1-c(g)^2}.
\end{equation}
Writing $y=e^{2g}>1$ gives
\begin{equation}
c(g)
=
u^2+
\sqrt{
1+u^4-u^2(y+y^{-1})
},
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{equation}
c'(g)
=
-\frac{
u^2(y-y^{-1})
}{
\sqrt{
1+u^4-u^2(y+y^{-1})
}
}
<0.
\end{equation}
Therefore $A_\diamond'(g)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact scalar ordering on the calibration orbit]
\label{cor:HN-monotone-order}
For the common normalization of Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge-nogo},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|g|\ge|g'|
\iff
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})
\ge
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}).
}
\end{equation}
Consequently every deterministic free conversion
$\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*}\succeq_{\FreeOps}\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}$
must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|g|\ge|g'|.
}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first statement follows from evenness in $g$ and strict monotonicity in $|g|$. The second
follows from monotonicity of $A_\diamond$ under free superchannels.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Phase-invariant unitary-congruence diagnostic]
\label{lem:cosquare}
For a unitary $U$ acting on a $d$-dimensional space define the unitary-congruence cosquare
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
C_{\rm cs}(U)=U^*U,
\qquad
\chi_{\rm cs}(U)
=
\frac1{d^2}
\left|\Tr(U^*U)\right|^2.
}
\end{equation}
Under
\begin{equation}
U\longmapsto e^{i\phi}V^TUV,
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\chi_{\rm cs}(e^{i\phi}V^TUV)
=
\chi_{\rm cs}(U).
}
\end{equation}
For the two-site Julia unitary at $\tau_*$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\chi_{\rm cs}(\widetilde U_{K_g})
=
c(g)^2,
}
\end{equation}
with $c(g)$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge-nogo}. Since $c(g)>0$ and is strictly decreasing
in $|g|$ for $|g|>0$, $\chi_{\rm cs}$ is strictly decreasing in $|g|$ on the calibration orbit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\begin{equation}
U'=e^{i\phi}V^TUV.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
(U')^*U'
=
V^\dagger U^*(V^*V^T)UV
=
V^\dagger(U^*U)V,
\end{equation}
so $C_{\rm cs}$ is unitarily similar to the original cosquare and its trace is invariant; the
phase cancels exactly. For a unitary $U$,
\begin{equation}
U^*U=(U^\dagger U^T)^\dagger,
\end{equation}
so it has the complex-conjugate eigenvalue multiset of the discrimination relative unitary.
In the present calibration the spectrum is the conjugate-symmetric set
$e^{\pm i\theta}$, each twice, and therefore
\begin{equation}
\Tr(\widetilde U_{K_g}^*\widetilde U_{K_g})
=
4\cos\theta(g)
=
4c(g).
\end{equation}
The four-dimensional normalization gives $\chi_{\rm cs}=c(g)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No nontrivial conversion in the ancilla-free unitary-congruence subclass]
\label{thm:rigidity}
Suppose $\Theta_V\in\FreeOps_{\rm uc}(L)$ maps
$\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*}$ to $\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|g|=|g'|.
}
\end{equation}
Thus no nontrivial attenuation or amplification occurs inside this explicitly defined
ancilla-free unitary-congruence subclass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality of the output unitary channels implies that their implementing unitaries differ only
by a global phase, so
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_{K_{g'}}
=
e^{i\phi}V^T\widetilde U_{K_g}V
\end{equation}
for some phase $\phi$. Lemma~\ref{lem:cosquare} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
c(g')^2=c(g)^2.
\end{equation}
Both $c(g)$ and $c(g')$ are positive, so $c(g')=c(g)$; strict monotonicity in $|g|$ then
implies $|g|=|g'|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status of the full conversion problem]
The rigorous conversion information established here has three distinct layers. First, the
monotone $A_\diamond$ gives the necessary condition $|g|\ge|g'|$ for any free conversion on the
calibration orbit. Second, the explicitly defined subclass $\FreeOps_{\rm uc}(L)$ is rigid:
within it, $|g|$ cannot change at all. Third, the full ancilla-assisted preorder remains open.
In particular, the sufficient direction
\begin{equation}
|g|>|g'|
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*}\succeq_{\FreeOps}\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}
\end{equation}
is not claimed and cannot be inferred from monotonicity alone. Establishing it requires an
explicit deterministic attenuation protocol in the full free-operation model.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact scope of the NHSE--resource correspondence}
\label{sec:NHSE-scope}

The channel resource constructed above detects transpose-reciprocity breaking. Conventional
non-Hermitian skin accumulation is a physical phenomenon that may arise through different
mechanisms. The exact logical scope of the correspondence is therefore stated in three layers:
a universal statement about transpose-reciprocity breaking, a one-way witness statement for the
corresponding NHSE sector, and a biconditional statement on model classes for which conventional
NHSE is itself equivalent to transpose-reciprocity breaking.

\begin{definition}[Transpose-reciprocity-breaking class]
\label{def:TR-breaking-class}
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\rm TRB}
=
\{H:\ H^T\ne H\}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Conventional NHSE class]
\label{def:conventional-NHSE-class}
Let $\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}$ denote a class of one-dimensional finite-range
Hamiltonians with open boundaries satisfying the conventional extensive skin criterion: there
exists an extensive bulk spectral window $I$ and a sequence $\ell_L=o(L)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
C_I^{\rm edge}(L,\ell_L)>0.
}
\end{equation}
The definition is based on OBC eigenstate concentration and does not by itself impose transpose
reciprocity breaking.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Transpose-reciprocity-breaking conventional NHSE class]
\label{def:TR-NHSE-class}
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\rm TR\text{-}NHSE}
=
\mathcal C_{\rm TRB}
\cap
\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}.
}
\end{equation}
Thus membership in $\mathcal C_{\rm TR\text{-}NHSE}$ explicitly includes both conventional OBC
skin accumulation and transpose-reciprocity breaking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact trajectory-level characterization of transpose-reciprocity breaking]
\label{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization}
Let $H$ be finite dimensional and let the operational time domain $\mathcal T$ contain a sequence
\begin{equation}
t_j\downarrow0.
\end{equation}
For any admissible strict scalar normalization $\kappa$, define
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)
=
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)>0
\iff
H^T\ne H.
}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)=0
\iff
H^T=H.
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $H^T=H$, then
\begin{equation}
U_H(t)^T=U_H(t)
\end{equation}
for every $t$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:H-to-channel} gives
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)})=0
\end{equation}
for every $t$. Hence the supremum vanishes.

Conversely, suppose $H^T\ne H$. The short-time expansion gives
\begin{equation}
U_H(t)^T-U_H(t)
=
-i(H^T-H)t+O(t^2).
\end{equation}
Since $H^T-H\ne0$, the analytic matrix-valued function
$t\mapsto U_H(t)^T-U_H(t)$ cannot vanish identically in any neighborhood of $t=0$. Therefore
there exists a sequence $t_j\downarrow0$ for which
\begin{equation}
U_H(t_j)^T\ne U_H(t_j).
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-support},
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t_j}^{(\kappa)})>0,
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-way resource witness for the transpose-breaking NHSE sector]
\label{cor:TR-NHSE-witness}
For every
\begin{equation}
H\in\mathcal C_{\rm TR\text{-}NHSE},
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0.
}
\end{equation}
Thus positivity of the trajectory-derived channel resource is a necessary operational
consequence of membership in the transpose-reciprocity-breaking sector of conventional NHSE.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $H\in\mathcal C_{\rm TR\text{-}NHSE}$ implies $H^T\ne H$. Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization}
then gives $A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0$ for the tight gauge.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not claimed by the witness theorem]
\label{rem:witness-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization} is a theorem about transpose-reciprocity
breaking, not a theorem that every Hamiltonian exhibiting conventional NHSE must satisfy
$H^T\ne H$. Consequently, the implication
\begin{equation}
\text{conventional NHSE}
\Longrightarrow
A_\diamond^{\sup}>0
\end{equation}
is asserted here only for the explicitly defined sector $\mathcal C_{\rm TR\text{-}NHSE}$.
No universal converse for the full set $\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}$ is claimed.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Transpose-reciprocity-complete NHSE class]
\label{def:TR-complete-class}
A model class $\mathcal C$ is called \emph{transpose-reciprocity complete with respect to
conventional NHSE} if
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H\in\mathcal C
\Longrightarrow
\left[
H\in\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}
\iff
H^T\ne H
\right].
}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, within $\mathcal C$, conventional OBC skin accumulation and transpose-reciprocity
breaking define the same subset.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact NHSE--resource equivalence on a complete model class]
\label{thm:NHSE-resource-complete}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a transpose-reciprocity-complete NHSE class in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:TR-complete-class}. Suppose its operational time domain contains a sequence
$t_j\downarrow0$, and let the channel resource be defined with any admissible strict scalar
normalization or with the per-Hamiltonian tight normalization. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H\in\mathcal C
\Longrightarrow
\left[
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0
\iff
H\in\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}
\right].
}
\end{equation}
Thus the channel resource is an exact detector of conventional NHSE within the stated complete
model class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization},
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0
\iff
H^T\ne H.
\end{equation}
By the defining property of the class $\mathcal C$,
\begin{equation}
H^T\ne H
\iff
H\in\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}.
\end{equation}
Combining the two equivalences proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Logical hierarchy]
\label{rem:logical-hierarchy}
The preceding results have three distinct logical strengths:
\begin{align}
H^T\ne H
&\Longrightarrow
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)>0,
\label{eq:TR-to-resource}
\\
H\in\mathcal C_{\rm TR\text{-}NHSE}
&\Longrightarrow
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0,
\label{eq:TRNHSE-to-resource}
\\
H\in\mathcal C,\quad
\mathcal C\ {\rm complete}
&\Longrightarrow
\left[
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0
\iff
H\in\mathcal C_{\rm NHSE}^{\rm conv}
\right].
\label{eq:complete-resource-equivalence}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:TR-to-resource} is universal within the finite-dimensional transpose-breaking
channel architecture. Equation~\eqref{eq:TRNHSE-to-resource} is the one-way NHSE witness
statement. Equation~\eqref{eq:complete-resource-equivalence} is the class-conditional exact
correspondence. No stronger universal NHSE equivalence is used elsewhere in the paper.
\end{remark}

\section{Diagnostic sector I: non-Bloch spectral structure}
\label{sec:diagnostic1}

The channel QRT is the primitive theory. Spectral quantities below are structured Hamiltonian
diagnostics, not additional free-operation monotones.

For a translation-invariant one-dimensional lattice,
\begin{equation}
H(\beta)=\sum_{r=-p}^{q}H_r\beta^r.
\end{equation}
For a fixed energy $E$, let the roots of
\begin{equation}
\det[H(\beta)-E]=0
\end{equation}
be ordered as
\begin{equation}
|\beta_1|\le\cdots\le|\beta_{2M}|.
\end{equation}
For nearest-neighbor $M$-channel models, the GBZ condition is
\begin{equation}
|\beta_M|=|\beta_{M+1}|.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[GBZ radius witness]
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rho_{\GBZ}(H)
=
\frac1a
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal C_{\GBZ}}
|\ln|\beta||.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Reciprocal root pairing]
\label{lem:reciprocal-roots}
Consider a clean reciprocal nearest-neighbor chain with invertible intercell hopping blocks and
\begin{equation}
H_{-1}=H_1^T,
\qquad
H_0=H_0^T.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
H(\beta)^T=H(\beta^{-1}),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\det[H(\beta)-E]
=
\det[H(\beta^{-1})-E].
}
\end{equation}
Thus nonzero roots occur in reciprocal pairs
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\beta\leftrightarrow\beta^{-1}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
H(\beta)^T
=
H_{-1}^T\beta^{-1}
+
H_0^T
+
H_1^T\beta
=
H_1\beta^{-1}
+
H_0
+
H_{-1}\beta
=
H(\beta^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Taking determinants proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reciprocity forces zero determinant winding]
\label{thm:winding-vanishes}
For a clean reciprocal chain and a reference energy $E$ in a point gap,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
W_{\rm BZ}(E)
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
dk\,\partial_k\log\det[H(k)-E]
=
0.
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Reciprocity implies $H(-k)=H(k)^T$, hence
\begin{equation}
\det[H(-k)-E]
=
\det[H(k)-E].
\end{equation}
Thus the logarithmic derivative is odd on the Brillouin zone and the integral vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reciprocal GBZ is the unit circle]
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:reciprocal-roots},
\begin{equation}
|\beta_M|=|\beta_{M+1}|=1,
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rho_{\GBZ}=0.
}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Reciprocal pairing gives
\begin{equation}
|\beta_j|\,|\beta_{2M+1-j}|=1.
\end{equation}
At the GBZ crossing, the two middle moduli are equal, so both are unity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hatano--Nelson spectral calibration]
\label{prop:HN-calib}
For
\begin{equation}
H_g(\beta)
=
m+Je^g\beta+Je^{-g}\beta^{-1},
\qquad
g\in\mathbb R,
\end{equation}
the two roots of $H_g(\beta)=E$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\beta_1\beta_2=e^{-2g},
\end{equation}
and the scalar GBZ radius is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rho_{\GBZ}=\frac{|g|}{a}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying the characteristic equation by $\beta$ gives a quadratic whose leading and
constant coefficients differ by $e^{\pm g}$, so the product of its roots is $e^{-2g}$. The
scalar GBZ condition forces equal root moduli and therefore
$|\beta_{\GBZ}|=e^{-|g|}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Necessity and uniform short-time bridge}
\label{sec:necessity}

\begin{assumption}[Clean finite-range class]
\label{ass:class}
$H$ is a one-dimensional translation-invariant finite-range single-particle Hamiltonian with
internal dimension $M$. For GBZ root-pairing conclusions we additionally assume nearest-neighbor
intercell blocks and their invertibility.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Real-space reciprocity]
For a translation-invariant Hamiltonian,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H_r=H_{-r}^T
\quad\text{for all }r.
}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the finite-size matrix satisfies $H^T=H$ in the fixed spatial basis.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Reciprocity is a necessary obstruction to conventional reciprocal-breaking NHSE]
\label{cor:necessity}
Within the clean class,
\begin{equation}
H^T=H
\Longrightarrow
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t})=0,
\qquad
W_{\rm BZ}(E)=0,
\end{equation}
and under the nearest-neighbor assumptions,
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\GBZ}=0.
\end{equation}
Therefore every conventional NHSE mechanism in the present transpose-reciprocity-breaking
sector must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{conventional transpose-reciprocity-breaking NHSE}
\Longrightarrow
\exists t:\ A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t})>0.
}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The channel statement follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:H-to-channel}; the spectral statements
follow from Sec.~\ref{sec:diagnostic1}. The final implication follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization} on the stated sector.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Transition-probability asymmetry defect]
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Damp(H)
=
\max_m
\sum_{n\ne m}
\left|
|H_{nm}|^2-|H_{mn}|^2
\right|.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Uniform thermodynamic short-time control}
\label{sec:uniform-short-time}

The thermodynamic short-time statement requires more than a pointwise expansion at each finite
system size. To prevent the remainder from growing with $L$, the small-time estimate is formulated
with an explicit system-size-independent bound.

\begin{assumption}[Uniformly bounded thermodynamic Hamiltonian family]
\label{ass:uniform-H-family}
Let $\{H_L\}_{L\ge1}$ be a family of finite-dimensional Hamiltonians satisfying
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sup_{L\ge1}\norm{H_L}_\infty
\le
h<\infty.
}
\end{equation}
For the thermodynamic NHSE applications below, we additionally assume uniformly bounded finite
interaction range.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform exponential remainder for the propagator]
\label{lem:uniform-propagator-remainder}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-H-family},
\begin{equation}
U_L(t)
=
\id-iH_Lt+R_L(t),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{R_L(t)}_\infty
\le
\frac12h^2t^2e^{ht}
}
\end{equation}
for every $L$ and $t\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using the exponential series,
\begin{align}
R_L(t)
&=
\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-itH_L)^r}{r!},
\\
\norm{R_L(t)}_\infty
&\le
\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{(ht)^r}{r!}
\le
\frac12h^2t^2e^{ht}.
\end{align}
The bound is independent of $L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform short-time control of transition intensities]
\label{lem:uniform-transition-intensity}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-H-family}, for every $m$ and every $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n\ne m}
\left|
|U_L(t)_{nm}|^2
-
t^2|(H_L)_{nm}|^2
\right|
\le
\mathcal E_h(t),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n\ne m}
\left|
|U_L(t)_{mn}|^2
-
t^2|(H_L)_{mn}|^2
\right|
\le
\mathcal E_h(t),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal E_h(t)
=
h^3t^3e^{ht}
+
\frac14h^4t^4e^{2ht}.
}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\max_m
\sum_{n\ne m}
\left|
|U_L(t)_{nm}|^2
-
|U_L(t)_{mn}|^2
-
t^2
\left(
|(H_L)_{nm}|^2
-
|(H_L)_{mn}|^2
\right)
\right|
\le
2\mathcal E_h(t).
}
\end{equation}
All constants are independent of $L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\begin{equation}
U_L(t)_{nm}
=
-it(H_L)_{nm}
+
R_L(t)_{nm}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
\left|
|U_L(t)_{nm}|^2
-
t^2|(H_L)_{nm}|^2
\right|
&\le
2t|(H_L)_{nm}||R_L(t)_{nm}|
+
|R_L(t)_{nm}|^2.
\end{align}
Summing and using Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align}
\sum_n
2t|(H_L)_{nm}||R_L(t)_{nm}|
&\le
2t
\left(
\sum_n|(H_L)_{nm}|^2
\right)^{1/2}
\left(
\sum_n|R_L(t)_{nm}|^2
\right)^{1/2}
\\
&\le
2th\norm{R_L(t)}_\infty
\\
&\le
h^3t^3e^{ht},
\end{align}
while
\begin{equation}
\sum_n|R_L(t)_{nm}|^2
\le
\norm{R_L(t)}_\infty^2
\le
\frac14h^4t^4e^{2ht}.
\end{equation}
This proves the first estimate. The row estimate follows by applying the same argument to
$U_L(t)^\dagger$ and $H_L^\dagger$. Combining the two estimates gives the final inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform finite-size short-time resource lower bound]
\label{prop:uniform-shorttime-bridge}
Let $\{H_L\}$ satisfy Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-H-family}, and let $\kappa_L(t)$ be an
admissible scalar normalization gauge satisfying
\begin{equation}
\kappa_L(t)
=
\kappa_0+O(t)
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$, with $\kappa_0>0$. Then there exist $t_0>0$ and $C_*>0$, independent
of $L$, such that for all $0<t<t_0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H_L,t})
\ge
\frac{t^2}{2\kappa_0}\Damp(H_L)
-
C_*t^3.
}
\end{equation}
Thus the leading short-time \emph{lower-bound coefficient} is uniform in system size.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:Adiamond-probe},
\begin{align}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H_L,t})
&\ge
\frac1{2\kappa_L(t)}
\max_m
\sum_n
\left|
|U_L(t)_{nm}|^2-|U_L(t)_{mn}|^2
\right|.
\end{align}
The diagonal term $n=m$ vanishes identically. Hence
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-transition-intensity} gives
\begin{equation}
\max_m
\sum_{n\ne m}
\left|
|U_L(t)_{nm}|^2-|U_L(t)_{mn}|^2
\right|
\ge
t^2\Damp(H_L)-2\mathcal E_h(t).
\end{equation}
Because
\begin{equation}
\kappa_L(t)^{-1}
=
\kappa_0^{-1}+O(t)
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$, and $\Damp(H_L)$ is uniformly bounded for a finite-range uniformly bounded
family, the normalization correction contributes only an $L$-independent $O(t^3)$ term.
The remainder $2\mathcal E_h(t)$ is likewise $O(t^3)$ uniformly in $L$. Collecting constants
proves the stated lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Thermodynamic small-time lower bound]
\label{cor:uniform-thermo-shorttime}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-shorttime-bridge},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
}{t^2}
\ge
\frac1{2\kappa_0}
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
\Damp(H_L).
}
\end{equation}
In particular, if
\begin{equation}
\limsup_{L\to\infty}\Damp(H_L)>0,
\end{equation}
then the thermodynamic channel resource is strictly positive at sufficiently small nonzero times.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
From Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-shorttime-bridge},
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H_L,t})
\ge
\frac{t^2}{2\kappa_0}\Damp(H_L)-C_*t^3.
\end{equation}
Taking $\limsup_{L\to\infty}$ gives
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
\ge
\frac{t^2}{2\kappa_0}
\limsup_{L\to\infty}\Damp(H_L)
-
C_*t^3.
\end{equation}
Divide by $t^2$ and take $\liminf_{t\to0^+}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform thermodynamic persistence of the Hatano--Nelson channel resource]
\label{prop:HN-thermo-persistence-uniform}
Consider the open uniform Hatano--Nelson family
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H_{g,L}
=
m\id
+
Je^gT_+
+
Je^{-g}T_-,
\qquad
T_-=T_+^T,
}
\end{equation}
with fixed $J>0$, $g\in\mathbb R$, and system-independent $m,J,g$. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{H_{g,L}}_\infty
\le
|m|+2J\cosh|g|
=:h_g
}
\end{equation}
for every $L$. Moreover, for all $L\ge3$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Damp(H_{g,L})
=
4J^2\sinh(2|g|).
}
\end{equation}
Let the normalization satisfy
\begin{equation}
\kappa_L(t)
=
\kappa_0+O(t)
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$, with $\kappa_0>0$. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
\ge
\frac{2J^2\sinh(2|g|)}{\kappa_0}t^2
-
C_gt^3
}
\end{equation}
for sufficiently small $t$, where $C_g$ is finite and independent of $L$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
}{t^2}
\ge
\frac{
2J^2\sinh(2|g|)
}{
\kappa_0
}.
}
\end{equation}
Hence, for every $g\ne0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)>0
}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently small $t>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $\norm{T_\pm}_\infty=1$,
\begin{align}
\norm{H_{g,L}}_\infty
&\le
|m|+Je^g+Je^{-g}
\\
&=
|m|+2J\cosh|g|,
\end{align}
uniformly in $L$.
For a bulk site $m$, the two neighboring bonds contribute
\begin{align}
\left|
|H_{m+1,m}|^2-|H_{m,m+1}|^2
\right|
&=
J^2|e^{2g}-e^{-2g}|,
\\
\left|
|H_{m-1,m}|^2-|H_{m,m-1}|^2
\right|
&=
J^2|e^{2g}-e^{-2g}|.
\end{align}
Thus, for $L\ge3$,
\begin{align}
\Damp(H_{g,L})
&=
2J^2|e^{2g}-e^{-2g}|
\\
&=
4J^2\sinh(2|g|).
\end{align}
The result follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-thermo-shorttime}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is actually uniform in the thermodynamic limit]
\label{rem:uniform-thermo-meaning}
The preceding proposition is stronger than a pointwise finite-size expansion. The coefficient of
$t^2$ is independent of $L$, and the remainder is controlled by a constant depending only on the
uniform Hamiltonian bound and the normalization gauge. Thus the statement
$A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)>0$ at sufficiently small nonzero $t$ is not obtained by assuming that a
finite-size error remains bounded in the thermodynamic limit; the $L$-uniform bound is established
explicitly.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Tight normalization in the Hatano--Nelson family]
\label{rem:HN-tight-thermo}
For the per-Hamiltonian tight normalization,
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{H_{g,L}}^{\rm tight}(t)
=
(1+\varepsilon_0)
\norm{U_{H_{g,L}}(t)}_\infty^2,
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
\lim_{t\to0^+}
\kappa_{H_{g,L}}^{\rm tight}(t)
=
1+\varepsilon_0
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_0=1+\varepsilon_0
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
}{t^2}
\ge
\frac{
2J^2\sinh(2|g|)
}{
1+\varepsilon_0
}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{remark}

\section{Multichannel sharpness: winding cancellation}
\label{sec:sharpness}

\begin{proposition}[Winding-cancellation skin counterexample]
\label{prop:winding-cancellation}
Let
\begin{equation}
H_{\oplus,g}(k)
=
H_g(k)\oplus H_{-g}(k),
\qquad
g\ne0.
\end{equation}
Then the two decoupled sectors have opposite conventional skin accumulation, while
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
W_{\oplus,g}(E)=0
}
\end{equation}
for every reference energy in a common point gap. At nontrivial times away from exact reciprocal
points,
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_{\oplus,g})>0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each block is a Hatano--Nelson chain, with right eigenvectors related to reciprocal standing
waves by opposite diagonal similarity transforms. Thus the two sectors accumulate at opposite
boundaries. Since $H_{-g}(k)=H_g(-k)$, their determinant winding numbers cancel:
\begin{equation}
W_{\oplus,g}(E)
=
W_g(E)+W_{-g}(E)
=
0.
\end{equation}
For $g\ne0$, the direct-sum Hamiltonian is not transpose reciprocal in the fixed block basis,
so Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization} gives positive trajectory-derived channel
resource.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This construction shows why the channel resource should not be identified with a single scalar
determinant winding integer.
\end{remark}

\section{Diagnostic sector II: disordered Lyapunov structure}
\label{sec:diagnostic2}

\begin{definition}[Transfer cocycle and Lyapunov exponents]
\label{def:lyapunov}
Let
\begin{equation}
\Psi_{n+1}=T_n(E)\Psi_n,
\qquad
T^{(N)}=T_N\cdots T_1.
\end{equation}
If the limits exist and
\begin{equation}
s_1(T^{(N)})\ge\cdots\ge s_{2M}(T^{(N)}),
\end{equation}
define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\gamma_j(E)
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{Na}\ln s_j(T^{(N)}).
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary invariance of Lyapunov spectrum]
\label{prop:boundary-lyap}
If
\begin{equation}
T'^{(N)}
=
A_\partial T^{(N)}B_\partial
\end{equation}
with uniformly bounded invertible endpoint matrices, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\gamma_j'(E)=\gamma_j(E).
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Uniformly bounded endpoint factors modify each singular value only by multiplicative constants
independent of $N$. Dividing the logarithm by $Na$ removes these constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Symplectic singular-value pairing]
\label{lem:symplectic-svd}
Let
\begin{equation}
J
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\id_M\\
-\id_M&0
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and suppose $S$ obeys
\begin{equation}
S^TJS=J.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sigma_j(S)\sigma_{2M+1-j}(S)=1.
}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From $S^TJS=J$,
\begin{equation}
S^{-1}=-JS^TJ.
\end{equation}
Since $J$ is unitary and transpose preserves singular values, $S^{-1}$ has the same singular
values as $S$. The singular values of $S^{-1}$ are
$1/\sigma_{2M},\ldots,1/\sigma_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reciprocity-induced Lyapunov pairing]
\label{thm:symplectic-pairing}
Let
\begin{equation}
H_{n,n+1}=t_n^T,
\qquad
H_{n+1,n}=t_n,
\qquad
H_{n,n}=m_n,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
m_n^T=m_n,
\qquad
\sup_n\norm{t_n}<\infty,
\qquad
\sup_n\norm{t_n^{-1}}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\gamma_j(E)
=
-\gamma_{2M+1-j}(E).
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Psi_n=(\chi_n,\chi_{n-1})$. The eigen-equation gives
\begin{equation}
t_n^T\chi_{n+1}
=
(E-m_n)\chi_n
-
t_{n-1}\chi_{n-1},
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
T_n
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(t_n^T)^{-1}(E-m_n)
&
-(t_n^T)^{-1}t_{n-1}
\\
\id
&
0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The bilinear Wronskian
\begin{equation}
\mathcal W_n(\psi,\phi)
=
\psi_n^Tt_n^T\phi_{n+1}
-
\phi_n^Tt_n^T\psi_{n+1}
\end{equation}
is independent of $n$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
T_n^T\Omega_{n+1}T_n
=
\Omega_n,
\qquad
\Omega_n
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-t_{n-1}\\
t_{n-1}^T&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
With
\begin{equation}
P_n=\diag(\id,-t_{n-1}),
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{equation}
P_n^TJP_n=\Omega_n,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\widetilde T_n
=
P_{n+1}T_nP_n^{-1}
\end{equation}
is symplectic. Lemma~\ref{lem:symplectic-svd} gives singular-value pairing for products,
while the uniformly bounded endpoint factors do not alter the Lyapunov limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Imaginary-gauge spatial excess]
Suppose a reciprocal disordered backbone has Lyapunov spectrum
\begin{equation}
\left\{
\pm\frac{L_j(E)}a
\right\}_{j=1}^M,
\qquad
0\le L_1(E)\le\cdots\le L_M(E),
\end{equation}
and the nonreciprocal member is generated by
\begin{equation}
H_{g,\omega}=S_gH_{0,\omega}S_g^{-1},
\qquad
S_g|n\rangle=e^{-gn}|n\rangle.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm Lyap}^{\rm skin}(H_g;\mathcal E)
=
\frac1a
\sup_{E\in\mathcal E}
[|g|-L_1(E)]_+.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Clean Lyapunov--GBZ correspondence]
\label{prop:lyap-gbz}
For a clean constant transfer matrix with nondegenerate diagonalizable spectrum,
\begin{equation}
T
=
V\diag(\beta_1^\downarrow,\ldots,\beta_{2M}^\downarrow)V^{-1},
\qquad
|\beta_1^\downarrow|
\ge\cdots\ge
|\beta_{2M}^\downarrow|,
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\gamma_j(E)
=
\frac1a\ln|\beta_j^\downarrow(E)|.
}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
|\beta_M|
=
|\beta_{M+1}|
\iff
\gamma_M=\gamma_{M+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The diagonalizable eigenvector factors contribute only $O(1)$ to the logarithms of singular
values of $T^N$, while the diagonal powers contribute $N\ln|\beta_j|$.
\end{proof}

\section{Diagnostic sector III: boundary concentration}
\label{sec:diagnostic3}

Let normalized right eigenvectors be $|\psi_{a,L}^R\rangle$ and define
\begin{equation}
p_{a,L}(n)
=
\frac{
\sum_\alpha
|\langle n,\alpha|\psi_{a,L}^R\rangle|^2
}{
\sum_{m,\alpha}
|\langle m,\alpha|\psi_{a,L}^R\rangle|^2
}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Edge concentration]
For an extensive spectral window $I$ and boundary thickness $\ell$,
\begin{equation}
C_I^L(L,\ell)
=
\frac1{N_I(L)}
\sum_{a\in I}
\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}
p_{a,L}(n),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
C_I^R(L,\ell)
=
\frac1{N_I(L)}
\sum_{a\in I}
\sum_{n=L-\ell+1}^{L}
p_{a,L}(n),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
C_I^{\rm edge}
=
\max\{C_I^L,C_I^R\},
\qquad
\Delta_I^{\rm edge}
=
|C_I^L-C_I^R|.
\end{equation}
Conventional extensive skin accumulation means that there exists $\ell_L=o(L)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
C_I^{\rm edge}(L,\ell_L)>0.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Collective decay profile]
For edge $\epsilon\in\{L,R\}$, let $P_I^\epsilon(r)$ denote the thermodynamic averaged
eigenstate intensity at distance $r$ from that edge, whenever the limit exists, and set
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
G_I^\epsilon(r)
=
-\ln\frac{P_I^\epsilon(r)}{P_I^\epsilon(0)}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scale-resolved spatial witness]
For a reference function $f(r)\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_f^{(I,\epsilon)}(H)
=
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{G_I^\epsilon(r)}{f(r)}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lyapunov blindness to subexponential profiles]
\label{thm:blindness}
If
\begin{equation}
G(r)\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{G(r)}r\to0,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{G(r)}r
=
0.
}
\end{equation}
Thus a vanishing linear decay coefficient does not imply absence of edge concentration.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The conclusion is immediate from the asymptotic assumption. Edge concentration is an integrated
spatial property, whereas the linear coefficient only measures one asymptotic scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Stability under finite-depth local unitary processing]
\label{prop:finite-depth-stability}
Let $V_L$ be a depth-$d$ local unitary circuit with gate range $r_0$, independent of $L$. A
boundary projector supported on width $\ell$ is mapped to an operator supported within width
$\ell+O(dr_0)$. Hence an extensive edge-concentration criterion is stable under such processing
up to a subextensive change of boundary thickness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each circuit layer enlarges the support of a boundary observable by at most the gate range.
After $d$ layers the enlargement is $O(dr_0)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Diagnostic sector IV: dynamical channel signatures}
\label{sec:diagnostic4}

For a localized input,
\begin{equation}
P_t(n|m)
=
\frac{|U_H(t)_{nm}|^2}
{\sum_r|U_H(t)_{rm}|^2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Localized reflection asymmetry]
For a site $m$ sufficiently far from boundaries so that the reflection $n\mapsto2m-n$ is defined
on the relevant support, let
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal R_mP)(n|m)=P(2m-n|m).
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
D(t;m)
=
\frac12
\|P_t(\cdot|m)-\mathcal R_mP_t(\cdot|m)\|_1.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dynamical witnesses]
Define
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm dyn}^{\max}(H)
=
\sup_{t>0}\sup_mD(t;m),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm dyn}^{\infty}(H)
=
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_mD(t;m).
\end{equation}
These are operational witnesses, not QRT monotones.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Dynamical gauge embedding]
Let
\begin{equation}
H_g=S_gH_0S_g^{-1},
\qquad
S_g|n\rangle=e^{-gn}|n\rangle,
\qquad
H_0^T=H_0.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
U_g(t)=S_gU_0(t)S_g^{-1},
\end{equation}
and whenever the denominator is nonzero,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{U_g(t)_{nm}}{U_g(t)_{mn}}
=
e^{-2g(n-m)}.
}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
a_{\rm dyn}(t;r)
=
\frac1{2r}
\ln
\left|
\frac{U_g(t)_{r0}}{U_g(t)_{0r}}
\right|
=
-g
}
\end{equation}
whenever the ratio is defined.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The similarity relation and $U_0(t)^T=U_0(t)$ imply
\begin{equation}
\frac{U_g(t)_{nm}}{U_g(t)_{mn}}
=
\frac{e^{-g(n-m)}U_0(t)_{nm}}
{e^{g(n-m)}U_0(t)_{mn}}
=
e^{-2g(n-m)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocal stochastic post-processing contracts dynamical asymmetry]
Let $K_t$ be a stochastic kernel commuting with reflection around $m$. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
D(K_tP_t)\le D(P_t).
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use $\ell^1$ contractivity:
\begin{equation}
D(K_tP_t)
=
\frac12
\|K_t(P_t-\mathcal R_mP_t)\|_1
\le
\frac12
\|P_t-\mathcal R_mP_t\|_1.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Operational lower bound from transition amplitudes]
\label{prop:Adiamond-probe}
For the Julia-dilated channel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t})
\ge
\frac12
\max_m
\sum_n
\left|
|K_{H,t}(n,m)|^2
-
|K_{H,t}(m,n)|^2
\right|.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Prepare the physical input $|m\rangle\langle m|$ and perform the projective measurement whose
outcomes include the physical-sector site states. Under $\mathcal N_{H,t}$ the corresponding
physical-site probabilities are $|K_{H,t}(n,m)|^2$, while under $\mathcal N_{H,t}^{\rev}$ they are
$|K_{H,t}(m,n)|^2$. Data processing for trace distance and the defining optimization of the
diamond norm yield the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-range weighted propagation bound]
\label{prop:weighted-bound}
Let $H$ be bounded and finite range. Let the position operator satisfy
\begin{equation}
X|n,\alpha\rangle=n|n,\alpha\rangle.
\end{equation}
For $\mu>0$, define
\begin{equation}
J_{\sigma\mu}
=
\left\|
e^{\sigma\mu X}He^{-\sigma\mu X}
\right\|_\infty,
\qquad
\sigma=\pm1.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
|U_H(t)_{nm}|
\le
\begin{cases}
\exp[J_{+\mu}t-\mu(n-m)],&n\ge m,\\
\exp[J_{-\mu}t-\mu(m-n)],&n<m.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|U_H(t)_{nm}|
\le
\exp[-\mu|n-m|+J_\mu^\star t],
\qquad
J_\mu^\star
=
\max\{J_{+\mu},J_{-\mu}\}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conjugating the exponential by $e^{\sigma\mu X}$ and taking operator norms gives
\begin{equation}
\left\|
e^{\sigma\mu X}
U_H(t)
e^{-\sigma\mu X}
\right\|_\infty
\le
e^{J_{\sigma\mu}t}.
\end{equation}
Taking matrix elements yields the result.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Hatano--Nelson/resource equivalence}
\label{sec:HN-exact}

\begin{proposition}[Transpose-reciprocity completeness of the uniform Hatano--Nelson family]
\label{prop:HN-complete-class}
Consider the thermodynamic uniform Hatano--Nelson family
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H_{g,L}
=
m\id
+
Je^gT_+
+
Je^{-g}T_-,
\qquad
T_-=T_+^T,
\qquad
J>0.
}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H_{g,L}^T=H_{g,L}
\iff
g=0.
}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $g\ne0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rho_{\GBZ}
=
\frac{|g|}{a}
>0,
}
\end{equation}
and the OBC right eigenvectors obey
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\psi_{j,g}(n)
\propto
e^{-gn}
\sin\left(
\frac{\pi jn}{L+1}
\right).
}
\end{equation}
Thus conventional OBC skin accumulation occurs for every fixed $g\ne0$. Consequently, the
uniform Hatano--Nelson family is transpose-reciprocity complete with respect to conventional
NHSE:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
g\ne0
\iff
H_{g,L}^T\ne H_{g,L}
\iff
\rho_{\GBZ}>0
\iff
\text{conventional OBC NHSE}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The transpose exchanges the two nonreciprocal hopping amplitudes:
\begin{equation}
H_{g,L}^T
=
m\id
+
Je^{-g}T_+
+
Je^gT_-.
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
H_{g,L}^T=H_{g,L}
\iff
e^g=e^{-g}
\iff
g=0.
\end{equation}
The GBZ result follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:HN-calib}. For open boundaries,
\begin{equation}
H_{g,L}
=
S_gH_{0,L}S_g^{-1},
\qquad
S_g|n\rangle=e^{-gn}|n\rangle,
\end{equation}
so right eigenvectors are obtained from reciprocal standing-wave eigenvectors by the exponential
gauge factor $e^{-gn}$. Hence $g>0$ produces left-edge accumulation, $g<0$ produces right-edge
accumulation, and $g=0$ gives the reciprocal extended limit. This establishes the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact trajectory-level NHSE/resource equivalence for uniform Hatano--Nelson]
\label{thm:HN-exact}
Assume the operational time domain is $(0,\infty)$ and use the tight normalization of
Definition~\ref{def:tight-normalization-gauge}. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_g)>0
\iff
g\ne0
\iff
\rho_{\GBZ}>0
\iff
\text{conventional OBC NHSE}.
}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every $g\ne0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
}{t^2}
\ge
\frac{
2J^2\sinh(2|g|)
}{
1+\varepsilon_0
}
>0.
}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first line follows by combining Proposition~\ref{prop:HN-complete-class} with
Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization}. The thermodynamic bound is
Proposition~\ref{prop:HN-thermo-persistence-uniform} with
$\kappa_0=1+\varepsilon_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the exact Hatano--Nelson theorem]
\label{rem:HN-exact-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:HN-exact} is an exact solved subtheory. Its biconditional NHSE statement
relies on the fact that, within the uniform Hatano--Nelson family, conventional OBC skin
accumulation is equivalent to transpose-reciprocity breaking. The theorem is therefore not a claim
that $A_\diamond^{\sup}>0$ is a universal detector of every possible NHSE mechanism.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Multichannel cancellation does not invalidate the scope theorem]
\label{prop:multichannel-scope}
For the direct-sum family
\begin{equation}
H_{\oplus,g}(k)
=
H_g(k)\oplus H_{-g}(k),
\qquad
g\ne0,
\end{equation}
the determinant winding may vanish,
\begin{equation}
W_{\oplus,g}(E)=0,
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}
H_{\oplus,g}^T\ne H_{\oplus,g}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_{\oplus,g})>0.
}
\end{equation}
Thus the channel resource detects transpose-reciprocity breaking even when a single
determinant-winding scalar cancels between channels.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The opposite Hatano--Nelson sectors contribute opposite determinant windings, so the total
winding cancels. However,
\begin{equation}
H_{\oplus,g}^T
=
H_{-g}\oplus H_g
\ne
H_g\oplus H_{-g}
=
H_{\oplus,g}
\end{equation}
for $g\ne0$ in the fixed channel basis. Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization}
therefore yields positive trajectory-derived resource.
\end{proof}

\section{Extensive cellular resource}
\label{sec:extensive}

The following extensive construction is deliberately formulated at the level of independent
channel subsystems. A direct-sum single-particle Hamiltonian does not in general produce a tensor
product of Julia channels under a single global normalization, so no such identification is made.

\begin{definition}[Cellular channel array]
A cellular array consists of independent cell subsystems with primitive channel objects
$\mathcal N_{c_1},\ldots,\mathcal N_{c_n}$ and composite channel
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N
=
\bigotimes_{k=1}^n\mathcal N_{c_k}.
}
\end{equation}
If each cell object is generated by a Hamiltonian $H_{c_k}$, its own normalization gauge and
canonical Julia completion are used before tensor-product assembly. This tensor-product assembly
is an operational cellular construction and is not identified with the Julia channel obtained by
applying one global normalization to a single-particle direct-sum Hamiltonian.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Spatially local free superchannel]
A free superchannel on the cellular array is spatially local if
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Theta
=
\bigotimes_{k=1}^n\Theta_k,
\qquad
\Theta_k\in\FreeOps_L.
}
\end{equation}
Denote this subclass by $\FreeOps_{\rm loc}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This is a strict cellwise locality class. More general finite-depth, bounded-range superchannels
would require an explicit rule for how the spatial partition is transformed and how inter-cell
memories are treated.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Block-additive fixed-time resource]
For
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N
=
\bigotimes_{k=1}^n\mathcal N_k,
\end{equation}
define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_\diamond(\mathcal N)
=
\sum_{k=1}^nA_\diamond(\mathcal N_k),
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_{\rm rob}(\mathcal N)
=
\sum_{k=1}^n\Rrob(\mathcal N_k).
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Extensivity and local monotonicity]
\label{thm:extensive}
For every spatially local free superchannel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_\diamond(\Theta(\mathcal N))
\le
E_\diamond(\mathcal N),
\qquad
E_{\rm rob}(\Theta(\mathcal N))
\le
E_{\rm rob}(\mathcal N).
}
\end{equation}
For a homogeneous array of $n$ identical cells with
$A_\diamond(\mathcal N_c)=a>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_\diamond(\mathcal N)
=
na.
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the fixed-time monotonicity theorem to each cell and sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Time-optimized block-additive resource]
For a collection of independent cell Hamiltonians $\{H_{c_k}\}_{k=1}^n$, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_\diamond^{\sup}
=
\sum_{k=1}^nA_\diamond^{\sup}(H_{c_k}),
\qquad
E_{\rm rob}^{\sup}
=
\sum_{k=1}^nR_{\rm rob}^{\sup}(H_{c_k}).
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Trajectory-derived extensivity and rescaling invariance]
\label{thm:extensive-sup}
Under cellwise free trajectory transformations,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_\diamond^{\sup}(\mathfrak\Theta\mathfrak N)
\le
E_\diamond^{\sup}(\mathfrak N).
}
\end{equation}
and similarly for $E_{\rm rob}^{\sup}$. Moreover $E_\diamond^{\sup}$ is additive under
independent cell concatenation and invariant under a common positive energy rescaling of all cell
Hamiltonians.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply trajectory-level monotonicity and tight-gauge energy-rescaling invariance to every cell,
then sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete witness on the Hatano--Nelson gauge sector]
\label{thm:complete-witness}
Let every cell object be drawn from a Hatano--Nelson gauge orbit. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_\diamond^{\sup}>0
\iff
\text{at least one cell has }A_\diamond^{\sup}>0.
}
\end{equation}
For a cell that is itself a thermodynamic uniform Hatano--Nelson family,
Theorem~\ref{thm:HN-exact} identifies this positivity with conventional OBC skin accumulation
in that cell family.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The sum is nonnegative, so it is positive exactly when at least one summand is positive.
The Hatano--Nelson identification follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:HN-exact}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the extensive extension]
The quantity $E_\diamond^{\sup}$ is an operationally extensive resource under explicitly cellwise
local free operations. It should not be confused with an intrinsic tensor-power rate of the global
single-particle QRT, which collapses by Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded-global}. Nor does a finite
cell by itself constitute a thermodynamic NHSE system; the conventional NHSE interpretation applies
when that factor is a thermodynamic cell family.
\end{remark}

\section{Buffered local reduction for genuinely coupled chains}
\label{sec:windowed}

A genuinely coupled single-particle chain does not carry a canonical spatial tensor-product
factorization: its Hilbert space is the direct sum
$\mathcal H_L=\bigoplus_n\mathcal h_n$. Therefore the expression
$\rho_V\otimes\id_{\bar V}/d_{\bar V}$ and a spatial partial trace are not defined on the primary
single-particle QRT object without an additional local tensor-product realization. The local
reduction below is consequently stated as a separate conditional architecture. For the native
single-particle setting, Proposition~\ref{prop:Adiamond-probe} is the unconditional local probe
statement used in this paper.

\begin{definition}[Tensor-product local realization]
\label{def:tensor-local-realization}
A channel family admits a tensor-product local realization on a window decomposition if its
underlying input and output Hilbert spaces are explicitly identified as
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal K_L
=
\mathcal K_V\otimes\mathcal K_{\bar V}.
}
\end{equation}
The local reduced channel construction below is asserted only for such realizations. No canonical
tensor-product factorization is inferred from the single-particle spatial direct sum.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reduced local channel]
\label{def:reduced-local-channel}
Let $\mathcal N_L$ be a CPTP bistochastic channel in a tensor-product local realization and fix
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sigma_{\bar V}
=
\frac{\id_{\bar V}}{d_{\bar V}}.
}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathfrak R_V[\mathcal N_L](\rho_V)
=
\Tr_{\bar V}
\left[
\mathcal N_L
\left(
\rho_V\otimes\sigma_{\bar V}
\right)
\right].
}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathfrak R_V[\mathcal N_L]$ is CPTP and bistochastic.
\end{definition}

\begin{proof}
Complete positivity and trace preservation are immediate. For unitality,
\begin{align}
\mathfrak R_V[\mathcal N_L](\id_V)
&=
\Tr_{\bar V}
\left[
\mathcal N_L
\left(
\frac{\id_V\otimes\id_{\bar V}}{d_{\bar V}}
\right)
\right]
\\
&=
\Tr_{\bar V}
\left[
\frac{\id_V\otimes\id_{\bar V}}{d_{\bar V}}
\right]
=
\id_V.
\end{align}
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Buffered central window]
Let $V_j$ be a central interval of width $\ell$, and let $W_j\supset V_j$ contain a buffer of
thickness $b$ on both sides. Assume the buffered windows are mutually disjoint. Let $H_{W_j}$
denote the Hamiltonian restricted to $W_j$ with couplings crossing the boundary of $W_j$ removed.
The isolated buffered channel is constructed using the same normalization gauge as the full channel
comparison on the chosen operational interval. In particular, the comparison is required to use
the same scalar normalization $\kappa(t)$ for the full and restricted channels; an alternative
normalization may be used only when its induced discrepancy is absorbed into explicit buffer error
constants below.
\end{definition}

For a tensor-product local realization, define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(L)}
=
\mathfrak R_{V_j}[\mathcal N_{H_L,t}],
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(W)}(\rho)
=
\Tr_{W_j\setminus V_j}
\left[
\mathcal N_{H_{W_j},t}
\left(
\rho\otimes
\frac{\id_{W_j\setminus V_j}}{d_{W_j\setminus V_j}}
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Reciprocity-stable buffer hypothesis]
\label{ass:buffer-stability}
Fix a compact operational time interval $\mathcal T_0$ and a class of finite-range, uniformly
bounded chains with a common strict-contraction bound
$\norm{K_{H_L,t}}_\infty\le q<1$. For the windows above, assume that there exist constants
$C_0,C_1,\mu_{\rm buf}>0$, independent of bulk position, system size, and
$t\in\mathcal T_0$, such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\left\|
\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(L)}
-
\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(W)}
\right\|_\diamond
\le
C_0e^{-\mu_{\rm buf}b},
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\left\|
(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(L)})^{\rev}
-
(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(W)})^{\rev}
\right\|_\diamond
\le
C_1e^{-\mu_{\rm buf}b}.
}
\end{equation}
The hypothesis is an explicit reduced-channel statement. It can be checked directly by numerical
channel evaluation, or established analytically in a model class by proving the two replacement
estimates. No stronger implication from quasi-locality alone is assumed here.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Buffered local-channel reduction under reciprocity-stable buffer stability]
\label{thm:buffered-reduction}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:buffer-stability}, for every $t\in\mathcal T_0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\left|
A_\diamond(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(L)})
-
A_\diamond(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(W)})
\right|
\le
\frac{C_0+C_1}{2}
e^{-\mu_{\rm buf}b}.
}
\end{equation}
No unconditional robustness truncation estimate is required for the results below.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the triangle inequality,
\begin{align}
\left|
A_\diamond(\mathcal A)-A_\diamond(\mathcal B)
\right|
&\le
\frac12
\left\|
(\mathcal A-\mathcal A^{\rev})
-
(\mathcal B-\mathcal B^{\rev})
\right\|_\diamond
\\
&\le
\frac12
\left(
\|\mathcal A-\mathcal B\|_\diamond
+
\|\mathcal A^{\rev}-\mathcal B^{\rev}\|_\diamond
\right).
\end{align}
Apply the two bounds in Assumption~\ref{ass:buffer-stability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Optional one-sided variant]
Because $\rev$ is a continuous involution on the finite-dimensional superoperator space, one may
replace the second buffer estimate by a finite-dimensional norm bound
\begin{equation}
\|\mathcal A^{\rev}-\mathcal B^{\rev}\|_\diamond
\le
C_{\rev}(d_V)
\|\mathcal A-\mathcal B\|_\diamond
\end{equation}
for fixed window dimension $d_V$. The two-sided formulation used in the main theorem is
preferable because it is directly testable and avoids importing a non-intrinsic continuity
constant.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[How the buffer hypothesis is to be used]
The operational content of the coupled-chain extension is separated into two levels. The exact QRT
monotonicity applies to the full reduced channel whenever a tensor-product local realization is
specified. The approximation of that reduced channel by an isolated buffered window is an
additional, explicitly testable locality hypothesis. This avoids promoting a proof sketch for a
nonlinear Julia completion into an unconditional theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Local reduced-channel resource]
For disjoint windows $V_1,\ldots,V_J$ in a tensor-product local realization,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(L,t)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{J}
A_\diamond(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(L)}).
}
\end{equation}
Define the isolated-buffer benchmark by
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm iso}(L,t)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{J}
A_\diamond(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(W)}).
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact local monotonicity]
\label{prop:local-reduced-monotone}
Let
\begin{equation}
\Theta_j:\mathsf{Obj}(V_j)\to\mathsf{Obj}(V_j)
\end{equation}
be free superchannels acting independently on the reduced channels. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^{J}
A_\diamond(\Theta_j(\mathcal M_{V_j}^{(L)}))
\le
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(L,t).
}
\end{equation}
The same statement holds for $\Rrob$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:Adiamond} and Theorem~\ref{thm:robustness} to each reduced channel,
then sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exponential coupling control under reciprocity-stable buffer stability]
\label{prop:local-coupling-control}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:buffer-stability},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\left|
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(L,t)
-
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm iso}(L,t)
\right|
\le
\frac{J(C_0+C_1)}2
e^{-\mu_{\rm buf}b}.
}
\end{equation}
If fixed-width windows tile the bulk so that $J=\Theta(L)$, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac1L
\left|
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(L,t)
-
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm iso}(L,t)
\right|
=
O(e^{-\mu_{\rm buf}b}).
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sum the single-window estimate from Theorem~\ref{thm:buffered-reduction} over $j$ and use the
triangle inequality. If $J=\Theta(L)$, division by $L$ leaves the same exponential dependence
on $b$ up to a constant prefactor.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Bulk local resource density]
\label{prop:bulk-local-density}
If the coupled tensor-local channel realization is translation invariant in the bulk, then
\begin{equation}
a_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(t)
=
\limsup_{L\to\infty}
\frac1L
E_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(L,t)
\end{equation}
is finite for fixed $\ell,t$, and under Assumption~\ref{ass:buffer-stability},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\left|
a_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm loc}(t)
-
a_{\diamond,\ell,b}^{\rm iso}(t)
\right|
\le
C_{\ell,t}e^{-\mu_{\rm buf}b}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finiteness follows from $A_\diamond\le1$. The error estimate follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-coupling-control} and the uniform tiling assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
These statements provide a controlled local reduction for coupled chains only when an explicit
spatial tensor-product realization of the channel is supplied. For the native single-particle
Julia channel, the local diagnostic used without this extra architecture is the transition-amplitude
probe of Proposition~\ref{prop:Adiamond-probe}. The present results do not claim monotonicity under
arbitrary coherent global superchannels acting on the full chain, and they do not derive exponential
buffer convergence solely from Lemma~\ref{lem:quasi-julia}.
\end{remark}

\section{Symmetry-extended architecture}
\label{sec:symmetry-extended}

\begin{definition}[Covariance datum]
A covariance datum on $\mathsf{Obj}_L$ is a map
$r:\mathsf{Obj}_L\to\mathsf{Obj}_L$
satisfying
\begin{enumerate}
\item $r^2=\mathrm{id}$;
\item $r$ is convex-linear;
\item $r(\mathsf{Obj}_L)\subseteq\mathsf{Obj}_L$;
\item $r$ acts cellwise on tensor-product arrays;
\item free superchannels satisfy $\Theta\circ r=r\circ\Theta$.
\end{enumerate}
Transpose $r(\mathcal N)=\mathcal N^{\rev}$ is an example.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Symmetry-extended free set and asymmetry]
For a finite family $\mathscr G$ of covariance data,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\F_{\mathscr G}
=
\bigcap_{r\in\mathscr G}\mathrm{Fix}(r),
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\mathscr G}(\mathcal N)
=
\frac12
\max_{r\in\mathscr G}
\|\mathcal N-r(\mathcal N)\|_\diamond.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Symmetry-extended monotonicity]
\label{thm:symmetry-extended}
For every $\mathscr G$-free superchannel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\mathscr G}(\Theta(\mathcal N))
\le
A_{\mathscr G}(\mathcal N).
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathscr G}(\mathcal N)=0
\iff
\mathcal N\in\F_{\mathscr G}.
\end{equation}
On a cellular array,
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathscr G}(\mathcal N)
=
\sum_kA_{\mathscr G}(\mathcal N_k)
\end{equation}
is monotone under symmetry-covariant cellwise free superchannels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $r^*$ attain the maximum in $A_{\mathscr G}(\Theta(\mathcal N))$. Covariance gives
$r^*\Theta=\Theta r^*$, so
\begin{align}
A_{\mathscr G}(\Theta(\mathcal N))
&=
\frac12
\|\Theta(\mathcal N)-\Theta(r^*(\mathcal N))\|_\diamond
\\
&\le
\frac12
\|\mathcal N-r^*(\mathcal N)\|_\diamond
\\
&\le
A_{\mathscr G}(\mathcal N).
\end{align}
Faithfulness follows from the finite maximum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The present paper uses the single covariance datum $\mathscr G=\{\rev\}$. A different NHSE
sector can be incorporated only after its physical symmetry action has been explicitly defined and
shown to satisfy the covariance axioms. In particular, no reciprocal non-Abelian $\mathbb Z_2$
skin resource or Lindbladian skin resource is claimed to have been constructed here.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Postselection versus genuinely open quantum dynamics}

The present CPTP embedding is a completion of a normalized non-Hermitian propagation operator.
It is not a claim of equivalence with arbitrary gain/loss Lindblad dynamics. A genuinely open
system resource theory would require an enlarged object class, for example CPTP dynamical maps or
Lindbladian generators, with its own free operations. This distinction is substantive: recent work
has shown that Lindbladian skin topology can retain information that is invisible in postselected
non-Hermitian Hamiltonian descriptions \cite{ChaduteauLindblad2026}.

\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The primitive theory is finite-time and operational. Its objects are quasi-local CPTP
bistochastic channels, its free channels are transpose reciprocal, and its free operations are
covariance-constrained quantum superchannels that preserve the object class. Covariance is imposed
on the stated object class; no stronger covariance on a larger space is silently assumed. The
Julia dilation is a canonical representation choice for the normalized propagator, not a
uniqueness theorem for physical dilations. The only no-ancilla rigidity statement proved here
uses the explicitly defined unitary-congruence subclass $\FreeOps_{\rm uc}(L)$.

Several clarifications are central.

First, the finite-size quasi-locality definition is deliberately separated from the thermodynamic
locality layer. The fixed-$L$ object class is suitable for finite-dimensional QRT statements, while
thermodynamic claims use sequences with $L$-uniform locality constants and superchannels with the
locality certificate of Definition~\ref{def:locality-certificate}. Thus finite-dimensional global
maps are not silently treated as thermodynamically local.

Second, channel transpose is a linear involution but an anti-homomorphism under composition:
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal A\circ\mathcal B)^{\rev}
=
\mathcal B^{\rev}\circ\mathcal A^{\rev}.
\end{equation}
For this reason the reciprocity-covariance condition belongs to the complete superchannel. The
main monotonicity theorems never use transpose as a diamond-norm isometry.

Third, the calibration family is ordered exactly by the monotone $A_\diamond$:
\begin{equation}
|g|\ge|g'|
\iff
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})
\ge
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}).
\end{equation}
This proves the necessary condition $|g|\ge|g'|$ for free conversion, and rules out deterministic
gauge amplification. It does not establish deterministic attenuation in the full ancilla-assisted
superchannel class. The ancilla-free unitary covariant subclass is rigid by the cosquare invariant.
A full sufficiency theorem requires an explicit deterministic attenuation protocol.

Fourth, normalization dependence has been separated from the symmetry question. The condition
$A_\diamond=0$ is invariant under every finite strict scalar normalization, while the numerical
value can change and can even be driven to zero by taking the normalization arbitrarily large.
The quantitative normalization-equivalence theorem controls this dependence only inside a common
strict-contraction ball. The trajectory-derived tight gauge removes the separate overall energy-
rescaling ambiguity when the full positive-time domain is optimized.

Fifth, the exact generalization to NHSE is intentionally class based. Theorem~\ref{thm:trajectory-TR-characterization}
is a theorem about transpose-reciprocity breaking. A converse with conventional NHSE is available
only on model classes in which conventional NHSE is itself equivalent to transpose-reciprocity
breaking. The uniform Hatano--Nelson gauge orbit is the primary exact example. Reciprocal
non-Abelian $\mathbb Z_2$ skin physics is explicitly outside this sector.

Sixth, the fixed-time global monotones are bounded by one, so their tensor-power rates vanish.
Extensivity requires either the explicit tensor-product cellular construction with cellwise local
operations or the local-channel construction for coupled systems, the latter only under an
additional tensor-product local realization.

Seventh, a direct-sum single-particle Hilbert space does not possess a canonical spatial tensor
product. Therefore the buffered reduction is conditional: it assumes an independently supplied
tensor-product local realization and explicitly controlled buffer estimates for both the reduced
channel and its transpose. No partial trace over a single-particle spatial complement is taken.

Eighth, the coupled-chain buffer comparison is normalization-consistent by construction: the same
scalar normalization is used for the full and isolated buffered channels, unless a separate
normalization-discrepancy estimate is included in the explicit error constants. This prevents an
unnoticed change of normalization gauge from being misidentified as a locality effect.

Ninth, the exact closed form of
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)
=
\sup_{t>0}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{\rm tight})
\end{equation}
beyond the two-site calibration and thermodynamic lower bound remains unresolved. The full
ancilla-assisted covariant superchannel classification also remains open.

Tenth, the cellular extensive extension is an operational tensor-product construction. It is not
asserted to equal the canonical Julia channel of a globally normalized single-particle direct-sum
Hamiltonian. This distinction is essential because the scalar normalization and Julia defect
operators generally do not factorize under a global normalization.

Finally, the present theory deliberately excludes genuinely Lindbladian resource objects. This is
not a technicality: recent work distinguishes Lindbladian skin topology from postselected
non-Hermitian Hamiltonian topology \cite{ChaduteauLindblad2026}. Likewise, reciprocal non-Abelian
$\mathbb Z_2$ skin physics lies outside the transpose-reciprocity-breaking sector; a recent 2026
PRL provides an explicit reciprocal non-Abelian mechanism \cite{TangZ2Skin2026}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We constructed a fixed-time quantum-channel resource theory for the
transpose-reciprocity-breaking sector associated with conventional NHSE, with quasi-local CPTP
bistochastic objects, reciprocal free channels, covariance-constrained free superchannels, and
faithful monotones $A_\diamond$ and $\Rrob$.

The Julia completion provides a canonical CPTP operational representation of normalized
non-Hermitian propagation. The bistochastic object restriction closes the channel transpose
operation, so the free set is mathematically well typed. The general Julia transpose identities
are
\begin{equation}
D_K^T=D_{K^*},
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}^T=D_{K^T},
\end{equation}
which give
\begin{equation}
\widetilde U_K^T=\widetilde U_{K^T}.
\end{equation}
The canonical Julia choice is treated as a representation convention rather than as a uniqueness
claim about all physical dilations.

The asymmetry has a direct operational meaning:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)
=
2p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}
(\mathcal N,\mathcal N^{\rev})
-
1.
}
\end{equation}
Thus the resource is exactly twice the optimal one-shot advantage over random guessing when
detecting whether a channel and its reciprocal transpose are being presented.

On the two-site Hatano--Nelson calibration, the exact channel asymmetry is strictly increasing
in $|g|$ under a common strict normalization. Therefore the scalar parameter $|g|$ is exactly
ordered by $A_\diamond$ on that calibration orbit, and every deterministic free conversion obeys
the necessary condition $|g|\ge|g'|$. The stronger biconditional conversion law is not claimed
because deterministic attenuation in the full ancilla-assisted class requires an explicit protocol.
Within the explicitly defined ancilla-free unitary-congruence subclass, the phase-invariant
cosquare scalar $\chi_{\rm cs}$ makes $|g|$ rigid.

The normalization convention is treated as an operational gauge. Within the strict scalar class,
the resource support is normalization independent, while quantitative values are controlled by
an explicit continuity bound. Under the per-Hamiltonian tight gauge and full positive-time
optimization,
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(\lambda H)
=
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H).
\end{equation}

The trajectory-derived extension is a genuine time-indexed free-operation construction: fixed-time
monotonicity holds pointwise in $t$, so both the trajectory-derived asymmetry and robustness are
monotone under trajectory-level free maps.

The thermodynamic short-time theorem uses an explicitly $L$-uniform propagator remainder and an
$L$-uniform transition-intensity estimate. For the uniform Hatano--Nelson family this yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)
}{t^2}
\ge
\frac{
2J^2\sinh(2|g|)
}{
1+\varepsilon_0
}
>0
\qquad
(g\ne0),
}
\end{equation}
so the thermodynamic resource persistence is not a finite-size artifact at the level proved here.

The exact logical content of the NHSE correspondence is now separated into three layers. First,
for finite-dimensional Hamiltonians with a time domain accumulating at zero,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}>0
\iff
H^T\ne H.
}
\end{equation}
Second, transpose-reciprocity-breaking conventional NHSE necessarily carries positive channel
resource. Third, on transpose-reciprocity-complete model classes, conventional NHSE is exactly
characterized by positivity of the trajectory-derived resource. The uniform Hatano--Nelson family
is the principal exact example:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_g)>0
\iff
g\ne0
\iff
\rho_{\GBZ}>0
\iff
\text{conventional OBC NHSE}.
}
\end{equation}

The cellular extension gives an exactly additive extensive resource under an explicitly defined
tensor-product laboratory architecture. For genuinely coupled systems, a buffered reduced-channel
construction gives a controlled local benchmark only when a tensor-product local realization is
independently specified and an explicit reciprocity-stable buffer hypothesis controls both the
reduced channel and its transpose. This formulation separates exact QRT monotonicity from model-
dependent locality estimates and does not use the false assumption that channel transpose is a
diamond isometry.

Spectral, disorder, spatial, and dynamical quantities remain diagnostics rather than axioms of the
QRT. Clean reciprocity forces zero determinant winding and reciprocal roots; reciprocal disordered
chains have Lyapunov pairing; and the $g\oplus(-g)$ construction demonstrates that scalar winding
can cancel while the channel resource remains positive.

The resulting architecture is therefore a genuine channel QRT for the transpose-reciprocity-breaking
sector associated with conventional NHSE, supplemented by a trajectory-derived extension, an
explicitly tensor-product extensive theory, and a conditional coupled-chain reduction. It is not a
universal resource theory for every possible NHSE mechanism. Extending the architecture to reciprocal
non-Abelian $\mathbb Z_2$ skin physics or to genuinely Lindbladian skin topology requires identifying
the corresponding physical covariance data and, in the latter case, enlarging the primitive object
class.
