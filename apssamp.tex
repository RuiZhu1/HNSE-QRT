\title{A Quantum Resource Theory of Transpose-Reciprocity Breaking from Non-Hermitian Propagators:\\
Exact Hatano--Nelson Calibration and Uniform Short-Time Persistence in the Single-Particle Thermodynamic Limit}

\author{Ann Author}
\affiliation{Authors' institution and/or address}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We formulate a fixed-time quantum resource theory of transpose-reciprocity breaking for channels
constructed from normalized non-Hermitian propagators by a canonical Julia unitary completion.
The primitive objects are CPTP bistochastic channels on the doubled physical--environmental Hilbert
space; free channels are invariant under channel transpose, and free superchannels are physically
realizable, object-preserving superchannels that commute with this transpose involution. This
covariance is an axiomatic resource-neutrality criterion, not a microscopic hardware cost model.
The channel asymmetry
$A_\diamond=\tfrac12\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond$
is faithful and equals $2p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}-1$ in one-shot discrimination of a channel from
its reciprocal transpose; the generalized robustness is another faithful monotone.

For the two-site Hatano--Nelson gauge orbit at a calibration time, $A_\diamond$ has a closed form
and is strictly increasing in $|g|$ under a common strict normalization. Hence any deterministic
free conversion on that orbit must satisfy $|g|\ge |g'|$. An explicitly defined ancilla-free
unitary-congruence subclass is rigid, $|g|=|g'|$, while deterministic attenuation for the full
ancilla-assisted free-superchannel class is left open.

The quantitative resource value is normalization dependent, whereas its zero-versus-positive
support is invariant under every finite strict scalar normalization for the canonical Julia
representative. A per-Hamiltonian tight gauge gives exact invariance under overall positive energy
rescaling after optimization over all positive times. For uniformly bounded finite-range chains,
an $L$-uniform short-time estimate yields a lower bound in the single-particle thermodynamic limit;
for the uniform Hatano--Nelson family this bound is strictly positive for every $g\ne0$.

The resource detects transpose-reciprocity breaking, not non-Hermiticity or skin accumulation in
full generality. An exact biconditional with conventional OBC NHSE holds on model classes where
conventional NHSE is independently equivalent to transpose-reciprocity breaking, in particular
for the uniform Hatano--Nelson family. For coupled chains we prove exponential buffer stability
only for the compressed-contraction Julia channel $\Lambda_V$; no identification with a genuine
many-body reduced channel is claimed. Numerical checks support the stated locality and
finite-size persistence claims but are not used as proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and operational scope}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantum resource theories specify an object class, a free subset, admissible free transformations,
and a conversion preorder; channel resource theories naturally use quantum superchannels for the
transformations \cite{ChitambarGour,LiuYuan,ChiribellaSupermap}. Throughout, $\norm{\cdot}$ denotes
the Hilbert-space operator norm $\norm{\cdot}_{2\to2}$. Here the primitive resource is
not ``non-Hermiticity'' or NHSE itself, but violation of transpose reciprocity in a fixed physical
basis.

The construction is
\begin{equation}
H\;\longrightarrow\;U_H(t)\;\longrightarrow\;K_{H,t}
\;\longrightarrow\;\widetilde U_{K_{H,t}}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal N_{H,t}.
\label{eq:architecture}
\end{equation}
where the propagator is divided by a positive scalar so that $K_{H,t}$ is a strict contraction,
and the contraction is completed to a canonical Julia unitary. This is a representation convention
for the present QRT, not a uniqueness statement about all physical open-system realizations of a
non-Hermitian evolution.

The reciprocity datum is a fixed basis. Let $\mathcal C(X)=X^*$ be complex conjugation in that
basis. For a bistochastic channel $\mathcal N$ with Kraus operators $\{K_a\}$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^{\rev}(X)=\sum_a K_a^T X K_a^*.
\label{eq:channel-transpose}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^{\rev}=\mathcal C\circ\mathcal N^\dagger\circ\mathcal C,
\label{eq:time-reversal}
\end{equation}
so $\mathcal N=\mathcal N^{\rev}$ is a time-reversal-like covariance condition in the chosen
reciprocity frame. It is related in spirit to, but is not identified with, standard quantum detailed-balance frameworks \cite{Agarwal1973,Alicki1976,Kossakowski1977};
no equivalence between the two notions is assumed.

A central scope distinction will be maintained throughout:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{QRT resource}=\text{transpose-reciprocity breaking},
}
\end{equation}
while NHSE is a distinguished calibration sector. We also keep five mathematically distinct
objects separate: the full Julia-channel monotone $A_\diamond$; the physical-input witness
$A_\diamond^{\rm phys}$, for which no full-$\FreeOps_L$ monotonicity is claimed; the compressed
single-particle channel $\Lambda_V=\mathcal N_{P_VKP_V}$; and, only when an independent tensor-product
realization is supplied, a genuine reduced channel $\mathfrak R_V$. The covariance-matrix quantity
$\Xi_V:=d_{\bar V}^{-1}K_{V\bar V}K_{V\bar V}^\dagger$ is a separate diagnostic, not a CPTP
reduced-channel component. No identification among these objects is assumed beyond the relations proved explicitly below. The resource can be positive for Hermitian,
gauge-trivial systems that violate transpose reciprocity in the fixed frame; conversely, other
NHSE mechanisms, including reciprocal non-Abelian $\mathbb Z_2$ skin effects and genuinely
Lindbladian skin topology, lie outside this single-involution construction
\cite{OkumaEtAl2020,TangZ2Skin2026,ChaduteauLindblad2026}.

\section{Canonical Julia channel and transpose covariance}
\label{sec:channel}

\begin{definition}[Single-particle Hilbert space]
For a chain of length $L$ with internal dimension $M$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_L=\operatorname{span}\{|n,\alpha\rangle:
1\le n\le L,\ 1\le\alpha\le M\},
\end{equation}
with a fixed spatial and internal basis. The canonical Julia completion acts on
$\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L=\mathcal H_L\oplus\mathcal H_L^{\rm env}$, where the environmental copy
carries the corresponding basis.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Admissible normalized propagator]
Let $\mathcal H_{\rm adm}$ be a transpose-closed Hamiltonian class with a uniform operator-norm bound
$\sup_{H\in\mathcal H_{\rm adm}}\norm{H}\le h_{\rm adm}<\infty$, and let
$U_H(t)=e^{-iHt}$. A common scalar normalization is
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\varepsilon(t)=[1+\varepsilon(t)]\kappa_*(t),\qquad
\kappa_*(t)=\sup_{H\in\mathcal H_{\rm adm}}\norm{U_H(t)}^2.
\end{equation}
Then $\kappa_*(t)\le e^{2h_{\rm adm}t}<\infty$, so the common normalization is well defined. Here $\varepsilon(t)>0$.
\begin{equation}
K_{H,t}=\frac{U_H(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa_\varepsilon(t)}},
\qquad
\norm{K_{H,t}}<1,
\end{equation}
and, because transpose preserves the operator norm,
\begin{equation}
K_{H^T,t}=K_{H,t}^T.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Julia completion]
For every strict contraction $K$, define
\begin{equation}
D_K=(\id-K^\dagger K)^{1/2},\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}=(\id-KK^\dagger)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\widetilde U_K=
\begin{pmatrix}
K&D_{K^\dagger}\\
D_K&-K^\dagger
\end{pmatrix}.}
\end{equation}
Then $\widetilde U_K$ is unitary. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
D_K^T=D_{K^*},\qquad D_{K^\dagger}^T=D_{K^T},\qquad
\widetilde U_K^T=\widetilde U_{K^T}.}
\end{equation}
Consequently the canonical Julia channel
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N_K(X)=\widetilde U_KX\widetilde U_K^\dagger
\end{equation}
is CPTP, unital, and obeys
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\mathcal N_K^{\rev}=\mathcal N_{K^T}.}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Unitarity follows from the standard defect identities
$D_{K^\dagger}K=KD_K$ and
$D_KK^\dagger=KD_{K^\dagger}$. The transpose identities follow by functional calculus.
The channel statement follows from the transposed implementing unitary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Canonical reciprocity criterion]
For a strict contraction $K$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_K^{\rev}=\mathcal N_K
\iff K^T=K.
}
\label{eq:canonical-criterion}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The forward implication follows because equality of unitary-conjugation channels implies
$\widetilde U_K^T=e^{i\phi}\widetilde U_K$. Transposition gives $e^{2i\phi}=1$. The choice
$e^{i\phi}=-1$ would require a positive definite defect operator to equal the negative of another
positive definite defect operator, which is impossible for a strict contraction. Hence $e^{i\phi}=1$
and $K^T=K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is dilation independent]
The canonical criterion \eqref{eq:canonical-criterion} is a statement about the chosen Julia
representative. For an \emph{arbitrary} unitary dilation $V$ with physical block $V_{11}=K$, one
can only conclude
\begin{equation}
V^T\propto V\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K^T=\pm K
\end{equation}
(for $K\ne0$). Thus the correct universal dilation-independence statement is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
K^T\ne\pm K\\[-1mm]
\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{every unitary dilation of }K\text{ is non-reciprocal}.
\end{gathered}}
\end{equation}
The stronger implication with $K^T\ne K$ is false in general; the canonical Julia construction
is therefore kept explicit throughout the paper.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Fixed-time resource channel]
The primitive object associated with $(H,t)$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\mathcal N_{H,t}=\mathcal N_{K_{H,t}}.}
\end{equation}
The resource depends on the chosen scalar normalization and reciprocity frame.
\end{definition}

\section{Free channels, free superchannels, and monotones}
\label{sec:qrt}

\begin{definition}[Finite-size object class and free set]
Let
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{Obj}_L=\{\mathcal N\in\Chan(\widetilde{\mathcal H}_L):\mathcal N(\id)=\id\},
\end{equation}
namely the CPTP bistochastic channels on the doubled space, and define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\F_L=\{\mathcal N\in\mathsf{Obj}_L:\mathcal N^{\rev}=\mathcal N\}.}
\end{equation}
At fixed finite $L$ this is a symmetry resource theory. Thermodynamic locality is imposed only
through $L$-uniform family conditions.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Basic properties of channel transpose]
On $\mathsf{Obj}_L$, the map $\mathcal N\mapsto\mathcal N^{\rev}$ is a linear continuous involution,
preserves CPTP bistochasticity, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal A\circ\mathcal B)^{\rev}
=\mathcal B^{\rev}\circ\mathcal A^{\rev},
\qquad
(\mathcal N\otimes\id_E)^{\rev}
=\mathcal N^{\rev}\otimes\id_E.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For Kraus operators $K_a$, use $(K_a^T)^\dagger=K_a^*$ and bistochasticity to verify trace
preservation and unitality. Kraus-representation independence follows from the usual unitary
Kraus mixing. The anti-homomorphism property follows from $(AB)^T=B^TA^T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Free superchannel]
A deterministic superchannel has the form
\begin{equation}
\Theta(\mathcal N)=\Gamma_{\rm post}\circ(\mathcal N\otimes\id_E)\circ\Gamma_{\rm pre},
\end{equation}
with CPTP pre- and post-processing \cite{ChiribellaSupermap}. It is free when
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Theta$ is physically realizable,
\item $\Theta(\mathsf{Obj}_L)\subseteq\mathsf{Obj}_L$,
\item $\boxed{\Theta(\mathcal N^{\rev})=[\Theta(\mathcal N)]^{\rev}}$ for all
$\mathcal N\in\mathsf{Obj}_L$.
\end{enumerate}
The covariance requirement is imposed on the complete superchannel. We denote the class by
$\FreeOps_L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Physical status of the free-operation choice]
The class $\FreeOps_L$ is an axiomatic covariance class. It guarantees resource non-generation,
but it is not claimed that every mathematically free superchannel is zero-cost in a specific
laboratory technology. A hardware-level resource theory would require an independently specified set of symmetry-neutral
fabrication and control primitives. The present results therefore establish resource-theoretic
monotonicity for the chosen symmetry class, rather than a microscopic zero-cost theorem for laboratory
engineering.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Channel asymmetry]
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)=\frac12\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond.
}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Operational discrimination]
For equal priors, the optimal one-shot success probability for discriminating $\mathcal N$ from
$\mathcal N^{\rev}$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}=\frac12+\frac14\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond
 =\frac{1+A_\diamond(\mathcal N)}2.}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{A_\diamond=2p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}-1.}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity and faithfulness]
For every $\Theta\in\FreeOps_L$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{A_\diamond(\Theta(\mathcal N))\le A_\diamond(\mathcal N),}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)=0\iff \mathcal N\in\F_L.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Covariance yields
$[\Theta(\mathcal N)]^{\rev}=\Theta(\mathcal N^{\rev})$.
Contractivity of the diamond norm under physical superchannels then gives the inequality;
faithfulness is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Generalized robustness]
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Rrob(\mathcal N)=\inf\left\{s\ge0:\exists\,\mathcal M\in\mathsf{Obj}_L,
\frac{\mathcal N+s\mathcal M}{1+s}\in\F_L\right\}.}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Robustness monotonicity and boundedness]
For every free superchannel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Rrob(\Theta(\mathcal N))&\le\Rrob(\mathcal N),\\
0\le A_\diamond(\mathcal N)&\le\Rrob(\mathcal N)\le1.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Rmax(\mathcal N)=\log[1+\Rrob(\mathcal N)]}
\end{equation}
with the max-relative-entropy form
\begin{equation}
\Rmax(\mathcal N)=
\min_{\mathcal F\in\F_L}
\log\min\{\lambda\ge1:\lambda\mathcal F-\mathcal N\ \text{is CP}\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\Rrob(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})\le1$ and $\Rmax(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})\le\log2$ for all $n$ when the free set on the tensor-power space is defined by the same involution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Free mixtures are mapped to free mixtures by a free superchannel, giving monotonicity. The bound
$\Rrob\le1$ follows by choosing $\mathcal M=\mathcal N^{\rev}$, since
$(\mathcal N+\mathcal N^{\rev})/2\in\F_L$; the same identity gives $A_\diamond\le\Rrob$.
The $\Rmax$ equivalence follows by setting $s=\lambda-1$ and observing that a CP, TP, unital
map divided by $s$ belongs to $\mathsf{Obj}_L$. The tensor-power bound follows from
$\tfrac12[\mathcal N^{\otimes n}+(\mathcal N^{\rev})^{\otimes n}]\in\F_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Physical-input asymmetry]
The full $A_\diamond$ is the QRT monotone of the complete canonical Julia channel. To describe
laboratory probes that cannot prepare the environmental input block, let
$P_{\rm phys}$ denote the projector onto $\mathcal H_L$ and let the reference system satisfy
$\dim R\ge\dim\mathcal H_L$. Define
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\rm phys}(\mathcal N)=
\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal D(\mathcal H_L\otimes R)}
\frac12\lVert\Delta_\rho\rVert_1,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\rho=(\mathcal N\otimes\id_R-\mathcal N^{\rev}\otimes\id_R)(\rho).
\end{equation}
where the input is embedded into the physical block selected by $P_{\rm phys}$, with no support
on the environmental input block. This restricted quantity is an
operational witness; no monotonicity under the full $\FreeOps_L$ class is claimed.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Physical-input probe]
For the canonical Julia channel,
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\rm phys}(\mathcal N_K)
\ge
\frac12\max_m\sum_n
\left||K_{nm}|^2-|K_{mn}|^2\right|.
\label{eq:physical-probe}
\end{equation}
Under strict contraction, $A_\diamond^{\rm phys}=0$ iff $K^T=K$, so the physical-input witness
has the same support as the canonical full-channel monotone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the lower bound, use the physical basis input $|m\rangle$ and measure the physical site basis.
Under $\mathcal N_K$ and $\mathcal N_K^{\rev}$ the corresponding physical probabilities are
$|K_{nm}|^2$ and $|K_{mn}|^2$, respectively, so data processing gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-probe}.
For the support statement, suppose $A_\diamond^{\rm phys}=0$. Then, in particular, for every pure
physical input $|\psi\rangle$, the two pure output states of the isometries
$\widetilde U_K P_{\rm phys}$ and $\widetilde U_K^T P_{\rm phys}$ coincide as density operators.
If $\dim\mathcal H_L>1$, linearity implies that the two isometries differ on the physical input
subspace by one common phase,
$\widetilde U_K^T P_{\rm phys}=e^{i\phi}\widetilde U_K P_{\rm phys}$. For
$\dim\mathcal H_L=1$, $K^T=K$ is automatic. Comparing the physical diagonal and defect blocks gives
$K^T=e^{i\phi}K$ and $D_K^T=e^{i\phi}D_K$. Strict contraction makes both defect operators positive
definite, so $e^{i\phi}=-1$ is impossible; hence $e^{i\phi}=1$ and $K^T=K$. The converse follows from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-criterion}. Finally, $A_\diamond^{\rm phys}\le A_\diamond$ because the former
optimizes over a restricted set of inputs.
\end{proof}

\section{Normalization gauge and trajectory resource}
\label{sec:gauge}

\begin{definition}[Admissible scalar gauge]
A positive scalar $\kappa(H,t)$ is admissible when
\begin{equation}
\kappa(H,t)>\norm{U_H(t)}^2,
\qquad
\kappa(H^T,t)=\kappa(H,t),
\end{equation}
and, on thermodynamic families of interest, it is uniformly bounded above and below on compact
time intervals. When a short-time expansion is used,
\begin{equation}
\kappa(H_L,t)=\kappa_0+O(t)
\end{equation}
uniformly in $L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Gauge-independent support]
For every finite-dimensional $H,t$ and every admissible scalar gauge,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)})=0
\iff U_H(t)^T=U_H(t).
}
\end{equation}
Thus the zero-versus-positive support is gauge independent within the finite strict scalar class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because the normalization is scalar,
$[K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}]^T=K_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}$ iff $U_H(t)^T=U_H(t)$; then use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-criterion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative gauge continuity]
Suppose two gauges give strict contractions obeying
$\norm{K_j}\le q<1$. Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\left|A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{K_1})-A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{K_2})\right|\\
&\qquad\le
2\left(1+\frac{q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}\right)\norm{K_1-K_2}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
For the same propagator $U_H(t)$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{K_1-K_2}
=\norm{U_H(t)}
\left|\kappa_1^{-1/2}-\kappa_2^{-1/2}\right|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Large-normalization degeneration]
For fixed $H,t$,
\begin{equation}
K^{(\kappa)}=U_H(t)/\sqrt\kappa\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\widetilde U_{K^{(\kappa)}}\longrightarrow
\begin{pmatrix}0&\id\\\id&0\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{K^{(\kappa)}})\longrightarrow0}
\end{equation}
as $\kappa\to\infty$. Quantitative resource values are therefore gauge dependent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory resources and tight gauge]
For an admissible scalar gauge $\kappa$, define the trajectory-derived resource
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)=\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t}^{(\kappa)}).
\end{equation}
Fix $\varepsilon_0>0$ and define the per-Hamiltonian tight gauge by
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\kappa_H^{\rm tight}(t)=(1+\varepsilon_0)\norm{U_H(t)}^2.}
\end{equation}
Then $\norm{K_{H,t}^{\rm tight}}=q_{\rm tight}=(1+\varepsilon_0)^{-1/2}<1$. For
$\mathcal T=(0,\infty)$ write
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)=A_{\diamond,{\rm tight}}^{\sup}(H;(0,\infty)).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact energy-rescaling covariance]
For every $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\mathcal N_{\lambda H,t}^{\rm tight}=\mathcal N_{H,\lambda t}^{\rm tight},
\qquad
A_\diamond^{\sup}(\lambda H)=A_\diamond^{\sup}(H).}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use $U_{\lambda H}(t)=U_H(\lambda t)$ and the definition of the tight normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Trajectory characterization of transpose-reciprocity breaking]
Let the operational time set contain a sequence $t_j\downarrow0$. For every admissible strict scalar
gauge $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_{\diamond,\kappa}^{\sup}(H;\mathcal T)>0
\iff H^T\ne H.
}
\label{eq:trajectory-characterization}
\end{equation}
In particular, the statement holds for the tight gauge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $H^T=H$, then $U_H(t)^T=U_H(t)$ for all $t$. Conversely,
$U_H(t)^T-U_H(t)=-i(H^T-H)t+O(t^2)$, so for sufficiently small positive $t$ the two propagators
differ. Gauge-independent support then gives a strictly positive channel asymmetry.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Hatano--Nelson calibration and conversion constraints}
\label{sec:hn}

Consider
\begin{equation}
H_g=m\id+t_0(e^g|1\rangle\langle2|+e^{-g}|2\rangle\langle1|),
\qquad m,t_0\in\mathbb R,\quad t_0>0,
\end{equation}
with calibration time
\begin{equation}
\tau_* = \frac{\pi}{2t_0}.
\end{equation}
Up to a global phase,
\begin{equation}
U_g(\tau_*)=-i
\begin{pmatrix}0&e^g\\ e^{-g}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
With a common normalization $\kappa>e^{2g_{\max}}$ on $|g|\le g_{\max}$, put
$u=\kappa^{-1/2}$. Then
\begin{equation}
K_g=-iu\begin{pmatrix}0&e^g\\e^{-g}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_{K_g}=\diag(a,b),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
a=\sqrt{1-u^2e^{-2g}},
\qquad
b=\sqrt{1-u^2e^{2g}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact two-site channel asymmetry]
At $\tau_*$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})
&=\sqrt{1-c(g)^2},\\
c(g)&=u^2+\sqrt{(1-u^2e^{2g})(1-u^2e^{-2g})}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
For $g>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\frac{d}{dg}A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})>0.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The discrimination relative unitary
$W_g=\widetilde U_{K_g}^\dagger\widetilde U_{K_g}^T$ has eigenvalues
$e^{\pm i\theta(g)}$, each twice, with
\begin{equation}
\cos\theta(g)=u^2+ab=c(g).
\end{equation}
The exact unitary-channel discrimination formula gives
$A_\diamond=\sqrt{1-\nu(W_g)^2}$, where $\nu$ is the distance from the origin to the numerical
range of $W_g$ \cite{Acin2001}. In the strict calibration regime the numerical range is the
segment joining $e^{\pm i\theta}$, hence $\nu=c(g)$. Writing $y=e^{2g}$ gives
\begin{equation}
c'(g)=
-\frac{u^2(y-y^{-1})}{\sqrt{1+u^4-u^2(y+y^{-1})}}<0,
\end{equation}
so $A_\diamond$ is strictly increasing.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Necessary conversion order]
For the common normalization,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|g|\ge|g'|
\iff
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})
\ge
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}).}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*}\succeq_{\FreeOps}\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}
\Longrightarrow |g|\ge|g'|.
}
\end{equation}
The converse is not established.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Ancilla-free unitary-congruence subclass]
For a unitary $V$, let $\mathcal V(X)=VXV^\dagger$ and define
\begin{equation}
\Theta_V(\mathcal N)=\mathcal V^{\rev}\circ\mathcal N\circ\mathcal V,
\qquad
\mathcal V^{\rev}(X)=V^TXV^*.
\end{equation}
We call this the unitary-congruence subclass and denote by $\FreeOps_{\rm uc}$ those members that
preserve the object class.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Cosquare invariant]
For a unitary $U$ define
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm cs}(U)=U^*U,
\qquad
\chi_{\rm cs}(U)=\frac1{d^2}|\Tr(U^*U)|^2.
\end{equation}
Then $\chi_{\rm cs}$ is invariant under
$U\mapsto e^{i\phi}V^TUV$. For the two-site Julia unitary,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\chi_{\rm cs}(\widetilde U_{K_g})=c(g)^2.}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity in the unitary-congruence subclass]
If $\Theta_V\in\FreeOps_{\rm uc}$ maps $\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*}$ to
$\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}$, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{|g|=|g'|.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Output unitary channels differ only by a global phase, so the cosquare invariant is unchanged.
Thus $c(g)^2=c(g')^2$, and positivity plus strict monotonicity in $|g|$ gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conversion status]
The results distinguish three statements. The monotone proves the necessary attenuation order
$|g|\ge|g'|$ for the full free-superchannel class; the unitary-congruence subclass is rigid; and
explicit deterministic attenuation to the particular target channel $\mathcal N_{g',\tau_*}$ remains
an open feasibility problem for the full ancilla-assisted class. No sufficiency claim is made.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact scope of the NHSE correspondence}
\label{sec:nhse-scope}

For a clean nearest-neighbor translation-invariant chain,
\begin{equation}
H(\beta)=H_{-1}\beta^{-1}+H_0+H_1\beta.
\end{equation}
Define the GBZ radius by
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rho_{\GBZ}=
\frac1a\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal C_{\GBZ}}|\ln|\beta||.
}
\end{equation}
Here ``conventional OBC NHSE'' denotes extensive boundary accumulation of OBC eigenstates in the
standard non-Bloch sense; for the scalar Hatano--Nelson family this is equivalent to $|g|>0$.
If
\begin{equation}
H_{-1}=H_1^T,
\qquad H_0=H_0^T,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
H(\beta)^T=H(\beta^{-1}),
\end{equation}
so zeros of the characteristic equation occur in reciprocal pairs. For a point-gap energy $E$
with $\det[H(k)-E]\neq0$ for all real $k$, the determinant winding therefore vanishes,
\begin{equation}
W_{\rm BZ}(E)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\,\partial_k\log\det[H(k)-E]=0.
\end{equation}
More specifically, assume $H_1$ and $H_{-1}$ are invertible so that the nearest-neighbor
characteristic equation has degree $2M$ with finite nonzero roots counted with multiplicity, and
assume the standard generic GBZ root-ordering prescription applies at $E$. If
$|\beta_1|\le\cdots\le|\beta_{2M}|$ and the GBZ condition is
$|\beta_M|=|\beta_{M+1}|$, reciprocal pairing implies
$|\beta_j|\,|\beta_{2M+1-j}|=1$ and hence
$|\beta_M|=|\beta_{M+1}|=1$, giving $\rho_{\GBZ}=0$. This is a conditional multichannel
statement under the displayed hypotheses, not a universal implication from reciprocity alone.

For the scalar Hatano--Nelson family,
\begin{equation}
H_g(\beta)=m+Je^g\beta+Je^{-g}\beta^{-1},
\end{equation}
and the two roots obey $\beta_1\beta_2=e^{-2g}$, so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\rho_{\GBZ}=|g|/a.}
\end{equation}
Under OBC,
\begin{equation}
H_{g,L}=S_gH_{0,L}S_g^{-1},
\qquad S_g|n\rangle=e^{-gn}|n\rangle,
\end{equation}
so the right eigenvectors satisfy
\begin{equation}
\psi_{j,g}(n)\propto e^{-gn}\sin\frac{\pi jn}{L+1}.
\end{equation}
Thus the uniform Hatano--Nelson family obeys
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
g\ne0
&\iff H_{g,L}^T\ne H_{g,L}\iff \rho_{\GBZ}>0\\
&\iff \text{conventional OBC NHSE}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Exact HN resource witness]
With operational time domain $(0,\infty)$ and the tight gauge,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_g)>0
&\iff g\ne0\iff \rho_{\GBZ}>0\\
&\iff \text{conventional OBC NHSE}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
This is an exact result for the uniform Hatano--Nelson family, not a universal theorem for all
NHSE mechanisms.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Hermitian counterexample to a universal NHSE interpretation]
Let $H=H^\dagger$ but $H^T\ne H$. Then, for any time domain accumulating at zero,
$A_\diamond^{\sup}(H)>0$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
H_\phi=J\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}
\left(e^{i\phi}|n+1\rangle\langle n|+e^{-i\phi}|n\rangle\langle n+1|\right)
\end{equation}
has positive trajectory resource for $\phi\notin\pi\mathbb Z$, even though
$H_\phi=DH_0D^\dagger$ with diagonal unitary $D$ and therefore has the same site-intensity
profiles as the real symmetric chain $H_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The exact logical hierarchy is therefore
\begin{equation}
H^T\ne H\iff A_\diamond^{\sup}>0,
\end{equation}
while an NHSE biconditional requires an additional model-class assumption that conventional
NHSE is equivalent to transpose-reciprocity breaking. Reciprocal non-Abelian $\mathbb Z_2$ skin
physics is outside this class \cite{OkumaEtAl2020,TangZ2Skin2026}.
\end{remark}

\section{Uniform short-time persistence in the single-particle thermodynamic limit}
\label{sec:thermo}

\begin{assumption}[Uniform finite-range family]
A family $\{H_L\}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sup_L\norm{H_L}\le h<\infty,
\end{equation}
and has uniformly bounded finite interaction range.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform propagator remainder]
Under the assumption,
\begin{equation}
U_L(t)=\id-iH_Lt+R_L(t),
\qquad
\boxed{\norm{R_L(t)}\le\frac12h^2t^2e^{ht}.}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the exponential series and
$\sum_{r\ge2}(ht)^r/r!\le\frac12h^2t^2e^{ht}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Transition-probability asymmetry defect]
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Damp(H)=\max_m\sum_{n\ne m}
\left||H_{nm}|^2-|H_{mn}|^2\right|.}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the local witness]
$\Damp(H)>0$ is a sufficient condition for the quadratic short-time lower bound below, but it is
not a characterization of transpose-reciprocity breaking. In particular, phase-only or gauge-type
reciprocity breaking may have $|H_{nm}|=|H_{mn}|$ for all $n,m$ and hence $\Damp(H)=0$ while
$H^T\ne H$.\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform transition-intensity expansion]
There is an $L$-independent function
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_h(t)=h^3t^3e^{ht}+\frac14h^4t^4e^{2ht}
\end{equation}
such that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\max_m\sum_{n\ne m}
\left|
|U_L(t)_{nm}|^2-|U_L(t)_{mn}|^2\right.\\
&\left.\qquad
-t^2\big(|(H_L)_{nm}|^2-|(H_L)_{mn}|^2\big)
\right|
\le2\mathcal E_h(t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $U_{nm}=-it(H_L)_{nm}+R_{nm}$ and use
$||x+y|^2-|x|^2|\le2|x||y|+|y|^2$, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and the previous lemma.
Apply the same argument to $U_L^\dagger$ for the row estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform finite-size short-time resource bound]
Let the scalar normalization satisfy
$\kappa_L(t)=\kappa_0+O(t)$ uniformly in $L$, with $\kappa_0>0$. Then there are $t_0,C_*>0$,
independent of $L$, such that
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H_L,t})
\ge
\frac{t^2}{2\kappa_0}\Damp(H_L)-C_*t^3,
\qquad0<t<t_0.}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the physical-input probe Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-probe}. The transition-intensity lemma gives
the numerator lower bound. Since $\kappa_L(t)^{-1}=\kappa_0^{-1}+O(t)$ uniformly and
$\Damp(H_L)$ is uniformly bounded for a finite-range uniformly bounded family, all corrections
are $L$-independent $O(t^3)$ terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Short-time persistence in the single-particle thermodynamic limit]
With
\begin{equation}
A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)=\limsup_{L\to\infty}A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H_L,t}),
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}\frac{A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)}{t^2}
\ge
\frac1{2\kappa_0}\limsup_{L\to\infty}\Damp(H_L).}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform Hatano--Nelson persistence]
For
\begin{equation}
H_{g,L}=m\id+Je^gT_++Je^{-g}T_-\,,\qquad T_-=T_+^T,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\norm{H_{g,L}}\le|m|+2J\cosh|g|=:h_g,
\end{equation}
and, for $L\ge3$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Damp(H_{g,L})=4J^2\sinh(2|g|).}
\end{equation}
Therefore, for any short-time admissible normalization,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)}{t^2}
\ge
\frac{2J^2\sinh(2|g|)}{\kappa_0}.}
\end{equation}
For the tight gauge, $\kappa_0=1+\varepsilon_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The norm bound follows from $\norm{T_\pm}=1$. A bulk site has two bonds, each contributing
$J^2|e^{2g}-e^{-2g}|$, giving the stated defect. Apply the preceding corollary.
\end{proof}

\section{Locality and buffered compressed channels}
\label{sec:locality}

At fixed time, finite-range bounded Hamiltonians generate exponentially quasi-local propagators.
For the defect operators of the Julia dilation, the strict-contraction gap is what permits the
functional-calculus argument to be closed in a weighted norm.

\begin{lemma}[Quasi-locality of the Julia completion]
Suppose $H$ is finite range and uniformly bounded and the normalized propagator satisfies
$\norm{K_{H,t}}\le q_t<1$ uniformly in $L$. Then, at each fixed finite time, $U_H(t)$,
$K_{H,t}$, $D_{K_{H,t}}$, $D_{K_{H,t}^\dagger}$, and $\widetilde U_{K_{H,t}}$ are exponentially
quasi-local: there are $C_t,\mu_t>0$ independent of $L$ such that
\begin{equation}
\norm{\widetilde P_nA\widetilde P_m}
\le C_t e^{-\mu_t|n-m|}
\end{equation}
for each operator in the list.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The finite-range propagator has a factorially decaying path expansion. To make the functional
calculus explicit, introduce the weighted conjugations
$A_{\sigma\mu}=e^{\sigma\mu X}Ae^{-\sigma\mu X}$ for $\sigma=\pm1$. For a
finite interaction range $R$ and uniformly bounded local matrix elements, finite range implies
\begin{equation}
\norm{H_{\sigma\mu}-H}\le C_H(e^{\mu R}-1)=:\delta(\mu),
\qquad \delta(\mu)\to0\quad(\mu\to0).
\end{equation}
Duhamel's formula then gives a corresponding bound
$\norm{U_{\sigma\mu}(t)-U(t)}\le C_t\delta(\mu)$ on each fixed finite-time interval.
Because $\norm K\le q_t<1$, choosing $\mu$ small enough yields weighted strict contraction
$\norm{K_{\sigma\mu}}\le q_\mu<1$ for both signs. Define
$K^{\sharp}_{\sigma\mu}:=e^{\sigma\mu X}K^\dagger e^{-\sigma\mu X}$; then
$K^{\sharp}_{\sigma\mu}=(K_{-\sigma,\mu})^\dagger$, so it obeys the same uniform bound.
Consequently, for $Z=K^\dagger K$,
$Z_{\sigma\mu}=K^{\sharp}_{\sigma\mu}K_{\sigma\mu}$ satisfies
$\norm{Z_{\sigma\mu}}\le q_\mu^2<1$. The absolutely convergent binomial expansion
\begin{equation}
(\id-Z)^{1/2}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}c_r Z^r
\end{equation}
therefore converges after weighted conjugation, and the same argument applied to $KK^\dagger$
gives the complementary defect. This produces a uniform finite weighted norm for both defect
operators and hence exponential off-diagonal decay. The constants are independent of $L$ because
the range and norm bounds entering $C_H$ and $q_t$ are uniform.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dynamical transition-amplitude witness]
For the canonical Julia channel,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H,t})\ge
\frac12\max_m\sum_n
\left||K_{H,t}(n,m)|^2-|K_{H,t}(m,n)|^2\right|.}
\label{eq:prop23}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The quantity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prop23} is an operationally accessible basis-probe lower bound and
is the object evaluated in the supplied numerical script. Conditional physical transition
probabilities are normalization independent:
\begin{equation}
P_t(n|m)=
\frac{|U_H(t)_{nm}|^2}{\sum_r|U_H(t)_{rm}|^2}.
\end{equation}
For an imaginary-gauge family $H_g=S_gH_0S_g^{-1}$ with $H_0^T=H_0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{U_g(t)_{nm}}{U_g(t)_{mn}}=e^{-2g(n-m)},
\end{equation}
whenever the ratio is defined. These transition and reflection asymmetries are diagnostics, not
additional QRT monotones.

\begin{definition}[Compressed-contraction Julia channel]
For a spatial window $V$ and a strict contraction $K$ on the full chain, define the compression
\begin{equation}
K_V=P_VKP_V|_{\mathcal H_V},
\qquad
\Lambda_V=\mathcal N_{K_V}.
\end{equation}
Since $\norm{K_V}\le\norm K<1$, $\Lambda_V$ is again a well-defined bistochastic Julia channel.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Buffered convergence of the compressed channel]
Let $H_L$ be finite range $R$, $\norm{H_L}\le h$, let $W\supset V$ contain a buffer of thickness
$b>R$, and let $H_W=P_WH_LP_W$. Use the same scalar normalization for the full and buffered
propagators and assume
\begin{equation}
\norm{U_L(t)},\norm{U_W(t)}\le q\sqrt{\kappa(t)},
\qquad q<1,
\end{equation}
on a compact time interval with $\inf_t\kappa(t)>0$. Then, for any fixed weighted exponent
$\mu>0$ small enough,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\norm{K_V^{(L)}-K_V^{(W)}}\le C_Ue^{-\mu b},}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\norm{\Lambda_V^{(L)}-\Lambda_V^{(W)}}_\diamond
&\le C_\Lambda e^{-\mu b},\\
\norm{(\Lambda_V^{(L)})^{\rev}-(\Lambda_V^{(W)})^{\rev}}_\diamond
&\le C_\Lambda e^{-\mu b}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|A_\diamond(\Lambda_V^{(L)})-A_\diamond(\Lambda_V^{(W)})|
\le C_\Lambda e^{-\mu b}.}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta=H_L-(H_W\oplus0)$ and let $Q$ project onto the boundary layer of $W$ that is
within range $R$ of its complement. Since $b>R$, a Duhamel expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\norm{P_V(U_L(t)-U_W(t))P_V}
\le C_{U}(t,\mu)e^{-\mu b},
\end{equation}
by combining finite-range weighted propagation with $P_VQ=0$ and absorbing the fixed offset $R$
into the constant. With the same scalar normalization for the two propagators, the same estimate
holds for $K_V^{(L)}-K_V^{(W)}$. On the strict-contraction ball $\norm K,\norm J\le q<1$,
the defect map is explicitly Lipschitz because, for $A=\id-K^\dagger K$ and
$B=\id-J^\dagger J$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{A^{1/2}-B^{1/2}}\le\frac{\norm{A-B}}{2\sqrt{1-q^2}},
\end{equation}
and $\norm{A-B}\le2q\norm{K-J}$. The same bound applies to the complementary defect,
so $\norm{\widetilde U_K-\widetilde U_J}\le C_q\norm{K-J}$ for a constant depending only
on $q$. Finally, for the associated unitary channels,
$\norm{\mathcal U-\mathcal V}_\diamond\le2\norm{U-V}$, yielding the stated
$e^{-\mu b}$ bounds. Transposition preserves the same weighted estimate, so the reciprocal
channels obey the identical bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Important limitation]
The proposition concerns only the compressed single-particle channel $\Lambda_V$. A genuine local
reduced channel
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak R_V[\mathcal N_L](\rho_V)=
\Tr_{\bar V}[\mathcal N_L(\rho_V\otimes\id_{\bar V}/d_{\bar V})]
\end{equation}
requires an independently supplied tensor-product Hilbert-space realization. The native
single-particle space is a direct sum, not a canonical spatial tensor product. We therefore make no
claim that $\Lambda_V$ equals, approximates, or bounds such a many-body reduced channel. Likewise,
the covariance-matrix expression
$d_{\bar V}^{-1}K_{V\bar V}K_{V\bar V}^\dagger$ is a separate affine diagnostic and is not a
component of a CPTP reduced channel.
\end{remark}

\section{Relation to standard NHSE diagnostics and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The channel QRT is primary. Spectral winding, GBZ radius, transfer-matrix Lyapunov exponents,
edge-concentration measures, and dynamical reflection asymmetries are diagnostics of particular
Hamiltonian families rather than additional free-operation monotones.

For reciprocal disordered chains with invertible nearest-neighbor blocks, the transfer cocycle can
be symplectically gauged so that singular values occur in reciprocal pairs, yielding
\begin{equation}
\gamma_j(E)=-\gamma_{2M+1-j}(E).
\end{equation}
For the clean constant-transfer case, $\gamma_j=(1/a)\ln|\beta_j|$, so the usual GBZ crossing is
$\gamma_M=\gamma_{M+1}$. These results provide consistency relations between reciprocity and
standard non-Bloch/transfer diagnostics, but they do not promote those quantities to resource
monotones.

A useful sharpness example is the direct sum
\begin{equation}
H_{\oplus,g}(k)=H_g(k)\oplus H_{-g}(k),\qquad g\ne0.
\end{equation}
The two sectors have opposite skin accumulation and opposite determinant windings, so
$W_{\oplus,g}=0$, while $H_{\oplus,g}^T\ne H_{\oplus,g}$ and hence
$A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_{\oplus,g})>0$. In a species-twisted reciprocity frame the same direct sum is
reciprocal, illustrating explicitly that the resource is a frame-dependent time-reversal
asymmetry. This is why a broader symmetry architecture would require an explicitly supplied
involution $r$, rather than an unqualified claim of universality.

The particular global monotones used here are bounded by one, so they are persistence-type quantities rather
than extensive tensor-power rates. This statement concerns these max-type quantities, not asymmetry measures in general. An extensive quantity can still be defined operationally for an
explicit tensor-product array of independent cells by summing per-cell asymmetries, but that is a
different architecture from a globally normalized single-particle direct sum and is not needed for
the main results.

Finally, a genuinely open-system Lindbladian resource theory would require an enlarged primitive
object class. The present Julia-completed channel should not be identified with arbitrary gain/loss
Lindblad dynamics or with every microscopic realization of NHSE
\cite{ChaduteauLindblad2026}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have constructed a fixed-time channel resource theory of transpose-reciprocity breaking for
normalized non-Hermitian propagators represented by a canonical Julia completion. The central QRT
objects are CPTP bistochastic channels, free channels are transpose reciprocal, and free
superchannels are physically realizable, object-preserving, transpose-covariant superchannels.
The principal monotones are
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N)=\frac12\|\mathcal N-\mathcal N^{\rev}\|_\diamond
\end{equation}
and generalized robustness. The first has the operational discrimination meaning
$A_\diamond=2p_{\rm succ}^{\rm opt}-1$.

The canonical Julia representative obeys
\begin{equation}
D_K^T=D_{K^*},
\qquad
D_{K^\dagger}^T=D_{K^T},
\qquad
\widetilde U_K^T=\widetilde U_{K^T},
\end{equation}
so its reciprocity support is exactly the condition $K^T=K$. The quantitative resource value is
normalization dependent, but the zero-versus-positive support is not within the admissible strict
scalar class. Under the per-Hamiltonian tight gauge, the trajectory-derived supremum is invariant
under overall positive energy rescaling.

The two-site Hatano--Nelson orbit provides the exact calibration
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{g,\tau_*})=\sqrt{1-c(g)^2},
\end{equation}
with strict monotonicity in $|g|$. Hence every deterministic free conversion obeys the necessary
condition $|g|\ge|g'|$, while the explicitly restricted unitary-congruence subclass is rigid.
Whether the full ancilla-assisted free-superchannel class admits deterministic attenuation remains
open.

For uniformly bounded finite-range chains, an $L$-uniform short-time estimate gives
\begin{equation}
A_\diamond(\mathcal N_{H_L,t})
\ge
\frac{t^2}{2\kappa_0}\Damp(H_L)-O(t^3),
\end{equation}
with an $L$-independent remainder. For the uniform Hatano--Nelson family,
\begin{equation}
\liminf_{t\to0^+}
\frac{A_{\diamond,\infty}(t)}{t^2}
\ge
\frac{2J^2\sinh(2|g|)}{1+\varepsilon_0}>0
\qquad(g\ne0).
\end{equation}
On the same uniform Hatano--Nelson family,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
A_\diamond^{\sup}(H_g)>0
&\iff g\ne0\iff \rho_{\GBZ}>0\\
&\iff \text{conventional OBC NHSE}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
which is the principal exact NHSE witness result of the paper.

For coupled chains, the rigorous local result is instead
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_V=\mathcal N_{P_VKP_V},
\end{equation}
whose comparison to an isolated buffered window is exponentially controlled. No many-body
reduced-channel theorem is claimed, because the native single-particle direct sum carries no
canonical spatial tensor-product factorization. The numerical scripts are consistent with these
analytic results and, importantly, do not replace the compressed channel by a fictitious partial
trace construction.

The appropriate interpretation is therefore precise rather than universal: the paper supplies an
axiomatic symmetry QRT for transpose-reciprocity breaking, an exact Hatano--Nelson calibration sector,
and a uniform short-time persistence theorem in the single-particle thermodynamic limit. Universal coverage of all NHSE mechanisms, a microscopic
hardware theory of free operations, deterministic full-class attenuation, and the relation to
genuine many-body reduced channels remain open problems.
